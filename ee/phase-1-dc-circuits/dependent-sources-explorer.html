Dependent Sources Explorer

Four canonical controlled sources (VCVS, VCCS, CCVS, CCCS) plus a simple BJT small-signal model. Drive the controlling variable, watch the controlled output track.

Regime & assumptions

Regime
linear network small-signal ideal controlled source
Assumes
Controlling variable transferred instantaneously (no propagation delay, no bandwidth limit); transconductance / gain linear over the operating range; BJT linearized around the bias point (h-parameters or gm valid for vbe << VT ≈ 25 mV); no source impedance, no output saturation
Breaks down when
Real op-amps have finite GBP, slew rate, and ±Vsat clipping; BJT exits its linear region at vbe » VT or near cutoff/saturation; VBE drifts –2 mV/°C with temperature; large-signal swings invoke the full Shockley equation

A dependent source is a voltage or current whose value is set by another node's voltage or branch current — not by a battery, but by an algebraic rule. They model transistors and op-amps. The four types come from the 2×2 grid: {V-output, I-output} × {V-controlled, I-controlled}. Once you read the four boxes below, the BJT model collapses to "CCCS with gain β".

VCVS — Voltage-Controlled Voltage Source

Output voltage = µ · v_x, where v_x is a voltage elsewhere. Models an ideal op-amp.

v_out = µ · v_x
v_out = 20.0 V

VCCS — Voltage-Controlled Current Source

Output current = g_m · v_x. Models MOSFET transconductance.

i_out = g_m · v_x
i_out = 20.0 mA

CCVS — Current-Controlled Voltage Source

Output voltage = r · i_x. Less common; appears in transimpedance models.

v_out = r · i_x
v_out = 5.00 V

CCCS — Current-Controlled Current Source

Output current = β · i_x. Models BJT current gain (β = I_c / I_b).

i_out = β · i_x
i_out = 10.0 mA

Applied — BJT small-signal model (CCCS, I_c = β · I_b)

An NPN bipolar transistor in active mode: a small base current I_b commands a collector current I_c that is β times larger. This is the simplest dependent-source application — and the foundation of every linear amplifier.

I_c = β · I_b = 150 · 50 µA
I_c = 7.50 mA
I_e = I_b + I_c = 7.55 mA
α = β/(β+1) = 0.993

What to notice

  • Diamond symbol = controlled source — distinct from the circle (independent source). The arrow inside indicates current or voltage type.
  • Output tracks the controller linearly — slide v_x or i_x, the output moves proportionally. The slope is the gain (µ, g_m, r, β).
  • Units of the gain depend on the type — VCVS µ is dimensionless, VCCS g_m is siemens (A/V), CCVS r is ohms (V/A), CCCS β is dimensionless.
  • BJT in active mode is essentially a CCCS — a tiny I_b drives a β-times-larger I_c. β ≈ 100–300 typical, varies with current and temperature.
  • I_e = I_b + I_c by KCL at the emitter node. With β = 150, I_e is barely larger than I_c (just 0.7% extra).
  • For Rth analysis, you can't "deactivate" a dependent source — its value depends on the rest of the circuit. Instead use the test-source method: apply v_test, find i_test, R_th = v_test / i_test.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Don't deactivate dependent sources in R_th — they stay active. Apply a test source (1 V or 1 A) at the terminals; R_th = V_test / I_test.
  • Don't ignore the controller in superposition — independent sources deactivate one at a time; dependent sources always remain on.
  • Units of the gain matter — mixing up g_m (S) and µ (unitless) gives off-by-orders answers.
