Regime & assumptions

Regime
linear-resistive DC steady-state lumped
Assumes
Lumped-element analysis valid (wavelengths » physical size — the circuit is "small" relative to the EM field); Ri constant; wires lossless; no electromagnetic coupling between branches (no transformer action, no antenna effects)
Breaks down when
Frequencies push the physical size above ~λ/10 — KVL and KCL stop being well-defined per node and you need transmission-line analysis; magnetic mutual coupling between adjacent loops violates the no-EM-coupling assumption (transformer action smears the per-loop voltage budget)

These two laws are the bookkeeping rules behind every circuit analysis. KVL says energy is conserved around a loop: every volt the battery pushes is consumed by a drop. KCL says charge is conserved at a node: nothing piles up. Once you trust both, all circuits made of resistors and constant sources (no diodes, no transistors yet — "linear" circuits) become mechanical.

Walking the loop: imagine you're a charge carrier sliding around the circuit. When you cross a battery from − to +, your potential goes up (a "rise"). When you cross a resistor in the direction the current is flowing, your potential goes down (a "drop"). KVL says rises must equal drops over a closed walk — you return to the same potential you started at.

KVL — Series loop

KVL: +12.00 − 1.52 − 3.34 − 7.14 = 0.0 V
✓ sum = 0 (energy conserved)

Sign convention: Traverse the loop clockwise from the battery's +. A rise (− to +) is positive; a drop (+ to −, across resistors in the direction of current) is negative. The signed sum is always zero.

KCL — Parallel node

KCL at A: I_in = I1 + I2 + I3 → 200.0 = 120.0 + 54.5 + 25.5 mA
✓ I_in − ΣI_out ≈ 0 (charge conserved)

Sign convention: Currents into a node are positive; currents out are negative. The signed sum at any node is always zero. Charge cannot accumulate in steady-state.

What to notice

  • KVL is the loop rule — pick any closed loop, traverse it, and the signed voltages add to zero. No exceptions for linear circuits.
  • KCL is the node rule — pick any node, signed currents add to zero. Useful when sources are current-mode or when you want a single branch.
  • Smallest R hogs the current in the parallel circuit — try setting Rp1 to 10 Ω. Branch 1 takes nearly everything.
  • R_total in parallel is less than the smallest R — because adding more paths can only let more current through, never less.
  • Sign matters everywhere — flip the polarity convention and every term changes sign, but the sum is still zero. Pick a direction at the start and stick with it.
  • These two laws + Ohm's Law solve every linear DC circuit. Thévenin, Norton, superposition are all shortcuts that ultimately reduce to KVL + KCL.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

KVL
ΣV = 0 (around any closed loop)
KCL
ΣI_in = ΣI_out (at any node)
Origin
KVL = energy conservation; KCL = charge conservation.

SIGN CONVENTION

  • KVL traversal — going − to + (a rise) is +V; + to − (a drop) is −V.
  • KCL at a node — currents in are +, currents out are −. Signed sum = 0.
  • Pick a direction once — clockwise loops, "into node" arrows. Don't flip mid-equation.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Mixing sign convention mid-loop — every term flips. Result looks like KVL fails. It doesn't; the bookkeeping did.
  • Assumed current direction wrong — fine, the answer comes out negative. Don't fight it, just note "actually flows the other way."
  • Forgetting a branch at the node — count every wire touching the node, including the source return.
