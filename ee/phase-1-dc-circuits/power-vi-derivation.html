Power = V × I — Where the Watts Come From

One identity ties together every electrical unit you've met: joules, coulombs, volts, amps, and watts. Build it by watching the units cancel — then watch charge actually carry energy through a circuit, and see why a coulomb is never "fuller" than another.

The derivation — watch the coulombs cancel

Power = energy / time how fast energy is delivered
Power = ( energy/charge ) × ( charge/time ) multiply & divide by charge — nothing changes
Power = Voltage × Current energy/charge IS voltage · charge/time IS current
= ( J/C ) × ( C/s ) = J/s = W the coulombs cancel — joules per second left over
A volt is joules per coulomb. An amp is coulombs per second. Multiply them and the coulombs cancel, leaving joules per second — a watt.

See it flow

Voltage sets how much energy each coulomb carries down the drop. Current sets how many coulombs arrive per second. Power is the two multiplied — the energy reaching the load every second.

Why a coulomb is never "fuller" — energy lives in the drop

The unit J/C trips people up: how can one coulomb hold more energy than another? It can't. Energy isn't stored inside the charge — it depends on where the charge sits, exactly like gravity.

Same shape, same algebra: voltage is the "height," charge is the "mass." Volts × coulombs = joules, exactly as (g·h) × kg = joules. And because height is always measured from somewhere, voltage is always a difference — a bird on one wire feels no drop, so no energy moves through it.

What to notice

  • The coulombs cancel — only the product V × I matters. A 240 V × 0.5 A heater and a 12 V × 10 A winch both deliver 120 W. Tall-and-trickle equals short-and-torrent. Slide V down and I up in the conveyor to keep the same wattage.
  • Voltage = energy per coulomb (J/C). Like height is energy per kilogram. A coulomb isn't "charged up" with energy; it gains or loses energy by the voltage it moves through. Same coulomb, 9 V drop → 9 J; 1.5 V drop → 1.5 J.
  • Current = charge per second (C/s). The flow rate. 1 amp = 1 coulomb every second ≈ 6.24×10¹⁸ electrons/second streaming past a point.
  • Power = energy per second (J/s = W). A rate, like speed — not an amount. Energy = power × time, which is exactly why your electric bill is in kilowatt-hours (power × time = energy).
  • P = V·I is the universal definition. P = I²R and P = V²/R only come from substituting Ohm's law, so they hold for resistors only. An LED at 2.1 V and 10 mA dissipates 21 mW — only V·I gets that. See Ohm's Law Explorer.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Power
P = V · I
Units
(J/C)·(C/s) = J/s = W
Ohmic only
P = I²R = V²/R
Energy
E = P · t (J = W·s)
Charge
Q = I · t (C = A·s)

MEMORIZE

  • 1 W = 1 J/s — power is a rate.
  • 1 V = 1 J/C — voltage is energy per charge.
  • 1 A = 1 C/s — current is charge per time.
  • 1 C ≈ 6.24×10¹⁸ electrons.
  • In V·I the coulombs cancel.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • "Energy is stored in the charge." No — it's positional, like height. The charge is just the messenger.
  • "A watt is an amount of energy." No — a watt is a rate. Joules are the amount.
  • Using I²R on an LED/diode. Non-Ohmic — there's no fixed R. Only V·I is valid.
  • Forgetting voltage is a difference. 0 V across you = 0 J, no matter the absolute level.
