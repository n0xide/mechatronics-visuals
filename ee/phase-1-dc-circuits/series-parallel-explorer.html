Series vs. Parallel Explorer

Same three resistors, two topologies. Toggle between series and parallel. Watch R_total, V across each, I through each, and P in each recompute. Confirm power-sum closure.

This visual teaches the resistor-network reflex: given any combination, you can find every voltage, current, and power without algebra panic. Series resistors share current; parallel resistors share voltage. The two topologies are mirror images. Once you've internalised that, ladders and bridges become bookkeeping.

Real uses

  • LED current limit (series) — Pico 3.3V → R_series → LED (Vf=2.1V). Pick R so I≈10 mA. R = (3.3−2.1)/0.010 = 120 Ω.
  • Pull-up sizing (series w/ device input) — 10k pull-up from rail to GPIO input; when device pulls LOW, that 10k limits sink current to V/R.
  • Decoupling caps (parallel) — multiple values (100 nF + 10 µF) in parallel cover wide frequency range. Each branch is independent.
  • I²C pull-ups (parallel) — two 4.7 kΩ pull-ups on the same bus = 2.35 kΩ effective. Sink current at 3.3 V is 1.4 mA — within spec.

What to notice

  • Series: same current, voltage divides — the smallest R sees the smallest V; the largest R sees the most. Sum of voltages = V_supply (KVL).
  • Parallel: same voltage, current divides — the smallest R takes the most current. Sum of currents = I_total (KCL).
  • R_parallel is always less than the smallest R — adding paths can only increase current, never decrease it. Three 100 Ω in parallel = 33 Ω.
  • R_series is always more than the largest R — adding resistance in series can only reduce current, never increase it.
  • Power sums regardless of topology — P_total = ΣP_i. Verify by summing the P column and comparing to the Total row.
  • The voltage-divider formula is just Ohm's Law applied twice — V_R2 = I·R2 = (V_in / R_total) · R2. Memorise the shortcut; understand the derivation.

SHORTCUTS

  • R_parallel < smallest branch — always. Two equal R's in ‖ = R/2; N equal = R/N.
  • R_series > largest — always.
  • Series: same I, V divides by R-ratio.
  • Parallel: same V, I divides inversely by R-ratio.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Current divider is inverted — the smaller R takes more current. Opposite of the voltage divider.
  • V-divider formula — numerator is the resistor you're tapping across, not adjacent to. Easy to swap R₁ and R₂.
  • 1/R sum — students drop the reciprocal step and get nonsense. Add reciprocals, then invert.
