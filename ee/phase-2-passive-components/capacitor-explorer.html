A capacitor is two conductors separated by an insulator. Capacitance scales linearly with plate area and the dielectric constant — and inversely with plate spacing.

orange lines = electric field density (more lines = stronger field)

A real cap is a series RLC: ESL (lead/package inductance), ESR (loss), and ideal C. The minimum impedance happens at the self-resonant frequency (SRF). Above SRF, the inductance dominates and the cap behaves like an inductor.

1/(ωC) — ideal cap ωL — ESL |Z| total (sees both, plus ESR floor)

X5R/X7R MLCCs lose dramatic capacitance as you bias them with DC voltage. "10 µF 6.3V X5R" at 5 V applied may only be 3 µF. C0G/NP0 are flat. Electrolytic and tantalum stay near nominal.

What to notice

  • An MLCC is mostly air — Plate area is tens of mm², spacing is microns; the giant ε_r of X7R ceramic does the heavy lifting. C0G uses a low-ε ceramic, which is why C0G parts are physically larger for the same capacitance.
  • Above SRF, your bypass cap is an antenna — A 10 µF 0805 SRFs around 2–5 MHz. At 100 MHz it's a small inductor. That's why decoupling stacks pair a 10 µF bulk with a 100 nF and sometimes a 1 nF — each owns a different frequency band.
  • DC bias eats X5R alive — Drag the bias slider toward Vr and watch capacitance crash. The rule: size X5R parts at 2× the rated voltage if the cap value matters. Or use C0G when it must be exact.
  • ESR is the noise floor — Below SRF, ESR sets how low |Z| can go. Switching supply output caps use low-ESR types (polymer, ceramic) precisely because the ESR floor sets ripple voltage.
  • Electrolytic and tantalum don't derate with bias — But electrolytic has 100× the ESR of an MLCC. Pick by frequency, not just capacitance.

MEMORIZE

  • X7R/X5R DC-bias loss — drops 50–80 % at rated V_DC.
  • Tantalum — derate voltage 2× (operate at ≤ 50 % of rating).
  • Aluminum electrolytic — limited life; ESR rises as it dries out.
  • C0G/NP0 — stable; tiny C values, exact for filters/timing.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Cap is inductive above SRF — your "bypass" becomes an antenna.
  • Datasheet C ≠ operating C — ceramic capacitance shrinks under DC bias and temperature.
  • Polarity — reversing aluminum/tantalum kills them (often violently).
