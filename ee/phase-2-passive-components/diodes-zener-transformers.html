Diodes, Zener, transformers

Three near-cousins that all bend energy in deliberate ways: the diode rectifies, the Zener clamps, the transformer scales. Switch tabs to play with each — same shockingly simple equations underneath, very different jobs in a real schematic.

The diode equation I = Is·(e^(V/(n·VT)) − 1) is exponential — current rises by ~10× for each ~60·n mV increase in forward voltage (n is the ideality factor; ~60 mV/decade for an ideal n=1 diode, ~90 mV/decade for a typical n=1.5 silicon part, ~120 mV/decade at n=2). That's why "Vf" is a useful approximation: the curve is so steep that current pretty much pins to a fixed voltage once it conducts.

A Zener diode is just a regular diode that's allowed to operate in reverse breakdown at a precise V_z. Put it across the output of a series resistor and you get the simplest possible voltage regulator. Slide V_in past V_z and watch V_out flatten — but watch the power-in-Zener bar too.

V_out I_Z (Zener current) Current operating point
  • Below V_z — Zener is off; V_out follows V_in minus the load-driven R_s drop. No regulation here.
  • Above V_z — Zener clamps; excess current goes through it as I_Z. Power P_Z = V_z·I_Z. Pick R_s so I_Z stays in [I_zmin, P_max/V_z].
  • Heat budget — A 1 W Zener at 5.1 V can sink only ~200 mA. Linear regulators are wasteful; switching needed for high I.

An ideal transformer trades voltage for current at the ratio n = N₁/N₂, conserving power. Impedance reflects as the square: Z_pri = n²·Z_sec. That's the basis of audio-output matching, RF impedance transformation, and the working principle of every isolated power supply.

n = 10 · V₂ = 12.0 V · I₂ = 1.50 A · I₁ = 0.150 A · Z_pri = 800 Ω
  • Voltage scales with turns — V₂ = V₁/n. Step-down (n > 1) for utility-mains conversion; step-up (n < 1) for high-voltage gates and Tesla coils.
  • Current scales inversely — I₂ = n·I₁. Power conserved: V₁·I₁ = V₂·I₂ (ideal case).
  • Impedance reflects as n² — A 4 Ω speaker through a 1:5 transformer looks like 100 Ω at the primary. That's how a vacuum-tube output stage drives 8 Ω cones from a 5 kΩ plate impedance.
  • Power conservation is the law you can't break — Stepping up voltage 10× means stepping down current 10×. There's no free energy in a transformer.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE — V_f & V_T

  • Si diode — V_f ≈ 0.7 V.
  • Schottky — V_f ≈ 0.3 V.
  • LED red ≈ 1.8 V · green ≈ 2.1 V · blue/white ≈ 3.2 V.
  • Thermal voltage — V_T = 26 mV @ 25 °C.

DECISION RULE

  • Schottky — high-freq switching, low V_f drop, but leakier.
  • Si rectifier — general AC/DC, slower recovery, robust.
  • Zener — clamp / reference / over-voltage protection.
  • Transformer — V₂ = V₁/n · I₂ = n·I₁ · Z_pri = n²·Z_sec.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Zener heat — under load, dissipates V_z · I_z; size resistor + Zener wattage.
  • Transformer dot convention — same-polarity dots → in-phase; flipping a winding inverts the waveform.
  • Schottky leakage at high T — reverse current can climb 10× at 100 °C.
