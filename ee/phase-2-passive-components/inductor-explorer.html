Inductor explorer

An inductor is a coil — but the core material, current, and frequency conspire to hide that simple picture. Three things will surprise you: (1) cores saturate — past a certain current the magnetic field in the core can't get any stronger, so the coil's L collapses (the "saturation cliff"). (2) Self-resonant frequency (SRF) — windings have inter-turn capacitance, and above SRF the coil acts more like a capacitor. (3) I_sat on a datasheet usually means "the current at which L has dropped 30 %" — read the footnote.

Core material has finite magnetic permeability. Push enough current through the winding and the core's magnetic domains align fully — beyond that, the core can't store any more flux, so inductance falls toward the air-core value. This is saturation. Pick a core type and watch where the cliff is.

Above the SRF set by winding capacitance, the inductor's impedance starts to fall — it's now behaving like a capacitor. DCR sets the low-frequency floor; the parallel resonance with winding capacitance is the peak.

What to notice

  • Ferrite saturates earlier than iron powder — Ferrite has very high µr, so it stores a lot of flux per amp, but its magnetic domains saturate at low B. Iron powder has a distributed air gap mixed in, so it has lower µ (and needs more turns for the same L) but each grain saturates independently — the cliff smooths into a slope.
  • Air core never saturates — Set the core to "air" and the L curve is flat forever. The cost: huge winding for any meaningful L. That's why air-core inductors are only used in RF, where nH is enough.
  • Above SRF, your inductor is a capacitor — Just like a cap that turns inductive above its SRF. Your "100 µH choke" at 30 MHz might be a 2 pF cap. Always read the datasheet impedance plot.
  • DCR sets the noise floor; copper loss = I²·DCR — At low frequency, |Z| flattens at the DCR. In a buck converter at 1 A through 30 mΩ DCR, that's 30 mW of pure heat.
  • Isat is defined at 30 % L drop by convention — Some datasheets quote 10 %, some 30 %, some "temperature rise of 40 K". Compare like-for-like or you'll undersize the part.
  • Q = ωL/R drops at high f — Core losses (hysteresis, eddy current) climb with frequency. A ferrite that's Q=80 at 1 MHz can be Q=15 at 10 MHz. RF chokes use air or low-loss cores.

MEMORIZE

  • Ferrite — high µ, low Isat; great filters/EMI; saturates abruptly.
  • Iron powder — lower µ, higher Isat; distributed gap, soft saturation.
  • Air core — never saturates; tiny L; RF only.
  • Isat — usually quoted at 30 % L drop; check before comparing parts.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Operate well below Isat — leave margin; L collapses non-linearly at saturation.
  • Inductor is capacitive above SRF — winding capacitance dominates; |Z| drops.
  • Switching off an inductor — di/dt spike kills MOSFETs without a freewheel diode/snubber.
