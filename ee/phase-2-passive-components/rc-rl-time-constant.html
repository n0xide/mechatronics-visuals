What to notice

  • τ is the only thing that matters — Whether R is 1 kΩ + 1 µF or 1 MΩ + 1 nF, both give τ = 1 ms and look identical. The product RC sets the speed; the individual values are free design knobs (impedance, noise, leakage).
  • 63 / 86 / 99.3 — memorize these — At t = τ you've covered 63% of the gap, at 2τ 86%, at 5τ you're within 0.7%. "Wait 5τ for the transient to settle" is the working engineer's heuristic.
  • Same shape, different physics — RC stores energy in an E-field (½CV²), RL stores it in a B-field (½LI²). For RC τ = R·C grows with both R and C; for RL τ = L/R grows with L but shrinks with R. Toggle the mode and watch the slider direction flip meaning.
  • Time-domain ↔ frequency-domain — A slow RC (large τ) is a low-cutoff low-pass: f_c = 1/(2πτ). Pulling τ from 1 ms to 100 µs moves f_c from 160 Hz to 1.6 kHz — exactly how you tune an anti-alias filter to the ADC sample rate.
  • The peak current at t=0 — For RC charging the inrush is V/R (cap looks like a short). For RL the kickback when you open the switch is L·(di/dt) — limited by snubber, freewheel diode, or flying arc. Plan for it.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

τ (RC)
τ = R · C
τ (RL)
τ = L / R
step
V(t) = V_f · (1 − e^(−t/τ))
decay
V(t) = V_0 · e^(−t/τ)
f_c
f_c = 1 / (2π·R·C)

MEMORIZE

  • 1τ — 63 % of final value.
  • 2τ — 86 %.
  • 3τ — 95 %.
  • 5τ — 99.3 % (≈ "settled").

COMMON PITFALLS

  • 5τ ≠ instant — code/ADC sequences must wait at least 5τ before sampling.
  • Peak inrush — RC charging draws V/R at t = 0; size the source/switch for it.
  • RL kickback — opening an L mid-current creates a huge voltage spike; need a freewheel diode.
