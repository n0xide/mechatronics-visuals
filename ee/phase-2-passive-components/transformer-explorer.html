Transformer Explorer

A transformer trades voltage for current at the ratio of its turns. Connect a load on the secondary and the primary sees a reflected impedance — that's how a 4 Ω speaker hides behind an 8 kΩ tube plate, and how a USB-C charger talks to a 120 V wall. Slide N1/N2; watch V, I, and Z scale by the ratio (V), 1/ratio (I), and ratio² (Z). Then turn on the real-world parasitics: leakage inductance, magnetizing current, copper loss.

The three transformations: V, I, Z

An ideal transformer obeys two coupled laws (energy in = energy out, flux in = flux out). Those two laws produce three transformations of equal importance:

Faraday's law in each winding: V₁ = N₁·dΦ/dt, V₂ = N₂·dΦ/dt (same Φ since same core) Voltage ratio: → V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂ = a Conservation of energy (no losses): V₁·I₁ = V₂·I₂ Current ratio: → I₁/I₂ = N₂/N₁ = 1/a Reflected impedance: Z₁ = V₁/I₁ = (a·V₂)/(I₂/a) = a²·(V₂/I₂) = a²·Z₂ → Z₁ = a² · Z₂ — the rule that lets a 4 Ω speaker look like 4 kΩ to a 1000-Ω plate.

Real transformer parasitics

Switch the "Real" model above and three resistors / two inductors appear in the schematic. Each one is named — and each one matters.

Inductive (frequency-dependent)

Magnetizing inductance L_m — even with no load, the primary needs current to magnetize the core. L_m is large; reactive current is small but not zero. At DC, L_m looks like a short and the transformer can't pass DC at all.

Leakage inductance L_lk — flux that doesn't link both windings. Causes voltage spikes in flyback converters (snubber R+C kills them) and high-frequency rolloff in audio transformers.

Resistive (power-dependent)

Winding resistance R_cu — DC copper loss = I²·R_cu in each winding. The reason a 50 VA transformer is heavier than a 5 VA one: more copper, lower R_cu, less loss.

Core loss R_c — modeled as a resistor in parallel with L_m. Eddy currents + hysteresis. At line frequency, ~5 % of the rated power.

Coupling k — < 1 in real transformers. k=0.99 is excellent; k=0.95 is normal; k=0.7 is a flyback with a deliberate gap.

Worked example — tube power amp output

The constraints

Single-ended 6V6 plate at 250 V, plate current 35 mA. Plate dissipation = 250·0.035 = 8.75 W; max class-A audio output ≈ ¼ of that ≈ 2.5 W (use this as the design point P). Plate load wants ~5 kΩ for cleanest operation. Speaker is 8 Ω. Pick the turns ratio.

The arithmetic

Required Z_p / Z_s = 5000 / 8 = 625 a = √625 = 25 (turns ratio N1:N2) If N2 = 60 turns of #20 AWG: N1 = 1500 turns of #30 AWG (thinner because lower current) V_s at full power: V_p_rms = √(P·Z_p) = √(2.5·5000) ≈ 112 Vrms ; V_s_rms = 112/25 ≈ 4.47 Vrms ≈ 6.32 Vpk. (Check: V_s_rms² / Z_s = 4.47²/8 = 2.5 W ✓.) I_s at full power: 4.47/8 ≈ 559 mArms; I_p = 559/25 ≈ 22.4 mArms (plus 35 mA DC bias on the primary)

Why this works

The tube only knows it's looking at 5 kΩ — which is what it wants. The speaker only knows 4.47 Vrms is being applied to its 8 Ω — and absorbs 2.5 W. The transformer is the impedance matcher; without it, the tube wouldn't move the speaker at all (its plate impedance is 1000× higher than the cone).

Worked example — 5 V flyback SMPS

The constraints

Universal mains: 85–264 V_AC RMS input → roughly 100–375 V_DC on the bulk cap after bridge rectification (V_DC ≈ V_AC·√2 at high line, no/light load). Output: 5 V at 3 A. Switching MOSFET drain stress limit: 700 V. Pick the turns ratio.

The arithmetic

Reflected output voltage: V_or = a · (V_out + V_D) = a · (5 + 0.5) = 5.5·a (D = output Schottky) MOSFET drain stress at switch-off: V_in_max + V_or + spike margin ≤ 700 V Allocating: 375 + V_or + 130 (spike) ≤ 700 → V_or ≤ 195 → a ≤ 35 Picking a = 6 (typical for 5 V output): V_or = 33 V, peak drain = 375 + 33 + spike ≈ 480 V → comfortably under 700 V Primary peak current at 100 V_DC_min, 50 kHz, D = 0.4: I_p,pk ≈ 2·P/(V·D) ≈ 2·15/(100·0.4) = 0.75 A Secondary peak current: I_s,pk = a · I_p,pk = 4.5 A (needs an 8 A Schottky for margin)

What to notice

  • Z scales as a², V as a, I as 1/a. Drop a in half and Z drops by 4×. A 10:1 step-down means the primary side sees the load impedance times 100.
  • Transformers don't pass DC. The flux Φ has to change for V to appear in the secondary. A pure DC current produces a constant flux and zero secondary voltage. SMPS exploit this: you switch a DC source on/off to create AC for the transformer.
  • The "ideal" view ignores L_m, but L_m sets the low-frequency rolloff. Audio output transformers have huge cores (kg of iron) to make L_m large enough at 20 Hz.
  • Leakage inductance creates the snubber problem. When the primary switch opens, the energy stored in L_lk has nowhere to go — it spikes the drain voltage. Every flyback PSU has a snubber RC across the primary to dissipate that energy.
  • Galvanic isolation is free with a transformer. No DC path from primary to secondary. This is why mains-to-low-voltage power supplies use them — safety. Insulation rating (typically 1500–4000 V_RMS) is what gives "isolated" output rails.
  • Copper loss dominates at high current — I²R. Doubling current quadruples loss. Step-down transformers run hotter on the secondary because that's where the high I lives.

Where transformers show up

Real uses

  • Mains power supplies — 50/60 Hz line transformer + bridge + filter; large iron core, heavy.
  • Switch-mode (SMPS) — flyback, forward, half/full bridge; small ferrite core at 50 kHz–1 MHz; much smaller and lighter.
  • Audio output — push-pull or single-ended; matches tube plate impedance to speaker.
  • RF impedance match — balun (balanced–unbalanced); UnUns and step-up transformers in antenna feeds.
  • Isolation — medical, industrial, USB-PD. Insulation barrier rated kV_RMS.
  • Ethernet magnetics — small audio-frequency transformer on every RJ-45 to break ground loops between switches.
  • CT (current transformer) — one turn of mains wire passing through a toroid; secondary outputs current proportional to primary I, scaled by ratio.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

V ratio
V1/V2 = N1/N2 = a
I ratio
I1/I2 = N2/N1 = 1/a
Z reflection
Z_p = a² · Z_s
Power balance
V1·I1 = V2·I2 (ideal)
Faraday
V = N·dΦ/dt
Volt-second
B = (V·t)/(N·A_e) (avoid saturation)

SHORTCUTS

  • Step-down a > 1 → V drops, I rises, Z reflects up by a²
  • Step-up a < 1 → V rises, I drops, Z reflects down by a²
  • 1:1 isolation → no V/I change; only galvanic break
  • 4 Ω → 4 kΩ needs a = √1000 ≈ 31.6 (turns 32:1)
  • Audio-grade: k ≥ 0.99; flyback intentionally k ≈ 0.95

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Square-rooting wrong way — Z ratio is squared, so going Z_p → a uses √. Forgetting this gives you a 100× error.
  • DC through a transformer — saturates the core. Always design for AC; if DC is unavoidable (single-ended audio), use a gapped core.
  • No snubber on flyback primary — L_lk spike will exceed MOSFET V_DS rating and kill it. Always add R+C+D snubber.
  • Wrong polarity dot — dot at top of primary AND bottom of secondary inverts the phase; matters for push-pull and parallel windings.
  • Forgetting volt-second balance — in an SMPS, ∫V·dt during ON must equal ∫V·dt during OFF, or B drifts and the core saturates.
