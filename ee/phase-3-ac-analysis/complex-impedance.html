Complex Impedance vs. Frequency

Resistors, capacitors, and inductors each have a signature on a log-log impedance plot. Resistor is flat. Capacitor slopes down at −20 dB/decade (and sits at −90°). Inductor slopes up at +20 dB/decade (and sits at +90°). Combine them and the curves bend at corner frequencies.

What this teaches

Impedance generalizes resistance: Z_R = R, Z_L = jωL, Z_C = 1/(jωC) = −j/(ωC). Series adds Z; parallel adds Y=1/Z. The straight-line slopes on a log-log plot come straight from those formulas — multiplying ω by 10 multiplies |Z_L| by 10 (one decade up) and divides |Z_C| by 10 (one decade down).

What to notice

  • Slopes are integer multiples of 20 dB/decade — because |Z_L|=ωL and |Z_C|=1/(ωC) are pure powers of ω. Plot ω on a log axis and powers become straight lines.
  • R is the floor of the series RLC — at the resonant frequency f₀ = 1/(2π√LC), L's and C's reactances cancel and only R remains. The V-bottom of the orange/red curve sits exactly at R.
  • Parallel RLC inverts the shape — same f₀, but now the curve peaks at R (in an ideal lossless tank, it would peak at R·Q²). At resonance, L and C "swallow" each other and look like an open circuit.
  • Phase tells you who's winning — below f₀, the series circuit looks capacitive (−90° → 0°); above f₀, inductive (0° → +90°). The crossover at f₀ is exactly 0°.
  • Sliding R doesn't change f₀ — only damping. Watch the bottom of the V get sharper as R shrinks (high Q) and rounder as R grows (low Q).
  • Move the cursor through f₀ — the impedances |Z_L| and |Z_C| in the readout should match exactly when phase crosses zero.

|Z| = √(R² + X²)

MEMORIZE

  • C shorts at high f — |Z_C| → 0 as ω → ∞.
  • L opens at high f — |Z_L| → ∞ as ω → ∞.
  • Phase — R = 0°, C = −90°, L = +90°.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Don't sum magnitudes — sum complex numbers, then take |·|.
