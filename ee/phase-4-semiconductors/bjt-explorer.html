BJT Explorer

Four-tab tour of the bipolar junction transistor: physical structure, output I–V curves, switch-mode design, and common-emitter amplifier gain.

BJTs are current-controlled: a small base current Ib steers a much larger collector current Ic = β·Ib. The whole device economy collapses to picking your operating region — cutoff (off-switch), saturation (on-switch), or active (amplifier) — and choosing Rb, Rc, and Vcc so you land where you want.

In NPN the arrow on the emitter points out; in PNP it points in. Mnemonic: NPN = "Not Pointing iN."

Output characteristics (Ic vs Vce, stepped Ib)

β = 100. Each curve is a fixed Ib; the family sweeps Ib from 10 µA to 60 µA.

cutoff active (Ic = β·Ib) saturation

Curves rise steeply until Vce ≈ Vce_sat (~0.2 V) then flatten. In the flat region, Ic ≈ β·Ib regardless of Vce — that's the active region where amplifiers live.

Low-side NPN switch driving an LED

To guarantee saturation, you want β·Ib ≥ ~5× the load current (Ic_max from Vcc/Rc). Below that, the transistor stays in active region and acts as a current source — bad for switching, the LED dims.

Common-emitter amplifier — Av = −gm·Rc

Transfer: Vout = Vcc − Vin·Av (small-signal)

The inversion (Av is negative) means: Vin up → Ic up → drop across Rc up → Vout down. Bigger Rc gives more gain but pushes Q-point toward saturation — you trade headroom for gain.

What to notice

  • Three regions, one device — cutoff (Ib=0), active (Ic=β·Ib, Vce > 0.2 V), saturation (Vce ≈ 0.2 V, Ic limited by external Rc). Switch design lives in cutoff/saturation, amplifier in active.
  • Overdrive matters — for guaranteed saturation across temperature and β-spread, force Ib to 5–10× what β_min·Ic_load would need. Skimping leaves the BJT in active region, which dissipates more heat.
  • gm scales with Ic — gm = Ic/VT (VT = 26 mV at room temp). Higher bias current → more gain — but also more power dissipation and lower output swing headroom.
  • Inversion comes from the Rc drop — a CE stage flips signal polarity. Cascading two CE stages restores polarity (and squares the gain).
  • β is unreliable — vendor specs β as 50–300 for the same part number. Robust designs use voltage-divider bias plus emitter resistor so the Q-point is set by resistors, not by β.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • NPN — current C→E when V_B > V_E by ~0.7 V.
  • PNP — current E→C when V_B < V_E by ~0.7 V.
  • |V_BE| ≈ 0.7 V active; |V_CE_sat| ≈ 0.2 V.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • β drifts — varies with temperature and Ic.
  • Saturation kills β — use Ic = (Vcc − V_sat) / Rc, NOT β·Ib.
