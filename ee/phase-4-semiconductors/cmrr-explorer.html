CMRR Explorer

A difference amplifier outputs V_out = A_d·(V+ − V−) — but only if its resistors match exactly. Real resistors have tolerance (typically ±1 %), and that mismatch lets common-mode signals (V+ ≈ V−, both swinging together) leak into the output. CMRR measures how badly: CMRR = A_d / A_cm. For a textbook 4-R diff-amp at ±δ tolerance, CMRR ≈ (1+R2/R1) / (4δ); the 3-op-amp INA improves on that by stacking front-stage gain ahead of the back-end diff amp. The ideal vs the real difference amplifier

The standard difference amplifier uses 4 resistors, in two pairs: R1 (input) and R2 (feedback). The ideal output is V_out = (R2/R1)·(V+ − V−). The derivation assumes perfect matching: R1a = R1b, R2a = R2b. Reality: each resistor has tolerance δ, so their ratios differ by ~ 2δ.

Inverting input (V_-): KCL gives v_- = V+·R2/(R1+R2) (after also setting v_+ via R2/(R1+R2) divider) If pairs match exactly: V_out = (R2/R1)·(V+ − V−). A_cm = 0, CMRR = ∞. If R1a = R1·(1+δ₁), R1b = R1·(1+δ₂), R2a = R2·(1+δ₃), R2b = R2·(1+δ₄): the V_+ and V_- divider ratios differ. → Combined worst-case mismatch ≈ 4δ → A_cm ≈ 4δ·R2/(R1+R2) → CMRR = A_d / A_cm ≈ (1 + R2/R1) / (4δ) (standard Sedra/Smith result) For R2/R1 = 10 (gain 10) and δ = 1 %: CMRR = 11/0.04 = 275 ≈ 48.8 dB For 0.1 % resistors: CMRR = 275×10 = 2750 ≈ 68.8 dB. Each 10× tolerance improvement = 20 dB more CMRR. ⚠ Don't confuse this with the INA gain formula (1 + 2·R2/R_G) below — different topology, different expression.

Why the 3-op-amp INA is so much better

The instrumentation amplifier (3-op-amp INA) is a difference amp preceded by a high-impedance input stage that delivers gain. Two op-amps (a buffer + amplifier configured as a long-tail pair via R_G) provide differential gain (1 + 2·R2/R_G), while passing common-mode through with unity gain. Then the back-end diff amp subtracts the common mode at its low (unity) gain. CMRR scales linearly with the front-end gain.

Front stage differential gain: A_front = 1 + 2·R2/R_G Front stage common-mode gain: A_cm_front = 1 (both buffers pass it through) Back-end diff amp: A_back = R3/R3 = 1; A_cm_back = (4δ·R3) / R3 ≈ 4δ (limited by tolerance) Total differential gain: A_d = A_front · A_back = 1 + 2·R2/R_G Total CM gain: A_cm_total = A_cm_back ≈ 4δ (unchanged from back-end alone) → CMRR_INA = A_d / A_cm_total = (1 + 2·R2/R_G) / (4δ) Compare diff-amp CMRR ≈ (1+R2/R1)/(4δ): same form. Both topologies' CMRR scale linearly with gain; the INA's true advantage in production is not slope but the laser-trimmed δ (~0.01 %) and the high input impedance from the front-stage op-amps. A 100× gain INA at 1 % resistors: CMRR = 201/0.04 = 5025 ≈ 74 dB. A diff amp at the same gain 100 and 1 % resistors would also give ~74 dB — but you'd be hard-pressed to find a single-stage discrete diff amp at gain 100 with usable input impedance.

Worked example — ECG signal in a noisy lab

The setup

Human ECG signal: ±1 mV differential. Body acts as antenna for 60 Hz mains: ±1 V common-mode pickup. Signal-to-noise on differential: needs < 1 % distortion.

How much CMRR do we need?

CM = 1 V; want CM leakage at output ≤ 1 % of 1 mV signal at output Diff signal at output (gain 1000): 1000 · 1 mV = 1 V Allowed CM leakage at output: 1 % of 1 V = 10 mV CM at input = 1 V → max A_cm = 0.01 → CMRR ≥ 1000/0.01 = 100,000 = 100 dB

Pick the part

INA126 or AD8221 both deliver ≥ 100 dB at 60 Hz. A discrete diff amp with 1 % resistors would give only 50 dB — completely unusable for this signal. This is why you do not build precision medical instrumentation with discrete op-amps.

Worked example — strain gauge bridge

The setup

Wheatstone bridge with 350 Ω gauges, excitation 5 V. Common-mode at output of bridge: 2.5 V (mid-rail). Full-scale differential at output: ±10 mV. Need INA gain 200 to give ±2 V at output.

CMRR requirement

CM at INA input = 2.5 V; output CM with A_cm = 200/CMRR_lin For 0.1 % accuracy on a 2 V output: CM leakage ≤ 2 mV at output A_cm ≤ 0.002/2.5 = 0.0008 → CMRR ≥ 250,000 = 108 dB INA128 (120 dB typ) does this easily; INA126 (100 dB min) just barely meets spec.

What to notice

  • CMRR is a ratio, not an absolute number — you can have 80 dB CMRR with gain 10 (A_cm = 0.001) or gain 100 (A_cm = 0.01). Both are 80 dB.
  • Tighter resistor tolerance helps proportionally. 1 % → 0.1 % adds 20 dB. 0.1 % → 0.01 % adds another 20 dB. Beyond ±0.01 % becomes hard to find off-the-shelf.
  • Gain helps CMRR linearly in both topologies — every 2× gain adds 6 dB CMRR, whether INA or 4-R diff amp. The INA's real win isn't a different gain-CMRR slope, it's that its δ is laser-trimmed (~0.01 % vs the 1 % of discrete resistors), so the absolute CMRR floor is 40 dB higher at any gain. CMRR_diff ≈ (1+R2/R1)/(4δ); CMRR_INA ≈ (1+2R2/Rg)/(4δ).
  • CMRR is frequency-dependent. At DC: limited by R tolerance. At HF: limited by op-amp imperfect symmetry. Real INA datasheets specify CMRR(f). Above ~10 kHz, even an AD8221 drops to 80 dB.
  • Common-mode INPUT range matters too. Even with infinite CMRR, the INA's V+ and V− pins must stay within the supply rails. ±10 V CM on a 5 V INA = overflow, garbage.
  • Capacitive imbalance is just as bad as resistive. C-mismatch at the inputs degrades CMRR at HF even if R is perfect. INAs minimize this by trimming both R and C.

Where CMRR matters most

Real uses

  • Biomedical (ECG/EEG/EMG) — body's 60 Hz pickup is enormous; CMRR ≥ 100 dB mandatory.
  • Industrial strain gauges — bridge biased to mid-rail; mV signal at output.
  • High-side current sensing — shunt at 48 V or higher rail; INA measures voltage across shunt despite huge CM.
  • Differential I/O — RS-485, USB, HDMI; CMRR rejects ground-loop pickup between transmitter and receiver.
  • Long sensor cables — capacitive pickup on a 10 m thermocouple cable is mostly common-mode; high CMRR receiver kills it.
  • Audio balanced lines — XLR cables on a stage carry V_diff between pins 2 and 3; CMRR rejects hum from poor ground reference.

RULES OF THUMB

  • 1 % resistors → ~50 dB CMRR. 0.1 % → ~70 dB. 0.01 % → ~90 dB.
  • Integrated INAs achieve 100–130 dB with laser-trimmed resistors.
  • Add 20 dB per gain decade for INA (free CMRR boost).
  • CMRR drops at HF; use AC CMRR plot from datasheet.
  • Common-mode INPUT range must respect rails.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Using 5 % carbon resistors in a "precision" diff amp → CMRR ≈ 40 dB. Mains noise dominates.
  • Mismatched cable capacitance on the two input lines — kills HF CMRR even with perfect resistors.
  • Driving INA from high impedance source asymmetrically → input bias-current effects create offset.
  • Ignoring CM input range — INA still has DC swing limits inside its supply rails.
  • "Triboelectric" cable shake in long cables → low-freq CM noise, often not specified.
  • Ground-fault loop at the receiver — INA expects V_cm finite; rip ground reference, CM blows past limits.
