LDOs and Voltage References

Two panels — first how an LDO holds Vout regulated while Vin droops; second how Zener, band-gap, and buried-Zener references compare on tempco and use case.

Linear regulators answer a different question than switching converters. They burn the difference between Vin and Vout as heat — inefficient, but quiet and cheap. A "low-dropout" (LDO) variant uses a PMOS pass element so Vin only needs to exceed Vout by ~200 mV instead of the 2 V a classic 7805 demands. References go further: instead of just regulating, they aim for a precise, stable absolute voltage trimmed at the factory.

Headroom: Vin − Vout = 1.7 V. Dissipation: P_diss = (Vin − Vout) × Iload + Vin × Iq = 170 mW. Efficiency: 66%   (drops linearly as Vin grows — LDOs are terrible from a 12 V rail to 3.3 V).

PSRR vs frequency

PSRR (power supply rejection ratio) measures how much of Vin's ripple appears at Vout. Typical LDO: 60–80 dB at low frequency, rolls off above the feedback bandwidth (~10–100 kHz). That's why an LDO downstream of a switching converter scrubs the 100 kHz–1 MHz switching noise — but only partially.

Panel 2 — Voltage references compared

A reference's job is to produce a precise voltage independent of Vin and temperature. Tempco (ppm/°C) is the master figure of merit: 100 ppm/°C of 2.5 V = 250 µV/°C, which over a 50°C swing means 12.5 mV total drift — fine for 8-bit ADC, dreadful for 16-bit.

What to notice

  • LDO efficiency = Vout/Vin — going 12 V to 3.3 V at 1 A in an LDO burns 8.7 W as heat. Use a switcher first to get close, then LDO to clean. The LDO's PSRR rejects switcher ripple.
  • Dropout sets the minimum input — once Vin falls below Vout + V_do, the regulator drops out of regulation and Vout follows Vin minus V_do (roughly). Battery-powered designs run with very little headroom near end-of-life.
  • Quiescent current matters in standby — for a coin-cell sensor sleeping 99% of the time, an LDO with 1 µA Iq lasts years where one with 100 µA Iq lasts weeks.
  • PSRR rolls off with frequency — at DC an LDO has 80 dB rejection; at 1 MHz maybe 20 dB. Pair with a small LC filter for the worst switching harmonics if it matters.
  • References have ~3 tiers — Zener (cheap, drift), band-gap (most ADC refs you'll meet), buried-zener (instrumentation, voltage standards). Match your reference's tempco and noise to the ADC's effective number of bits.
  • Initial accuracy vs tempco are separate specs — a 2.5 V reference can be ±0.5% from part to part *and* drift 50 ppm/°C in any single part. Calibration handles the first; only physics handles the second.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • V_in ≥ V_out + V_do — dropout typ. 200 mV for LDO.
  • Iq matters for battery standby — µA vs mA = years vs weeks.
  • PSRR drops with f — 80 dB at DC, 20 dB at 1 MHz.

DECISION

  • LDO — low noise, V_in close to V_out.
  • Switcher — high efficiency, large step-down.
  • Band-gap — most common reference, ~1.25 V base.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Output cap ESR window — too low can cause instability on older LDOs; check datasheet stability range.
