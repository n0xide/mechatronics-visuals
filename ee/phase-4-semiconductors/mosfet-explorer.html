MOSFET Explorer

Four-tab tour of the N-channel MOSFET: physical structure, output I–V family, switch-mode RDS_on & gate charge, common-source amplifier.

MOSFETs are voltage-controlled: the gate–source voltage Vgs creates (enhancement) or pinches (depletion) a conducting channel between drain and source. No gate current flows in steady-state — the gate is a capacitor sitting on top of an oxide insulator. Saturation current obeys a square law: Id ∝ (Vgs − Vth)².

Enhancement vs Depletion — N-channel

The oxide insulator is what makes a MOSFET draw zero gate current in DC steady-state — utterly unlike a BJT. The gate is a tiny capacitor (Ciss) charged by your driver. Almost every power MOSFET in hobby designs is enhancement-mode; depletion devices appear in current sources and old JFET-based op-amp front ends.

Output characteristics (Id vs Vds, stepped Vgs)

Vth = 2.0 V. Curves are drawn at Vgs = Vth+0.5, +1.5, +2.5, +3.5, and +5 V (the +0.5 V curve is the lowest active trace; at exactly Vgs = Vth the device is at cutoff threshold).

cutoff (Vgs < Vth) triode (Vds < Vgs−Vth) saturation (Vds > Vgs−Vth)

The dashed boundary Vds = Vgs − Vth separates the resistive (triode) region from the constant-current (saturation) region. Switches operate in deep triode — Vds tiny, Id set by external load. Amplifiers operate in saturation — Id ≈ K·(Vgs−Vth)², flat against Vds.

Operating point summary

When Vgs falls below Vth, the channel disappears and Id collapses to leakage (~µA). The danger zone is partial-on: Vgs around Vth gives small Id but big Vds, so I·V can be 10× the steady-state dissipation — that's why fast gate transitions matter and why power FETs need real gate drivers.

Common-source amplifier — Av = −gm·Rd

Transfer (small-signal around Q-point)

Compared to a BJT at the same bias current, MOSFET gm is much lower (Id/VT for BJT vs 2·Id/Vov for FET; Vov ≫ VT=26 mV). MOSFET amplifiers compensate with much larger Rd and benefit from infinite input impedance.

What to notice

  • Gate draws no DC current — the oxide is an insulator. All gate current is transient: charging Ciss to switch states. Average Ig over many cycles = Qg × fsw.
  • Triode vs saturation — boundary is Vds = Vgs − Vth. Switches want triode (small Vds, set by Rload); amplifiers want saturation (flat Id, gain set by gm·Rd).
  • RDS_on is Vgs-dependent — a "logic-level" FET specced at Vgs=4.5 V can be 3–5× higher RDS_on at Vgs=3.3 V. Always check the curve, not just the headline number.
  • Switching loss dominates above ~50 kHz — Qg·fsw·Vcc / 2 grows linearly with frequency. At 1 MHz on a 12 V rail with Qg=30 nC, switching loss alone is ~180 mW.
  • Square-law I–V — Id ∝ (Vgs−Vth)² in saturation. Halving overdrive quarters the current; FETs are softer current sources than BJTs (where Ic is exponential in Vbe).

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • V_GS > V_th turns the channel on.
  • Logic-level FETs spec R_DS_on at V_GS = 2.5 or 4.5 V.
  • Gate is capacitive — no DC current, only Q·f transient.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Slow body diode — add a Schottky in parallel for fast switching.
  • Logic-level vs standard — at 3.3 V drive, a standard FET barely turns on.
