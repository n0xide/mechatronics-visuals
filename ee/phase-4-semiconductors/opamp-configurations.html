What to notice

  • Inverting vs non-inverting — same closed-loop gain math (1/β), but inverting has finite Rin and a virtual ground, non-inverting has near-infinite Rin and no inversion. Choose by input impedance need.
  • Buffer is the cheapest impedance fix — when a load is too low for a source, drop in a unity-gain follower. Costs one op-amp and zero design effort.
  • Difference amp needs matched resistors — CMRR (common-mode rejection ratio) is limited by resistor matching. 1% resistors give ~40 dB CMRR; for >80 dB you need an instrumentation amplifier IC (matched on-chip).
  • Integrators saturate from offset — any DC input offset is integrated forever. Real integrators add a large R parallel to C to cap DC gain.
  • Differentiators amplify noise — gain rises with frequency. Series R at input rolls off high-frequency noise; pure differentiators are rarely used outside textbook problems.
  • Plain comparator chatters on noise — without hysteresis, a noisy signal crossing the threshold toggles the output many times. Add positive feedback (Schmitt) and the output stays clean.

MEMORIZE

  • Inverting — virtual ground at the − input.
  • Non-inverting — R_in ≈ infinite (no current at +).

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Integrator drifts on offset — add a bleeder R across C.
  • Differentiator amplifies HF noise — add series R at input.
