Op-Amp Fundamentals

Visualize the two golden rules (V+ = V−, no input current), feedback's tradeoff between gain and bandwidth, and slew-rate's effect on fast signals.

An ideal op-amp has infinite open-loop gain A_ol, infinite input impedance, zero output impedance, and infinite bandwidth. Real op-amps approach the ideal closely enough that, under negative feedback, you can treat them as ideal — which lets you solve any feedback circuit using two rules:

The Golden Rules (only when negative feedback is present)

1. Open-loop vs closed-loop gain

A real op-amp has finite A_ol (say 100,000×). Negative feedback "spends" that surplus gain to lock the closed-loop gain to a ratio of resistors, immune to A_ol drift.

log: 100 to 1,000,000

Feedback fraction β = 1/A_cl_target. Actual closed-loop gain: A_cl = A_ol / (1 + β·A_ol).
When β·A_ol >> 1, A_cl → 1/β = A_cl_target. When β·A_ol ≈ 1, real A_cl falls short.
Loop gain (in dB): 80 dB. Larger = better precision.

2. Gain–bandwidth product (GBP)

For a unity-gain-compensated op-amp, A_cl × BW = GBP = constant. Pick gain; bandwidth is the leftover. Cheap op-amp GBP: 1 MHz. Premium video op-amp: 1 GHz.

log: 100 kHz to 1 GHz

−3 dB bandwidth: BW = GBP / A_cl = 100 kHz. At A_cl=100, BW shrinks 10×. The open-loop response is the "ceiling" — closed-loop never exceeds it.

3. Slew rate — the large-signal speed limit

Slew rate (V/µs) is the maximum output rate of change. A square wave's edges get linearly ramped instead of vertical. A sine wave at frequency f and amplitude V can be reproduced only if 2π·f·V ≤ SR.

log: 100 Hz to 1 MHz
input (ideal square) output (slew-limited)

Edge time to traverse 2·V: t_edge = 2·V / SR = 10 µs. Full-power bandwidth f_FP = SR / (2π·V) = 31.8 kHz.

What to notice

  • Golden rules require feedback — without negative feedback, the op-amp railed. V+ = V− is a consequence, not a fact, and it's enforced by the output reaching whatever value makes it true.
  • Closed-loop gain is set by resistors — drag A_ol down to 1000 and a 10× amplifier is still ≈9.9×. Drag A_ol down to 100, and you'll see the closed-loop start to deviate. This is the precision-by-feedback bargain.
  • GBP is the universal currency — choose between gain and speed. You cannot have both above the part's GBP. Cascading two A_cl=10 stages gives the same total gain with √(GBP_total) more bandwidth.
  • Small-signal vs large-signal speed — GBP says how fast tiny signals propagate; slew rate says how fast big swings can change. The smaller of the two limits you. For audio you usually hit GBP first; for fast logic interfacing, slew rate.
  • Full-power bandwidth — at amplitude V, an op-amp can output a clean sine only up to f_FP = SR/(2π·V). Try shrinking V on the slider — f_FP grows.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • Golden rules — V+ = V−, and I_in = 0.
  • A_ol ≈ 100 dB = 100,000× open-loop.
  • Both rules require negative feedback.

FORMULAS

GBP
A_cl × BW = GBP (constant)
Closed BW
BW = GBP / A_cl

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Slew rate is separate — limits large-signal BW independently of GBP.
  • GBP doesn't help if the SR ceiling is lower — the smaller one wins.
