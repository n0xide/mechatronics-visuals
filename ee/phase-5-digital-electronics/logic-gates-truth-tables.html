NAND as universal — step by step

A NAND gate alone can build every other gate. CMOS process designers ship NAND-only cell libraries for this reason. Each panel shows the construction with live signals — toggle A and B above to see them flow.

NOT from NAND

Tie both inputs together. NAND(A,A) = ¬A.

AND from NAND

NAND, then NOT (which is also a NAND). ¬¬(A·B) = A·B.

OR from NAND

Invert each input then NAND. By De Morgan: NAND(¬A,¬B) = A+B.

What to notice

  • The bubble means inversion — NAND = AND + bubble, NOR = OR + bubble. Once you see it that way, the symbols stop being a memory test.
  • XOR is the "different" gate, XNOR is the "equal" gate. XOR is what an adder uses for its sum bit.
  • Every CMOS gate has dual networks — a PMOS network that pulls the output to VDD and an NMOS network that pulls it to GND. They are never on at the same time.
  • NAND is faster than NOR in CMOS for the same transistor count. NAND has its slow PMOS network in parallel (only one PMOS needs to turn on to pull HIGH); NOR has PMOS in series, so two slow devices stack. To match speed, NOR's PMOS must be widened, making NOR cells bigger. That is why NAND, not NOR, dominates real libraries.
  • NOT-from-NAND is "free" — tying inputs together is just a routing choice, no extra transistors.

NAND-ONLY BUILDS

  • NOT = NAND(A,A) — tie inputs together.
  • AND = NAND then NOT (2 NANDs).
  • OR = NOT each input, then NAND (3 NANDs).
  • Bubble rule — bubble = inversion; pushing through swaps AND↔OR.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Fan-out limit — CMOS DC fan-out is essentially unlimited (rail-to-rail outputs); the real limit is speed. Each added input adds ~5 pF of gate capacitance, slowing the rising/falling edge. Practical rule: ~10 loads at typical speeds.
  • Propagation delay — total path delay = sum of gate delays, not max.
  • Floating inputs — unused inputs float to noise; tie to VCC or GND.
