Battery Charging & Protection

CC/CV profile for a 1S Li-Po, plus the protection toolkit: reverse-polarity FET, OVP, UVLO, OCP, thermal shutdown.

Lithium chemistries demand strict charge management. Push past 4.20 V per cell and the electrolyte starts to plate lithium on the anode (recoverable cycles drop fast; severe overcharge causes thermal runaway). Pull below ~2.5 V and copper from the current collector dissolves into the electrolyte; dendrites form on next charge. CC/CV is the standard: bulk the energy in at constant current while voltage rises, then hold voltage steady while current tapers. Stop when the taper current falls below C/10.

What to notice

  • CC phase delivers most of the energy — typically 60–80% of capacity is bulked in during CC. The CV phase only adds the last 20–40% but takes nearly as long, because the cell internal resistance limits how fast it can absorb at fixed voltage.
  • 0.5–1C is the sweet spot — slower (0.1C) is gentle on the cell, faster (2C) heats it. Above 1C, expect noticeable capacity fade over hundreds of cycles. Modern fast-charge Li-Po cells are rated to 3–5C, but it's a wear-life tradeoff.
  • Termination matters — keeping CV indefinitely "tops up" the cell but accelerates aging. Most chargers terminate at I < C/10 (e.g., 100 mA for a 1000 mAh cell), then stop. Some restart top-off if V drops below ~4.05 V.
  • P-MOSFET reverse polarity beats Schottky — a Schottky diode drops 0.4 V at 1 A = 0.4 W loss. A P-FET drops I·R_DSON = 20 mV at 1 A = 20 mW. The body diode provides the initial path; once V_in is positive, V_GS = -V_in turns the channel fully on.
  • OCP requires hysteresis — naive comparator OCP will oscillate at the threshold. Real OCP latches off and requires a fault-clear (cycle V_in, or recover after timer).
  • UVLO ≠ over-discharge protection — UVLO disables the converter when V_in is too low to regulate. To protect the battery from over-discharge, use a separate cell-voltage monitor IC (e.g., DW01A for 1S, BQ7693 for multi-cell packs).
  • Thermal shutdown is last-resort — by the time the IC hits 150 °C, the PCB is hot, components are stressed, and the user notices. Design for the heat first: copper area, thermal vias, ambient margins. TSD should fire only on unexpected faults.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Reverse-biased tantalum — instant short; tantalums fail unforgivingly under reverse polarity.
  • Fuse sized too tight — PPTC rating = 125–150% of max load, else nuisance trips on inrush.
