Boost Converter — Step-Up DC/DC

Inductor stores energy from V_in then dumps it through a diode at higher voltage. V_out = V_in / (1-D).

A boost converter takes a lower DC voltage and raises it. Unlike buck — where the inductor passes filtered DC — boost pumps the output: while the switch is ON, the inductor stores energy from V_in (output is supplied by C_out alone); while OFF, the inductor's collapsing field flings current through the diode into the load. There is no way to get V_out < V_in (the diode prevents it). Boost cannot regulate at D=1 (output collapses), so a minimum input voltage is required. Watch for the right-half-plane zero: stepping D up causes V_out to dip momentarily before rising — this is a fundamental control limit.

Topology — non-synchronous boost (TPS61023-style)

ONQ closed: V_in → L → GND. L charges. C_out supplies load alone. OFFQ open: L drives current through D → V_out. L discharges into C_out + load.

Inductor current and switch node — one period

I_L — inductor current (continuous in CCM) V_SW — switch node voltage (0 when ON, V_out when OFF)

RHP zero — step duty cycle up, watch V_out

Why: raising D shortens the OFF time → less time for L to deliver to output → C_out discharges → V_out drops. Only once I_L grows larger does V_out rise. This non-minimum-phase behavior limits feedback bandwidth on boost converters.

V_out ≤ V_in: a boost cannot regulate below its input. The diode forward-biases and V_out will just sit at V_in − V_D regardless of duty cycle.

What to notice

  • The diode is essential — without it, when Q opens, the inductor current would have nowhere to go (voltage spike, blown switch). The diode + C_out provide the path; the diode prevents reverse current.
  • Input current ≠ output current — average inductor current is I_out/(1-D), always higher than I_out. A 3.7 V → 12 V boost at 0.5 A draws ~1.62 A from V_in. Sizing wires and the inductor saturation rating is critical.
  • You can't fully turn it on — D→1 means the switch never opens, so the inductor never delivers energy to the output. Real ICs cap D around 90% and impose a minimum V_in (e.g., TPS61023 needs ≥0.5 V to start, ≥0.8 V to maintain).
  • RHP zero limits speed — control loop bandwidth on a boost is typically <1/10 of f_sw. If you want fast transient response, use a buck-boost or pre-buck topology.
  • Feedback divider sets V_out — the IC servos FB pin to V_ref by driving D. With V_ref = 0.5 V and V_out = 5 V, the divider must be 1:9 (R1=90 kΩ, R2=10 kΩ). Pick total resistance to keep divider quiescent current at ~5 µA.
  • PFM at light load — to keep efficiency high when I_out is tiny, the IC drops out of continuous switching and pulses periodically. Output ripple goes up but average current draw plummets.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Duty
D = 1 − V_in / V_out
I_L avg
I_L = I_out / (1 − D)
Divider
V_out = V_ref·(1 + R1/R2)

MEMORIZE

  • D → 1 is unbounded — if V_in dips too low, you can't reach V_out.
  • RHP zero — limits loop BW; output dips before it rises.
  • Input ripple low — good for battery sources.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • BW above RHP zero — for CCM boost, f_RHPZ = R_load·(1−D)²/(2π·L). Keep loop crossover well below this (rule of thumb: ≤ f_RHPZ/5) for stability.
  • Output ripple > input ripple — diode conducts pulses into C_out; needs low-ESR caps.
