Buck Converter — Step-Down DC/DC

High-side switch chops V_in. Inductor averages the chopped waveform into smooth V_out. Duty cycle D = V_out/V_in.

A buck converter trades current for voltage. When the high-side MOSFET is ON, the inductor sees (V_in - V_out) across it and current ramps up. When OFF, the freewheeling diode conducts and the inductor sees -V_out, so current ramps down. The output capacitor smooths the triangle current into a near-DC voltage. Choose L to keep ripple current ΔIL below ~30% of load. Choose C_out and f together to hit your output voltage ripple spec.

What to notice

  • Duty cycle is just a voltage ratio — D = V_out/V_in. To step 12 V down to 3.3 V, the switch is on 27.5% of the time. This is steady-state — ignore startup transients.
  • Inductor current is a triangle riding on DC — average equals I_out, peak-to-peak equals ΔI_L. The DC component does the work; the AC ripple is overhead.
  • Higher f or higher L → less ripple — both reduce ΔI_L proportionally. Higher f means smaller passives but more switching loss; bigger L means lower loss but a bigger, costlier inductor.
  • Light load drops you into DCM — when I_out falls below ΔI_L/2, the inductor current hits zero each cycle. Voltage regulation changes, output ripple shape changes, control loop bandwidth drops. Most ICs handle this automatically (PFM mode at light load).
  • Output ripple lives in C_out — for a given ΔI_L, doubling C_out halves ΔV_out. But ceramic cap ESR/ESL adds its own ripple; real designs combine bulk + ceramic.
  • Synchronous vs diode — replacing the freewheeling diode with a low-side MOSFET (Q2) saves the ~0.4 V diode drop. At 1 A out, that's 400 mW recovered. Below ~10 V output, this matters a lot.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Duty
D = V_out / V_in
Ripple
ΔI_L = (V_in − V_out)·D / (f·L)
V ripple
ΔV_out ≈ ΔI_L / (8·f·C_out)

MEMORIZE

  • ΔI_L target — 20–30% of I_out at rated load.
  • Sync buck — Q2 replaces diode, saves V_F drop.
  • CCM/DCM line — I_out > ΔI_L/2 keeps you in CCM.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • L too small — ripple climbs, RMS loss eats efficiency.
  • Light-load DCM — regulation and BW shift; verify across load range.
