Converter Output Filters

An SMPS output looks like a sawtooth riding on the desired DC: that's switching ripple. A single L+C low-pass shaves it; adding a downstream L+C stage (often called a "pi filter" loosely, though a true 3-element C-L-C from a voltage source is only 40 dB/dec — the model here treats it as two cascaded LC stages, 80 dB/dec total) gives 40 dB/decade extra attenuation; a stage with ESR-conscious C choice keeps high-frequency spikes off the rail. Why a low-pass filter is the entire game

An SMPS chopper produces a square waveform with edges at f_sw (typically 100 kHz to 2 MHz). After the inductor, the current is a triangle (continuous-conduction mode) sitting on the DC average. The cap removes the AC, leaving DC. The filter's job is to provide a low-impedance path to ground at f_sw and its harmonics, while not loading the DC.

Inductor ripple current: Δi_L = (V_in − V_out) · D / (L · f_sw), where D = V_out/V_in (duty cycle) This triangle current flows into the cap; the cap acts as charge buffer. Charge added per cycle: ΔQ = Δi_L / 8 / f_sw (area of triangle) Cap voltage ripple from charge alone: ΔV_C = Δi_L / (8·f_sw·C) Cap ESR creates an additional ripple: ΔV_ESR = Δi_L · ESR (just IR drop) → ΔV_o ≈ ΔV_C + ΔV_ESR (peak-to-peak)

The pi-filter (C-L-C) for extra attenuation

Add a small inductor and a second cap downstream of the converter's existing output cap. With the converter's own L+C_out already providing one 2nd-order stage, the added L+C cascades a second 2nd-order stage. Above both resonances, that's 80 dB/decade rolloff total instead of 40. Cost: extra components, extra layout, slight DC voltage drop across the added L. Used for: low-noise audio, RF reference rails, sub-mV-ripple ADC supplies.

Single L-C (buck L + C_out): |H(f)| ≈ 1 / |(f/f_res)² − 1| above f_res; rolloff 40 dB/dec Cascaded LC (buck L + C_out, then post-pi L + cap): rolloff 80 dB/dec above the higher of the two resonances. A standalone pi (voltage-source-driven C-L-C) is only 40 dB/dec — it's the buck L doing the first 40 dB/dec. At f_sw of 500 kHz, with both f_res ≈ 10 kHz: single stage gives 40·log₁₀(50) ≈ 68 dB attenuation; cascaded gives 80·log₁₀(50) ≈ 136 dB In practice, ESR floors the achievable attenuation around −80 to −100 dB; below that, layout and ground noise dominate.

ESR — the silent destroyer of low ripple

Datasheet ripple specs assume the cap acts as a pure capacitor. Real electrolytic caps have ESR (equivalent series resistance) of 50–500 mΩ at room temperature, often 2× worse at −40 °C. The triangle current going through ESR creates an IR drop that's linear, not capacitor-charge.

For SMPS output filtering, the modern best practice is a mix: a large electrolytic (47–470 µF) for bulk + a small ceramic (1–10 µF, X5R) for high-frequency. The electrolytic absorbs cycle-to-cycle ripple; the ceramic kills high-frequency spikes that the electrolytic's ESL can't.

Worked example — buck converter 12V → 3.3V at 2A

The design constraints

V_in = 12V, V_out = 3.3V, I_o = 2A, f_sw = 500 kHz. Spec: ΔV_o ≤ 30 mV pp. Δi_L = 30 % of I_o = 600 mA.

Pick L

D = V_out/V_in = 0.275 L = (V_in − V_out)·D / (Δi_L · f_sw) = (8.7·0.275)/(0.6·5e5) = 7.97 µH → pick 10 µH Recalculate Δi_L with 10 µH: 0.478 A (24 % — within spec)

Pick C

Required ΔV_C from cap alone: ≤ 20 mV (saving 10 mV for ESR) C = Δi_L / (8·f·ΔV_C) = 0.478 / (8·5e5·0.02) = 5.97 µF — pick 10 µF for margin Cap rating: V_out + margin = 6.3 V (so derate by 50 % → 6.3 V → 10 V cap) Cap I_rms = Δi_L/√12 = 138 mA — anything > 200 mA rated is fine

Cap selection

Murata GCM31MR71H106KE36 — 10 µF X7R, 50V, ESR 4 mΩ, I_rms 5A. Overkill, but cheap and bulletproof. Add a 100 nF in parallel for HF cleanup.

Achieved ΔV_o = ΔV_C + ΔV_ESR = 12 mV + 2 mV = 14 mV → 50 % spec margin.

Worked example — audio rail with sub-mV ripple

The constraints

Headphone amp +/-9 V rails, draw 200 mA each. Switcher front-end at 800 kHz. Target ripple: ≤ 200 µV pp on rails.

The math

Buck-boost converter output already has ~20 mV ripple at C1 — 100× too much vs the 200 µV spec. Need additional 40 dB → 100× attenuation at 800 kHz. Add post-stage: L_pi = 2.2 µH, C2 = 22 µF + 100 nF, downstream of the existing C1 = 47 µF. f_res_pi = 1/(2π·√(2.2 µH · 22 µF)) = 22.9 kHz; at 800 kHz that's 35× above → 40·log₁₀(35) ≈ 62 dB extra attenuation (2nd-order rolloff) 20 mV / 10^(62/20) = 20 mV / 1259 ≈ 16 µV — well under the 200 µV spec.

Caveat

Even with 200 µV electrical ripple, audio amp PSRR must be > 50 dB; otherwise the amp itself injects supply ripple into the audio. Choose op-amps like OPA1611 (110 dB PSRR @ 1 kHz, 75 dB @ 100 kHz).

What to notice

  • The ripple formula has Δi_L not I_o. Doubling load current doesn't double ripple (unless inductor saturates); it's the AC component that matters.
  • Higher f_sw helps everywhere: smaller L, smaller C, smaller ripple. Cost: more switching loss in the FET. Modern GaN switchers run at 1-5 MHz.
  • ESR limits ripple to a floor of Δi_L · ESR. No amount of capacitance reduces ESR ripple — you have to pick low-ESR caps (POSCAP, ceramic).
  • Two small ceramics in parallel beat one big electrolytic for HF ripple. ESL (inductance) of an electrolytic is ~5–10 nH; that's a 60 Ω impedance at 1 MHz.
  • Cap ESR rises 2-5× at −40 °C. For automotive/outdoor designs, derate or use POSCAP/MLCC.
  • Pi-filter inductor must carry full I_o. Don't undersize it; the inductor is in the main current path.
  • Resonance is dangerous below f_sw. If the LC filter resonates at f_sw or below, ripple amplifies instead of attenuating. Always place f_res > f_sw / 10.
  • Layout matters as much as components. A 2 mm trace = 2 nH; at 1 MHz, that's 12.6 Ω. Place caps close to the switch node ground.

Where filtering matters most

Real uses

  • SMPS output stages — every buck, boost, buck-boost, SEPIC, flyback.
  • ADC reference rails — 12-bit ADCs need µV-level cleanliness on VREF.
  • Audio supplies — DAC/op-amp rails must be quiet.
  • RF transmitter supplies — switching noise modulates the carrier.
  • PLL/oscillator supplies — power-supply noise = phase noise.
  • Long sensor cable shields — cable inductance + ground = pi-filter inadvertently.
  • EMI compliance — conducted emissions (FCC Part 15) require LC filtering at switcher frequencies.

RULES OF THUMB

  • Δi_L target: 20–40 % of I_o for buck.
  • Place f_res < f_sw / 10 for clean 40 dB attenuation.
  • ESR: ceramic ≪ POSCAP ≪ electrolytic.
  • Add a 100 nF ceramic in parallel with bulk cap for HF.
  • Derate cap voltage 50 % for reliability.
  • Cap I_rms rating ≥ Δi_L/√12.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Skipping ESR in the spec sheet calc — get nasty surprise at scope.
  • Ignoring ceramic DC bias derate — X5R can drop 50 % capacitance at rated V.
  • Resonance at f_sw — accidental amplification of ripple.
  • Long traces between cap and load — ESL adds 5 nH, ruins HF filtering.
  • Tantalum on input cap — inrush surge can short the tantalum (failure mode).
  • Forgetting cold ESR — automotive must specify −40 °C.
  • Single cap "ought to be enough" — high-current SMPS need 2-4 caps in parallel for ESR + ESL.
