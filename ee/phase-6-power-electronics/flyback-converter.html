Flyback Converter — the isolated buck-boost workhorse

The flyback is the most common isolated DC-DC topology under ~150 W. It uses a single coupled inductor (called a "flyback transformer," though it's not really a transformer — primary and secondary never conduct simultaneously) to store energy when the switch is on and release it through the secondary diode when the switch is off. Same topology as a buck-boost, but isolated. Phone chargers, PoE PDs, low-cost AC adapters, set-top boxes, smart-meter supplies — virtually all under-100 W mains-fed switchers are flybacks. This visual lets you build one interactively: sweep V_in / V_out / load / frequency / turns ratio, watch DCM-vs-CCM operation, primary peak current, and snubber voltage.

The math behind the flyback

During switch ON (time D·T_sw): Primary current ramps: di/dt = V_in / L_p Energy stored: E = ½ · L_p · I_pk² Secondary diode reverse-biased (V_in/N + V_out across it) During switch OFF (time (1−D)·T_sw): Secondary diode conducts; energy transfers to C_out + load Secondary current = N · I_pri (referenced) V_DS on switch = V_in + N · (V_out + V_F) + leakage spike CCM transfer function (current never zero): V_out / V_in = (1/N) · D / (1−D) Solve for D: D = N·V_out / (V_in + N·V_out) DCM transfer function (current goes to zero each cycle): V_out = V_in · D · √(R_load · T_sw / (2·L_p)) / N ⇒ V_out depends on load — needs feedback Boundary between CCM and DCM: L_p_crit = R_load · (1−D)² · T_sw / 2 (referred to secondary) Above L_p_crit: CCM. Below: DCM. Peak primary current (CCM): I_pk = I_avg + ΔI/2 where I_avg = P_in/(V_in·D), ΔI = V_in·D·T_sw / L_p Peak primary current (DCM): Energy per cycle: E = P_out / (η · f_sw) = ½ · L_p · I_pk² ⇒ I_pk = √(2·P_out / (η · L_p · f_sw)) Switch voltage: V_DS = V_in + N·(V_out + V_F) + V_spike(L_lk) Snubber clamps V_spike. Without snubber: V_spike can be 50-200 V.

Snubber design — clamping the leakage spike

When the switch turns off, the energy in the transformer's leakage inductance (~1-5% of L_p) has nowhere to go on the secondary side (diode is forward-biased, but mutual coupling is imperfect). This energy ramps the drain voltage up — sometimes 100-300 V above the reflected V_out — until the snubber circuit absorbs it.

Three common topologies

  • RCD snubber (resistor + cap + diode): cheapest. Diode catches the spike, cap absorbs energy, resistor bleeds it off between cycles. Dissipates the entire leakage energy as heat in R. Typical clamp voltage: V_clamp = 1.5-2× reflected V_out. R sized so V_clamp·V_clamp / R ≈ leakage power.
  • TVS / Zener clamp: a high-voltage TVS or Zener absorbs the spike directly. Simpler than RCD; less efficient (no recovery), but compact. Common for small adapters.
  • Active clamp: a second MOSFET + cap. Recycles the leakage energy back to V_in. 1-3% efficiency gain, more cost + control complexity. Used in high-end USB-PD bricks (Apple, GaN-based designs).

Leakage energy per cycle

E_lk = ½ · L_lk · I_pk² P_lk = E_lk · f_sw (all dissipated in RCD R) Example: L_lk = 5 µH, I_pk = 2 A, f = 100 kHz: E_lk = 10 µJ, P_lk = 1 W

Worked example — 65 W USB-C PD charger (the AC-adapter flyback)

Spec

Universal-input AC charger: 90-264 V AC → 20 V / 3.25 A = 65 W USB-PD output. After PFC + bulk cap, the flyback sees ~380 V DC bus (high-line) down to ~110 V (low-line). Target 100 kHz switching, > 90% η.

Design walkthrough

Pick turns ratio N = N_p/N_s. Reflected V_out: V_or = N · (V_out + V_F) ≈ N · 20.5 V Choose V_or = 120 V (V_DS_max = 380 + 120 + spike → 650 V switch with margin) → N = 5.85, round to 6 Switch must handle 380 + 120 + ~100 V spike = 600 V → 650 V SiC MOSFET or 700 V Si Operate in CCM at high-line, transition to DCM at low-line + light load High-line CCM duty: D = 6·20.5 / (380 + 6·20.5) = 123/503 = 0.245 Low-line CCM duty: D = 6·20.5 / (110 + 123) = 0.528 → designs check < 0.5 for transformer reset margin Pick L_p for CCM at half load: I_avg = 65 / 380 = 171 mA → L_p = V_in·D·T_sw / (2·ΔI_ratio·I_avg) For 30% ripple ratio: L_p ≈ 1 mH. Use 800 µH (standard core). Peak primary current at high-line CCM: ΔI = 380 · 0.245 · 10 µs / 800 µH = 1.16 A I_avg = 65 / (380 · 0.245) = 0.70 A · I_pk = 0.70 + 0.58 = 1.28 A Secondary peak: I_sec_pk = N · I_pk = 6 · 1.28 = 7.7 A → SiC Schottky or fast Si diode rated 10A/100V

Off-the-shelf controllers: TI UCC28780 (active-clamp flyback), STM L6566B, ON NCP1252. Add a synchronous rectifier on the secondary for 1-2% η improvement.

Quasi-resonant + valley switching — the free EMI win

Standard flyback hard-switches: when the SW turns on, V_DS jumps from clamped voltage to 0 instantly, dumping C_DS energy into the channel as loss. Quasi-resonant (QR) mode waits for the drain ringing to hit a "valley" (minimum) before turning on — soft(er) switching.

  • Operates always in DCM, so secondary current rings down before next pulse
  • Controller waits for the drain ring minimum (often 2nd or 3rd valley at light load) and fires at that moment
  • Frequency varies with load (lighter load → fewer rings before threshold)
  • EMI is 6-10 dB lower than fixed-f flyback because switching frequency spreads naturally
  • Common for sub-30 W adapters — Apple's classic 20 W charger is QR

Common pitfalls

  • Sizing the transformer for steady-state instead of cold-start surge — at startup, output cap is empty, full duty for tens of µs. Use peak-current limit to protect.
  • Skipping the snubber — leakage spike kills the switch in seconds at full load. Always include RCD or TVS, even on bench prototype.
  • Wrong gap calculation — flyback core gap stores ~all the energy. Wrong gap → wrong L_p → wrong saturation point. Verify with the gap formula: L_p = µ₀·A_e·N² / l_g.
  • RHP zero in CCM — pushes the maximum control-loop bandwidth to ~1/5 of f_sw. Don't try to cross over higher; the loop becomes unstable.
  • Forward-active clamp gotcha — different from flyback active clamp. Don't grab the wrong app note.
  • Magnetizing-current asymmetry — in heavily loaded flybacks, asymmetric switching can lead to flux-walking. Always use current-mode control with cycle-by-cycle limit.
  • EMI filter undersized — flyback's I_pk pulses 10× the average create huge LF EMI. Plan for a meaty common-mode + differential filter.
  • Loading regulation — at very light load (1% rated), flyback enters burst mode or skips cycles; V_out can ripple. Use minimum dummy load resistor (~ 1% rated current).
