Gate Drivers & Dead-Time

Half-bridge with bootstrap, PWMH/PWML with dead-time, and the shoot-through vs efficiency vs EMI tradeoff.

A MOSFET's gate is a capacitor — typically 1–10 nF for the kind of FETs you'd put in a buck or motor driver. To switch fast, you must charge and discharge that capacitance fast, which means sourcing amps of current for nanoseconds. An MCU pin maxing out at 8 mA cannot do this; you need a gate driver. In a half-bridge, things get worse: the high-side MOSFET's source is the switch node, which swings to V_in volts, so its gate must be driven above V_in. That is what the bootstrap capacitor solves.

Bootstrap cap charging

When Q_L is on, SW = 0 V and C_BS charges through D_BS to ≈VCC. When Q_H is on, SW rises to V_in and C_BS — now sitting V_in above ground — feeds the high-side gate driver, putting HO at V_in + VCC. The diode prevents discharge.

Shoot-through risk! t_dead ( ns) is shorter than the rise/fall time ( ns). Both FETs will be partially on at the transition — V_in to GND through low-resistance path. Either lengthen dead-time or lower R_G.
Excessive dead-time: t_dead is much longer than rise/fall. The body diode conducts unnecessarily, dropping ~0.7 V × I_load. Shorten dead-time to recover efficiency.

What to notice

  • R_G is a peak-current-limiting resistor — VCC=12 V, R_G=10 Ω gives ~1.2 A peak gate drive. Bigger R_G slows the FET (lower dV/dt, less EMI, more switching loss). Smaller R_G is faster but more EMI and ringing.
  • Dead-time is non-negotiable — any time both FETs are on simultaneously, V_in shorts through both R_DSON. Hundreds of amps for tens of ns can blow them outright. Always insert dead-time greater than worst-case (t_r + storage time + driver delay mismatch).
  • Body diode conducts during dead-time — both FETs off, but inductor wants to keep current flowing. Body diode of the low-side FET turns on, dropping 0.7 V instead of R_DSON·I. That dissipates extra power. The longer the dead-time, the worse this is.
  • Bootstrap cap must be sized — C_BS must hold enough charge to drive Q_H's gate (and supply leakage) through the longest high-side ON time. Rule of thumb: C_BS ≥ 10·Q_g/ΔV (where ΔV is acceptable C_BS voltage droop, e.g., 0.1 V).
  • Bootstrap fails at 100% duty — if the low-side never turns on, C_BS never recharges. The high-side gate slowly droops and eventually fails to fully enhance. Use a charge pump or isolated supply for permanent high-side ON.
  • dV/dt couples through C_GD — fast switching at SW capacitively couples back to the gate of the OFF FET via its Miller capacitance. If the OFF gate is high-impedance, it can be pulled up and partially turn on (Miller-induced shoot-through). A low-impedance gate-pull-down or a Miller clamp helps.

MEMORIZE

  • Bootstrap recharges when low-side is ON (SW pulled to GND).
  • Dead-time — prevents shoot-through; both FETs OFF.
  • Gate driver I_pk — 1–4 A; MCU pin is ~8 mA.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • PWMH/PWML delay mismatch — uneven prop delay → effective dead-time vanishes → shoot-through.
  • Dead-time too long — body-diode conduction wastes efficiency and bakes the FET.
