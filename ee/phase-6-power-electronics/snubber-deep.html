Snubber Deep-Dive — clamping leakage-inductance spikes

When a switch (MOSFET, IGBT) interrupts current in a transformer or inductor, the magnetic energy stored in leakage inductance (the part that didn't couple to the secondary) has nowhere to go. Voltage spikes 100-500 V above the operating point until either: the snubber absorbs it, a body diode reverse-recovers, or the silicon dies. This visual sizes three snubber topologies — RCD, TVS clamp, and active clamp — from the same inputs (L_lk, I_pk, f_sw, V_clamp), shows the energy budget, and quantifies the efficiency hit of each.

E_lk = ½·L_lk·I_pk² per cycle P_lk = E_lk · f_sw (dissipated by passive snubber) V_clamp typ 1.5-2× reflected V_or for flyback RCD: cheap, dissipative · TVS: compact, hot · Active clamp: recycles energy Leakage: typ 1-5 % of L_p for hand-wound transformers, 0.5-1 % for planar

The leakage-energy math

Energy stored in leakage inductance at switch-off: E_lk = ½ · L_lk · I_pk² [J per switching cycle] Average power that the snubber must absorb (passive case): P_lk = E_lk · f_sw [W] When switch opens at I_pk, voltage across L_lk would ring up to (theoretically) infinity if unclamped. With clamp at voltage V_clamp_total = V_in + V_or + V_clamp_excess: Energy in clamp = ½ · L_lk · I_pk² (all of it, since current must go to zero) Plus: continued leakage current discharges into the clamp at (V_clamp_excess) until current = 0 RCD snubber sizing: Pick V_clamp_excess (capacitor's ripple voltage, typical 10-30 V on top of average) R_snub determines dissipation: P_lk = V_clamp_excess² / R_snub ⇒ R_snub = V_clamp_excess² / P_lk C_snub holds the clamp voltage between cycles: τ = R·C should be 5-20× T_sw ⇒ C_snub = (5 to 20) · T_sw / R_snub TVS clamp: single Zener / TVS rated at V_clamp_total. Absorbs E_lk in each pulse. Dissipates same P_lk as RCD, but in the silicon. Use TVS rated > 5 × P_lk peak power. Active clamp: series cap + auxiliary MOSFET on the high side. The cap stores the leakage energy then releases it back through the magnetizing path at the next cycle. Roughly recovers 70-90 % of E_lk; the remainder is switching loss in the aux MOSFET. Typical efficiency improvement: +1-3 %.

RCD design walkthrough

1. Pick V_clamp_total (the limit you'll allow V_DS to reach)

V_clamp_total = V_in + V_or + V_clamp_excess. Choose so that V_DS_peak + 20 % margin ≤ MOSFET V_DS rating. Example: 700 V SiC MOSFET, V_in = 380 V, V_or = 125 V → V_clamp_excess_max = 700·0.8 − 380 − 125 = 55 V. Pick V_clamp_excess = 50 V.

2. R from average dissipation

P_lk = ½ · L_lk · I_pk² · f_sw R = (V_clamp_excess)² / P_lk Power rating ≥ 2 × P_lk (conservative for thermal margin)

3. C from ripple

Allow ~10 % ripple on V_clamp_excess across one cycle ΔV = E_lk / (C · V_clamp_excess) ΔV ≤ 0.1 · V_clamp_excess ⇒ C ≥ 10 · E_lk / V_clamp_excess²

4. Pick D (the snubber diode)

Reverse-recovery matters — fast or ultrafast diode rated for V_clamp_total + 20 %, average current ≥ 2 × I_lk_avg (typically 100-500 mA). MUR series or SiC Schottky.

Why active clamp wins above 65 W

Active clamp adds an auxiliary MOSFET (Q_aux) and clamp capacitor (C_clamp) in series across the primary winding. When the main switch (Q1) turns off:

  1. Leakage current dumps into C_clamp through Q_aux's body diode → C_clamp voltage rises (clamping V_DS)
  2. Q_aux turns on at zero current (ZCS) — no turn-on loss
  3. Magnetizing current reverses, charges back through Q_aux, partially recovering the leakage energy
  4. Q_aux turns off when current reaches zero (ZCS again)
  5. Main switch Q1 turns on at zero voltage (ZVS) thanks to the previous leakage-discharge cycle pre-resonating C_oss

Net: leakage energy that would have been heat in an RCD now circulates between L_lk and C_clamp, with only ESR + transistor on-state losses. Typical efficiency gain over RCD: 1-3 percentage points. At 65 W, that's 1-2 W less to dissipate. At 1 kW, 10-30 W. Worth the cost above ~65 W.

Modern GaN-based USB-PD bricks (Apple 65W, Anker GanPrime) almost all use active clamp. ICs: TI UCC28780, ON NCP1568, Power Integrations InnoSwitch.

Worked example — 65 W USB-PD flyback (PD 3.0)

Spec

Universal-input USB-PD charger: 90-264 V AC → ~380 V DC bus (after PFC + bulk) → 20 V / 3.25 A = 65 W. Flyback with N = 6, L_p = 800 µH, L_lk = 4 % of L_p = 32 µH. Wait, that's high — typical hand-wound is 1.5-3 %. For this example let's use L_lk = 16 µH (2 %). I_pk = 1.5 A (at high-line). f_sw = 100 kHz. 700 V SiC MOSFET.

RCD design

E_lk = ½ · 16e-6 · 1.5² = 18 µJ per cycle P_lk = 18e-6 · 100e3 = 1.8 W ← lost to heat in the snubber V_or = 6 · (20 + 0.5) = 123 V (reflected output + diode drop) V_clamp_total budget: 700 · 0.8 = 560 V max → V_clamp_excess = 560 − 380 − 123 = 57 V R_snub = 57² / 1.8 = 1805 Ω → use 1.8 kΩ, 3 W rated C_snub = 10 · 18e-6 / 57² = 55 nF → use 47 nF, 200 V film cap (margin for ripple) Diode: UR2J (UF4007 fast-recovery, 600 V, 2 A avg) — ample for 250 mA avg

Active-clamp alternative

Replaces R + 1.8 W heat with Q_aux (e.g., 600 V GaN, 20 mΩ) + 100 nF C_clamp + isolated gate drive Power recovered: 80 % of 1.8 W = 1.44 W back to V_in → only 0.36 W lost Efficiency: (1.8 − 0.36) / 65 = 2.2 % point improvement (from ~92 % → ~94.2 %) [80 % of 1.8 W recovered] Cost adder: ~$1.50 (Q_aux + driver IC); 65 W charger BoM ~$5 — adds 30 % Worth it for premium chargers (Anker Nano Prime); skip for entry-level (Apple base brick)

Common pitfalls

  • No snubber at all — even on a bench prototype with light load, leakage spikes will kill the switch within hours. Always include at least a TVS during bring-up.
  • Snubber under-rated for power — R selected for nominal load, then a step-load doubles I_pk → 4× E_lk → R cooks. Always design for worst-case I_pk (current limit threshold).
  • Diode reverse-recovery rings — slow Si diode (1N400x) recovers slowly and rings the leakage loop. Always use fast / ultrafast / SiC Schottky in snubber position.
  • RCD discharges through MOSFET — when MOSFET turns on, R_snub × τ_RC could be longer than t_on, leaving residual voltage. Design τ_RC = R·C ≤ 5 · T_sw.
  • Active clamp at light load — at < 20 % load, magnetizing current may not be enough to fully discharge C_oss for ZVS. Controller drops to burst mode (skip cycles) to maintain conditions.
  • Asymmetric clamp on push-pull — symmetrical topology needs matched snubbers per leg; one cold + one hot → uneven loss + premature failure.
  • Sat reactor saturation point — designed for nominal current; transient current saturates it, removes the dI/dt limit → leakage spike returns.
  • Loop layout — keep the snubber loop (D, R, C) short. Long traces add ~10 nH/cm parasitic, which COUPLES BACK to the primary as additional ringing.
