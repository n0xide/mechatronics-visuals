EMC Basics

Three demos: loop area drives radiated emissions; ferrite beads turn into resistors at RF; common-mode and differential-mode noise behave differently on a twisted pair.

EMC is the discipline of not radiating into your neighbor's spectrum (emissions) and not being upset by their broadcasts (immunity). The three pictures below cover the three building blocks: current loops radiate, ferrite beads absorb, and twisted pairs cancel.

Radiated field ∝ loop area × frequency × current

A current loop is a magnetic dipole antenna. Radiated field strength E ∝ A · f² · I in the far field. Small loop area is your single biggest EMC lever — way more effective than shielding.

Halve the loop area — halve the radiation. Doubling frequency quadruples the field. This is why high-speed designs obsess about return-path proximity.

Ferrite bead impedance vs frequency

A ferrite bead is a frequency-dependent resistor. At DC it's just a few mΩ — passes power without loss. Above ~10 MHz the magnetic core absorbs energy, behaving like a resistor (50–600 Ω typical). Above the bead's self-resonant point it goes capacitive and impedance drops again. The bead is a power-rail noise filter, not an inductor.

Cyan = total |Z|; orange = resistive R(f); green = reactive X(f). The peak at ~100 MHz is the rated frequency — choose bead by impedance at the noise frequency you're trying to kill.

When to use a ferrite bead

  • Power rails of noisy ICs — keep switching noise from contaminating supply.
  • Cable entries — clip-on ferrites on USB/Ethernet cables absorb common-mode radiation.
  • Not in series with high-dI/dt switching nodes — at high current the bead's R dissipates real watts.

Common-mode vs differential-mode noise on a twisted pair

Differential mode (DM): the signal — currents flow in opposite directions on the two wires, fields cancel in the far field. Common mode (CM): noise — currents flow in the same direction on both wires, fields add, both wires act as one antenna.

DM currents (opposite directions) → fields from each wire cancel. CM currents (same direction) → fields add, large radiated signal. Twisting makes the loop area in any direction small over a half-twist length, so common-mode receive immunity improves too.

What to notice

  • Loop area is king — every dB you can shave off the loop is a dB you don't have to recover with shielding. Route signal and return on adjacent layers.
  • Ferrite beads are resistors above their break frequency, not chokes. They dissipate noise as heat in the core, which is exactly what you want for a filter.
  • Pick the bead by the impedance you need at the noise frequency, not by inductance. A "600 Ω @ 100 MHz" bead can be 50 Ω at 10 MHz and useless.
  • Differential signaling = first-line EMC defense — LVDS, USB, Ethernet, CAN all use it because the receiver sees only the difference and the radiation cancels in the far field.
  • CM chokes ≠ DM inductors. Same physical part, different winding direction. A wrong-wound choke does nothing.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • Loop area × dI/dt = radiated emission.
  • Ferrite bead — high Z at HF, low DC R.
  • TVS — clamp V > signal swing, breakdown > supply.

DECISION

  • CM choke on power lines for common-mode noise.
  • Ferrite bead at IC power pin for HF rail noise.
  • TVS at every I/O connector.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • FCC Class B applies to residential — stricter than Class A.
  • Pre-cert modules save 6 months and a lot of $$$.
