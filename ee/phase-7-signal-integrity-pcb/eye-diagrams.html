Eye Diagrams — overlaying bit periods to see what your signal looks like

Take a long bit sequence on a high-speed serial bus, overlay all bit periods on top of each other on a scope (or with software), and you get an "eye" — a shape that reveals everything wrong with the channel: ISI (inter-symbol interference) closing it horizontally, jitter shaking the edges, amplitude noise closing it vertically. The eye height and width tell you how much margin you have before bit errors. This visual builds the eye step by step and lets you sweep parameters to see what closes it.

What closes the eye

  • ISI (inter-symbol interference) — high-frequency components attenuated by lossy traces, so a 1 doesn't fully transition before the next bit. The trailing edge of one bit blurs into the next, reducing eye height.
  • Reflections — impedance mismatches send energy back which overlaps later bits. Visible as ringing on the edges.
  • Jitter — clock-recovery imperfections move the bit sample point around. Widens edge crossings, reduces eye width.
  • Crosstalk — adjacent signals couple in. Random-looking noise floor in the eye.
  • Voltage noise — switching power, ground bounce, EMI. Closes eye vertically.

How to fix a closed eye

Closure sourceFix
ISI from lossy tracesPre-emphasis at transmitter; CTLE / DFE at receiver
ReflectionsSeries-terminate at source or parallel-terminate at sink; check stack-up Z₀
JitterCleaner clock source; PLL with better phase noise; spread-spectrum if it's RF interference
CrosstalkWider trace spacing (3W rule); ground guard traces; differential pair routing
Voltage noiseBetter decoupling; ferrite beads on supply; separate plane for analog

Worked example — USB 3.0 SuperSpeed eye on a 6-inch FR-4 trace

The setup

5 Gbps USB 3.0 (Gen 1), 6-inch differential trace on standard FR-4, 90 Ω differential impedance. What's the eye look like at the receiver?

The numbers

UI = 1 / 5e9 = 200 ps
FR-4 loss at 2.5 GHz (Nyquist of 5 Gbps): ~0.4 dB/inch → 2.4 dB over 6 inches
Eye height at TX: ±400 mV (USB 3 spec)
After 2.4 dB loss: ±400 × 10^(−2.4/20) = ±303 mV (closed by ISI; high-freq attenuated more)
USB 3 RX spec: ±100 mV minimum at RX after CTLE → fits with ~200 mV margin
Total budget: 2.4 dB trace + 1 dB connector ≈ 3.4 dB. USB 3 spec allows up to 12 dB end-to-end → headroom

If the trace gets longer

At 12 inches: 4.8 dB loss, eye drops to ±230 mV. Borderline without CTLE. Add CTLE peaking at 2.5 GHz to flatten the channel response — now ±330 mV with clean eye.

At 24 inches: 9.6 dB loss, raw eye ±130 mV. Requires CTLE + DFE (decision feedback equalizer) at RX. This is standard in USB 3 PHY chips (e.g., TUSB1310A, PI3DPX series).

The TX pre-emphasis / RX equalization toolkit

TechniqueWhereWhat it does
De-emphasisTXReduce voltage on long runs of same bit — emphasizes transitions over DC content
Pre-emphasisTXBoost the first bit after a transition — overdrives high-freq content lost to ISI
CTLE (Continuous-Time Linear Equalizer)RX analogHigh-pass / peaking filter that compensates for channel rolloff. ~10 dB of equalization at Nyquist.
DFE (Decision Feedback Equalizer)RX digitalSubtract ISI from previous bits using feedback loop. Adds another 6-10 dB equalization.
FFE (Feed Forward Equalizer)RX or TXFIR filter that compensates the channel impulse response. Common in PCIe Gen4+.

Measuring eye in the lab

  • Mask test on scope — most modern scopes (Keysight, Tek) have eye-mask analysis built in. Trigger on clock, accumulate many UIs, check if any sample falls inside the mask polygon. Pass = zero violations after N samples (typical N = 10^9 to validate 10⁻⁹ BER).
  • BERTscope — dedicated bit-error tester sweeps decision threshold + sample time, builds a bathtub curve. Reveals the eye's true horizontal and vertical margin.
  • SDR-based — for slower interfaces (1 Gbps and below), an oscilloscope or even SDR can capture and software-correlate to build the eye offline.
