Thermal via array for power packages

Why: a single via is ~80 °C/W thermal resistance. An 18-via array drops Θj-pad from ~80 to ~4 °C/W — enough to handle a 1 W loss without exceeding Tj-max. Standard for any QFN with a thermal pad, switching FET, regulator, or LED driver.

What to notice

  • Schematic ERC and PCB DRC catch different things — ERC checks net connectivity, DRC checks copper clearances. Both must pass; skipping either is a common bring-up regret.
  • Footprint mistakes are the #1 first-board failure — wrong pin pitch, wrong pad shape, mirrored package. Always 3D-render before fab to spot mismatches.
  • 2-layer is fine up to ~25 MHz with careful GND pour. Above that, the lack of a solid reference plane bites you in EMC. 4-layer adds maybe $5 to a small prototype board — worth it.
  • Inner planes need stitching vias — connect inner GND to top/bottom GND with a generous via grid near every IC and around the board edge for shielding.
  • Gerber export wrong layer set is a classic — JLCPCB's standard expects F.Cu, B.Cu, F.Mask, B.Mask, F.Silkscreen, B.Silkscreen, Edge.Cuts, and an Excellon drill file. KiCad's "Plot" preset for JLC handles this; double-check the layer checkboxes.
  • Thermal vias scale linearly — double the vias, halve the thermal resistance. Don't be shy with the array under any heat-generating package.

Worked example — 4-layer Pico W cyberdeck PCB

Board specs

100×75 mm, 4-layer, JLCPCB economical (1.6 mm board, 1 oz copper). Houses: Pico 2 W (RP2350), 3.5" SPI TFT, LoRa module (RYLR998), keyboard matrix, 1S LiPo charger.

Stackup

LayerFunctionWhy
1 (top)Components + signalsSMD pads + short signal runs; everything visible for assembly
2 (inner)Solid ground planeReference for all top-layer signals (controlled impedance, low EMI)
3 (inner)Power planes split: 3V3 / VBAT / VSYSLocal supply for each chip, low IR drop
4 (bottom)Spillover signals + GND fillsLong signal runs that didn't fit on top; GND pour stitches plane 2

Routing rules

Signal traces: 0.2 mm (~8 mil) / 0.15 mm clearance — comfortable margin over JLC's 5 mil (0.127 mm) standard-tier minimum; reduces yield risk on prototypes
Power traces: 0.5 mm min for 500 mA; calc trace width with PCB-calculator visual for higher currents
SPI to TFT: 8 MHz, no impedance match needed (short, no reflection at this speed)
LoRa antenna trace: 50 Ω microstrip on top, referenced to GND on layer 2. The relevant dimension is the L1→L2 prepreg, NOT the total 1.6 mm stackup. With ~0.25 mm prepreg / ~0.4 mm trace / 1 oz Cu / εr ≈ 4.4 FR-4: Z₀ ≈ 52 Ω. Fine-tune W (or use fab's stackup calculator) to land on 50 Ω.

Decoupling caps

Pico 2 W: 100 nF × 4 (one per VDD pin pair, < 3 mm via to plane 2)
TFT + LoRa: each gets a local 1 µF + 100 nF pair
Bulk: 10 µF + 47 µF at VSYS input (battery side)

Cost vs 2-layer

JLCPCB 2-layer 100×75 mm: $5 for 5 boards. 4-layer same size: $24 for 5 boards. The $19 delta buys: clean GND plane (better EMC + impedance control), separate power plane (less IR drop), 2× routing area (smaller board possible). For a noisy mixed-signal design like a cyberdeck with WiFi/BT/LoRa, this delta pays back in fewer revisions.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • 1 oz Cu = 35 µm thick (1 mil ≈ 25.4 µm).
  • JLCPCB standard min — trace/clearance 5 mil (0.127 mm), via drill 0.3 mm. Their "precision" tier goes to 3.5 mil at extra cost.
  • FR4 εr ≈ 4.2-4.5 (drops slightly with frequency; ~4.3 at 1 GHz, ~4.0 at 10 GHz).

DECISION — STACKUP

  • 2-layer — slow digital (< ~25 MHz) with careful GND pour, simple analog. No real reference plane.
  • 4-layer — anything fast (USB, DDR, HDMI, RF), mixed-signal, or EMC-sensitive. Solid reference plane on L2 is the win.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Silkscreen over pads = DRC fail. Move it off.
  • Thermal vias under exposed pad need solid or 4-spoke connection or they get starved by the relief.
