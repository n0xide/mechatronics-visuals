Skin Effect — current crowds to the surface at high frequency

At DC, current spreads uniformly through a conductor. At MHz and above, it crowds to the outer "skin" — a layer of thickness δ = √(2ρ/(ωμ)). Effective cross-section shrinks, so AC resistance RAC rises as √f. This is why high-frequency power inductors use Litz wire (many tiny strands) and why thick PCB power traces stop helping above ~10 MHz. This visual lets you sweep frequency, see δ collapse, watch current density redistribute across the cross-section, and read RAC/RDC at every step.

Math behind skin depth

Inside a good conductor, Maxwell's equations reduce to the diffusion equation: ∇²E = jωµσE Plane-wave solution into the conductor: E(z) = E₀ · exp(−z/δ) · exp(−jz/δ) Skin depth: δ = √(2 / (ωµσ)) = √(2ρ / (ωµ)) ρ = 1/σ = resistivity (Ω·m), µ = µ₀·µ_r, ω = 2πf Copper convenience formula (µ_r = 1): δ_Cu = 0.0660 / √f meters ⇒ δ × √f = 66 mm·Hz1/2 Examples: 60 Hz → 8.5 mm · 1 MHz → 65 µm · 1 GHz → 2 µm For a solid round wire of radius r: r ≪ δ: current uniform, R_AC ≈ R_DC r ≫ δ: current in an annulus of thickness δ at the surface A_eff ≈ 2π·r·δ (vs DC area π·r²) R_AC / R_DC ≈ r / (2δ) Litz wire (N stranded, each radius r_s < δ): each strand returns to R_DC behavior. N ≈ (r / r_s)² strands of the smaller wire to match the original cross-section. Practical: r_s ≤ δ/2 keeps R_AC ≈ R_DC. The "strands needed" scoreboard above uses r_s ≈ δ as a lower-bound count; tighten to r_s ≈ δ/2 (4× more strands) for true R_DC recovery.

Where it matters

  • High-frequency power inductors (SMPS, 100 kHz - 1 MHz) — solid wire's RAC dominates; use Litz wire (many insulated strands) so each strand stays smaller than δ.
  • RF coils + antennas (MHz - GHz) — Q = ω·L / R; RAC kills Q. Silver-plate the wire for the outer 2-5 µm and skin-conduction shifts into the lower-resistivity silver.
  • PCB power planes — for 50 MHz return currents, only the top few µm of copper conducts. 2 oz copper is barely better than 1 oz at HF — the bulk is dead weight.
  • Bus bars in motor drives (50-400 Hz at high amperes) — even mains frequency has noticeable skin effect at hundreds of amps. Bus bars are flat (high surface-to-volume ratio) to mitigate.
  • Transmission-line conductors — Z₀ at HF depends only on geometry, not the wire's R; but RAC adds attenuation (dB/m grows as √f).
  • Ferromagnetic shields and chokes — δ in pure iron (µ_r≈5000) at 60 Hz is ~0.3 mm; in mild steel (µ_r≈1000) ~0.6 mm — both far smaller than the ~8.5 mm in copper. That's why laminated cores need such thin laminations (0.35 mm typical for grain-oriented silicon steel).

Three frequency-specific worked examples

1. 60 Hz mains — overhead transmission line (ACSR cable)

Aluminum Conductor Steel Reinforced (ACSR), 25 mm overall diameter (r = 12.5 mm). δ_Al at 60 Hz = √(2·2.65e-8 / (2π·60·1.257e-6)) ≈ 10.6 mm.

r / δ = 12.5 / 10.6 = 1.18 (just above 1) R_AC/R_DC ≈ r/(2δ) only valid for r ≫ δ; at r/δ ~1, exact Bessel result ≈ 1.05× Result: only ~5% extra resistance at 60 Hz — acceptable for transmission lines.

That's why steel-cored ACSR works: the steel adds tensile strength but contributes little electrically; current rides in the aluminum's outer shell at 60 Hz.

2. 100 kHz SMPS transformer winding

Half-bridge converter at 100 kHz, primary 8 A RMS. Solid AWG 14 (1.628 mm dia, r = 0.814 mm)?

δ_Cu @ 100 kHz = 0.0660 / √1e5 = 209 µm r / δ = 0.814 / 0.209 = 3.9 R_AC / R_DC = 3.9 / 2 = 1.95× Loss ≈ doubled vs DC — bad. Switch to Litz: ~65 strands × AWG 32 (r_s = 0.1 mm < δ; ≈ matches AWG 14's cross-section) ⇒ R_AC/R_DC ≈ 1.0× ~50% reduction in conduction loss (skin-effect penalty removed; small ~10% bundle-fill penalty remains).

Decision rule: r/δ < 1: stay solid. r/δ > 2: use Litz. Between 1 and 2: marginal; weigh cost vs heating budget.

3. 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi trace (microstrip)

50 Ω microstrip on FR-4, 1 oz Cu trace (35 µm thick, 0.46 mm wide). What carries the current?

δ_Cu @ 2.4 GHz = 0.0660 / √2.4e9 = 1.35 µm Only the top 1-2 µm of copper conducts (and the bottom edge for return current). 35 µm Cu thickness is ~26× δ — most of the copper is electrically irrelevant. Surface roughness becomes critical: 2 µm RMS surface roughness adds ~30% more loss (current path zig-zags over rougher metal).

Implication: for HF and RF PCBs, use low-profile copper (RTF, HVLP, VLP) and low-loss laminates (Rogers, Megtron). Pure copper thickness doesn't matter once it exceeds 3-5 δ.

Proximity effect — when Litz alone isn't enough

Adjacent current-carrying conductors create magnetic fields that push current in their neighbors, further crowding it. In a tightly-wound transformer, proximity effect can dominate skin effect.

  • Litz construction — strands twisted with a specific pitch so each strand spends equal time at every position in the bundle's cross-section. Without proper interleaving, the outer strands carry more current.
  • Foil windings — for low-turn, high-current windings (transformer secondaries), use thin copper foil. Foil thickness ≤ 2δ uses all the copper effectively.
  • PCB / planar transformers — stacked spiral traces on multilayer PCBs are essentially foil. Standard for high-frequency, low-profile designs.
  • Dowell's equation — gives R_AC/R_DC for layered windings including proximity. Magnetics design tools (PI Magnetics Designer, ML Engineering) compute it automatically.

Common pitfalls

  • Picking wire too thick — at HF, copper outside δ is dead weight (and may increase loss via proximity effect with neighboring turns).
  • Forgetting harmonics — square-wave switching current has harmonics at 3f, 5f, 7f, … each with smaller δ. A 100 kHz fundamental with a strong 3rd harmonic at 300 kHz sees δ = 120 µm (vs 209 µm at the fundamental).
  • Litz wire termination — must connect all strands to the terminal. Simple solder dip won't catch insulated strands. Use proper tin-fluxed dip, ultrasonic welder, or crimped lugs.
  • Silver-plating depth — plating Cu wire with Ag only helps if the plating exceeds δ at operating frequency. At 1 GHz, ~5 µm Ag needed; thinner is cosmetic.
  • Forgetting ferromagnetic µ_r — δ in iron is √µ_r smaller than in copper at the same f. A 1 mm Fe wire at 1 kHz already has serious skin effect. Hysteresis adds loss on top.
  • PCB plane "thickening" myth — 4 oz copper plane is only marginally lower RAC than 1 oz above 10 MHz. Spend the budget on adding more planes / lower-loss dielectric instead.
  • Surface roughness at GHz — at 5G mmWave, 1 µm RMS surface roughness adds > 50% extra loss. Use RTF / VLP / HVLP copper finishes.
