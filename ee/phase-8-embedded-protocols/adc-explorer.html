SAR ADC algorithm
Aliasing & Nyquist

The successive-approximation register sees an analog input and guesses its code one bit at a time, from MSB to LSB. Each step: set the bit, see whether the DAC's output exceeds V_in; if yes, clear the bit, else keep it. After N steps, the register holds the N-bit code. The trial DAC inside the ADC is what gives "succ-approximation" its name.

Sample a 9 Hz sine at 10 Hz — you don't see a 9 Hz wave. You see a 1 Hz wave going the wrong direction. That ghost is an alias: any frequency f > f_sample/2 folds into the [0, f_sample/2] band at distance |f − round(f/f_sample)·f_sample|. The Nyquist limit is f_sample/2.

What to notice

  • SAR takes N + small cycles per conversion — N cycles to resolve N bits, plus a few for sample-and-hold acquisition. So 12-bit at 1 MHz SAR clock ≈ 14–16 µs total per sample → ~65 ksps sustained throughput. Conversion time scales linearly with resolution; throughput = 1 / (acquisition + conversion).
  • Each step halves the uncertainty — bit 7 splits [0, V_ref] in half, bit 6 splits the chosen half, and so on. Classic binary search.
  • Quantization error is always ≤ 1 LSB — but it never goes away with more samples. To reduce it you need more bits or oversampling + averaging (+1 ENOB per 4× oversample).
  • Nyquist is necessary, not sufficient — the theorem says perfect reconstruction needs f_s > 2·f_max (strict inequality) and ideal sinc reconstruction. Sampling at exactly 2·f_signal can land samples on the zero-crossings of a sine, capturing zero amplitude. Practical rule: f_s ≥ 5×f_max signal, plus an anti-aliasing filter before the sample-and-hold.
  • Aliases are real signal-level energy — software can't tell an alias from a legitimate low-frequency signal. The only fix is hardware filtering before the sample-hold capacitor.

MEMORIZE — topologies

  • SAR — most MCUs, N cycles for N bits, 10–18 bit, MHz rates.
  • ΔΣ (sigma-delta) — audio, 24-bit, slow (kHz).
  • Flash — fastest, low bits, used in scopes.
  • ENOB ≤ N — noise eats real resolution.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • No anti-alias filter — high-f content folds down into baseband; can't be removed in software.
  • High source impedance — > 1–10 kΩ; sample cap can't charge in t_acq.
  • Bad V_ref — accuracy is only as good as the reference; avoid V_cc as ref.
  • Ground noise — share AGND/DGND at one point only.
