DAC Explorer

An R-2R ladder needs only two resistor values to build any N-bit DAC. Toggle each bit and watch the output sum, then compare against the cheaper PWM+RC approach.

R-2R is the workhorse multi-bit DAC topology: every bit drives a 2R resistor that either connects to V_ref or to ground. The ladder math means every bit contributes exactly half the next one's weight at the output, and — critically — the output impedance stays constant at R regardless of code. Compare that to the cheap-and-cheerful PWM+RC, which trades chip area for bandwidth.

Glitch at mid-scale (0111 → 1000)

At the half-scale transition all four bits change simultaneously. If the MSB switches a fraction of a nanosecond after the LSBs clear, the intermediate code briefly drops the output toward zero before the MSB lifts it. That spike is glitch energy; in audio it's an audible click, in signal generation it's a spectral spur.

R-2R vs PWM + RC filter

PWM + RC is a poor person's DAC: you don't need any precision resistors, just one MCU PWM channel and a low-pass filter. The downside: ripple. The output is a clean DC plus a small AC ripple at the PWM frequency, attenuated by the filter rolloff.

What to notice

  • Each bit doubles the previous — for the 4-bit R-2R: bit 0 (LSB) contributes V_ref/16, bit 1 contributes V_ref/8, bit 2 contributes V_ref/4, bit 3 (MSB) contributes V_ref/2. Full-scale (code 1111) = 15/16 · V_ref, NOT V_ref — an N-bit DAC's max output is always (2^N − 1)/2^N of the reference. R-2R is the simplest topology that does this with only two resistor values.
  • Output impedance is constant = R — looking back from the output, you always see R regardless of which bits are on. That's why R-2R drives loads cleanly without a buffer for moderate currents.
  • Mid-scale is the worst glitch — going from 0111 to 1000 means all four bits change. Any timing skew between bit drivers shows up as a transient spike. In audio DACs, glitch energy is published in nV·s.
  • PWM+RC sets a ripple/bandwidth tradeoff — bigger RC → smaller ripple but slower response. Output bandwidth ≪ f_PWM. Cannot synthesize audio without going to 100+ kHz PWM.
  • Resistor matching limits accuracy — R-2R is only as linear as the resistor ratio precision. 1% resistors → ~1 LSB error at 6-7 bits. Monolithic DAC ICs trim resistors on-chip to 0.01%.

MEMORIZE — topologies

  • R-2R — only two R values, R_out constant = R.
  • PWM-DAC — RC filter with f_c ≪ f_PWM.
  • ΣΔ — audio, 16–24-bit, 1-bit stream + decimation.
  • Mid-scale glitch — worst at 0111 → 1000 (all bits flip).

TOPOLOGY DECISION

  • R-2R / monolithic — arbitrary waveforms, fast updates.
  • PWM-DAC — slow DC bias, set-point, contrast adjust.
  • ΣΔ — audio out.
  • Buffer the output — op-amp follower for any real load.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • PWM ripple visible — pick high f_PWM, tighter RC.
  • Output impedance loads downstream — voltage sags under current draw.
  • Resistor tolerance — 1% resistors limit R-2R to ~6–7 effective bits.
  • Glitch energy — needs deglitch S/H for audio-grade output.
