SPI Timing

Four SPI modes set by two bits: clock polarity (CPOL) and clock phase (CPHA). The combination determines whether data is sampled on a rising or falling edge.

SPI is full-duplex, master-clocked, and four-wire (SCK, MOSI, MISO, CS). The only ambiguity is when in the clock cycle the slave latches data. The four modes cover all four answers; every SPI device picks one (sometimes two) and its datasheet says which.

What to notice

  • CPOL = clock idle level — 0 means clock rests LOW, 1 means rests HIGH. Easy to set wrong; you'll get correct-looking but garbled data.
  • CPHA = sample on first or second edge — CPHA=0 samples on first edge after CS asserts, CPHA=1 samples on second edge. The change edge is always the opposite.
  • CS frames the transaction — slave drives MISO and accepts MOSI only while its CS is LOW. CS HIGH should three-state MISO so other slaves can use the bus.
  • Full duplex — every clock cycle both MOSI and MISO transfer one bit. Even "read-only" transactions still shift MOSI; protocols ignore the don't-care bits.
  • Daisy chain is simple but serial — to address slave 5 in a 5-chain, you must shift 40 bits. Independent CS lets you talk to any slave in 8 clocks; pick based on chain length and pin budget.

MEMORIZE

  • CS active LOW — frames each transaction.
  • Full-duplex — MOSI + MISO shift together on every clock.
  • MSB-first by convention (some devices LSB).
  • No ACK — master assumes slave heard.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Multi-device wiring — needs independent CS per slave OR daisy chain.
  • Long traces — capacitance drops max f_clk; keep CS/MISO short.
  • Wrong mode — data looks plausible but every byte shifted by 1 bit.
