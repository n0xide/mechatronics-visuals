Antenna Fundamentals

Radiation patterns, monopole length, polarization mismatch — the three first-principles concepts to design or debug an antenna.

Antennas turn current into propagating EM waves and vice versa. Their behavior is captured by three properties: radiation pattern (where the energy goes vs. angle), resonant length (set by wavelength, so by frequency), and polarization (orientation of the E-field). All three matter — a mismatched length wastes power as reflected current, a wrong pattern dumps energy into the ground, and crossed polarizations can nuke 10 dB or more.

A quarter-wave monopole resonates when its length equals c/(4f). It needs a ground plane (PCB copper, vehicle roof, or counterpoise) to act as the missing half. The calculator + all values below show free-space ideal (the convention used in datasheets and antenna catalogs); real builds use a velocity-factor K ≈ 0.95 (subtract ~5 % for tip-loading + dielectric loading from the wire's insulation). Polarization mismatch loss between two linearly polarized antennas is cos²(θ) in power, where θ is the angle between their E-fields. 0° = no loss, 45° = −3 dB, 90° = theoretically infinite (in practice cross-pol of ~20 dB sets the floor). Mounting a 915 MHz LoRa node sideways when the gateway is vertical can cost ~10 dB silently.

What to notice

  • Isotropic is fiction — no real antenna is perfectly isotropic; it's the dB reference baseline (0 dBi). Anything physical has some directivity.
  • Dipole's donut hides nulls overhead — a vertical dipole has near-zero gain straight up and straight down. Drone-to-base links should not use vertical dipoles end-on.
  • Monopole = half a dipole — same pattern but only above the ground plane, so all the energy goes into a half-space and gain doubles (+3 dB).
  • Higher frequency = shorter antenna — 2.4 GHz λ/4 is 31 mm; 915 MHz is 82 mm; 433 MHz is 173 mm (all free-space ideal — real builds are ~5 % shorter). Why sub-GHz nodes look "huge" compared to WiFi modules.
  • Polarization mismatch is invisible until you measure it — a tilted handheld receiver can drop 6–10 dB without any warning sign. Match orientation, or use circular polarization on one end.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Monopole needs a ground plane — without it the pattern collapses and SWR climbs.
  • Metal nearby detunes — battery, PCB ground, enclosure all shift resonance.
  • 90° polarization mismatch — full loss (cos²90° = 0). Never cross V and H ends.
