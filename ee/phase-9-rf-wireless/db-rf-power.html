Decibels & RF Power — dB, dBm, dBi, dBd

dB is just a logarithmic ratio. Multiplying becomes adding. Three rules to memorize and the rest is arithmetic.

RF lives in dB because the dynamic range is huge — a transmitter pushes +20 dBm (100 mW) and the receiver listens at −137 dBm (femtowatts). Linear numbers would be unreadable. Logarithms compress the scale: every +10 dB means ×10 in power, every +3 dB means ×2. Cascaded gains multiply in linear units but add in dB — which is why link budgets are addition tables. dBm is dB relative to 1 milliwatt (an absolute reference), dBi is antenna gain vs. an isotropic radiator, dBd is vs. a half-wave dipole (dBd = dBi − 2.15).

dBi vs dBd — antenna gain references

dBi = gain vs. an isotropic radiator (the ideal point-source baseline). dBd = gain vs. a half-wave dipole. A dipole has 2.15 dBi gain, so dBd = dBi − 2.15. Marketing tends to quote dBi (bigger number). Always check which one a datasheet uses — a "6 dB rubber duck" could mean 6 dBi (= 3.85 dBd) or 6 dBd (= 8.15 dBi).

What to notice

  • +3 / +10 is enough to estimate by sight — +13 dB = ×20, +23 dB = ×200, −7 dB ≈ ×0.2 (−10 +3). You don't need a calculator for ballpark link budgets.
  • Voltage gets the 20·log — because power scales with V². A doubling of voltage (+6 dB) is a quadrupling of power (also +6 dB). Stay in one column per calculation.
  • dBm is absolute, dB is relative — never add two dBm values together; "20 dBm + 20 dBm" is meaningless. You add dB (gain/loss) to dBm (power).
  • Tiny dBm numbers are tiny powers — −137 dBm is 0.02 femtowatts (2 × 10⁻¹⁷ W). At LoRa's 125 kHz channel this is ~20 dB below the thermal-noise floor; the receiver only recovers it via the 36 dB processing gain of SF12 chirp spreading.
  • dBi ≈ dBd + 2.15 — datasheet says "5 dBd"? That's 7.15 dBi. Always normalize when comparing antennas across vendors.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • 10× vs 20× — power uses 10·log, voltage uses 20·log. Pick one column, stay in it.
  • dBm ≠ dB — dBm is absolute power; dB is a ratio. You add dB to dBm, never two dBm values.
