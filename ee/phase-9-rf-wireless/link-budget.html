RF Link Budget

Will the receiver hear the transmitter? Add up the dBs end-to-end. If margin > 0, the link closes.

A link budget is just dB addition: RX_power = TX_power + TX_gain − FSPL + RX_gain. Margin = RX_power − sensitivity. Positive margin → link works (with some fade headroom); negative → link fails. Free-space path loss (FSPL) is the optimistic floor — real-world adds shadowing (~10–20 dB indoors), body absorption (~10 dB), building penetration (~15–25 dB), and multipath fading. Engineers target ≥ 10 dB margin for stable indoor links.

What to notice

  • Doubling distance costs 6 dB — FSPL goes as 20·log10(d). Going from 1 km to 2 km is the same path-loss cost as +6 dB extra TX power, or +6 dBi extra antenna gain.
  • Doubling frequency also costs 6 dB — at the same distance. 2.4 GHz vs. 915 MHz is roughly 8 dB more loss, which is why LoRa at sub-GHz reaches further than WiFi at the same power.
  • Sensitivity is the receiver's leverage — LoRa SF12 trades data rate for −137 dBm sensitivity vs WiFi's −85 dBm — a 52 dB advantage. Each 20 dB of extra link budget buys 10× the LOS distance (FSPL = 20·log d), so ~50 dB ≈ 300× range, ignoring frequency. Sub-GHz LoRa also beats 2.4 GHz WiFi by another ~8 dB on FSPL at the same distance.
  • FSPL is the optimistic floor — real indoor links add 20–40 dB of shadowing, body absorption, and multipath fading. Aim for ≥10 dB margin so fades don't drop the link.
  • Antenna gain is bidirectional and free — both ends are passive, so +3 dBi on each side gives +6 dB margin (= 2× range, since FSPL doubles per 6 dB). It's the cheapest dB you can buy.

MEMORIZE

  • LoRa SF12 — sensitivity ≈ −137 dBm
  • BLE 1 Mbps — sensitivity ≈ −95 dBm (coded PHY ≈ −103)
  • WiFi 2.4 GHz — sensitivity ≈ −85 dBm at high rates; −90 dBm at lowest MCS
  • FSPL @ 1 km, 915 MHz — ≈ 92 dB

COMMON PITFALLS

  • FSPL is LOS-only — add 10–20 dB shadowing indoors / through walls.
  • Body absorption — a hand on the antenna costs ~10 dB at 2.4 GHz.
  • Forgot the +32.45 — constant baked in when d is km and f is MHz.
