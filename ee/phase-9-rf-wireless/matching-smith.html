Matching Networks & Smith Chart

A simplified Smith chart with an L-network. Slide the components until the impedance trajectory lands at 50 Ω center. Watch VSWR drop.

A radio's output stage wants to see a specific impedance — usually 50 Ω. An antenna's feedpoint is rarely 50 Ω (quarter-wave monopole ≈ 35 Ω, weird PCB antennas can be anywhere). The mismatch reflects power back into the PA, wastes efficiency, and can damage the transmitter. A matching network — two or three reactive components — transforms the load impedance to look like 50 Ω at the desired frequency. The Smith chart is the graphical tool: it maps any complex impedance to a point inside a unit circle, with 50 Ω at the center. Series inductor moves you clockwise along a constant-resistance circle (increases reactance). Shunt capacitor moves you clockwise along a constant-conductance circle (in admittance space). Combine the two and you can reach any point — including the center.

What to notice

  • Series components touch reactance; shunt components touch susceptance — adding a series inductor changes X only. Adding a shunt cap changes B only (admittance world). That's why matching usually needs both.
  • The center is 50 Ω + j0 — every chart point is normalized to Z₀=50. Move the load (R or X sliders) and watch the red dot scoot. The goal is to land that dot on the green center.
  • VSWR=2 covers a generous region — anywhere inside the green dashed circle, you're under 12% reflected power. That's fine for ham radio and IoT modules; precision instrumentation wants VSWR ≤ 1.2.
  • Match is frequency-specific — L and C are reactances jωL and 1/jωC. A match at 915 MHz is partially detuned at 868 MHz; that's why every datasheet specifies "matched at f₀."
  • Real L-networks have two solutions — "lowpass L" (series-L + shunt-C) and "highpass L" (series-C + shunt-L). This visual implements the lowpass form, the common LoRa/BLE choice because it also rejects harmonics.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • VSWR = 1 — perfect match, 0% reflected.
  • VSWR ≤ 2 — < 11% reflected, generally OK.
  • Upper half of Smith chart — inductive (+jX).
  • Lower half — capacitive (−jX).
  • Center — 50 Ω + j0 (matched).
  • Right of center — r > 1 (resistance above Z₀). Left of center — r < 1 (below Z₀).

DECISION

  • L-network — 2 components, narrowband, simplest.
  • π or T-network — 3 components, higher Q control, better bandwidth tuning.
  • λ/4 transformer — when you have PCB real-estate at the target freq.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Lossless match needs resistive load — reactive loads are Q-limited in bandwidth.
  • PCB matching is trace-sensitive — pad parasitics shift the match; tune on the real board.
  • Match is freq-specific — 915 MHz match is detuned at 868 MHz.
