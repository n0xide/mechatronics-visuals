Wireless Spectrum — common bands 100 MHz – 6 GHz

Where the popular protocols live, on a log frequency axis. Click any band for modulation, data rate, range, and antenna size.

Most of the wireless protocols a hobbyist encounters fit between 100 MHz and 6 GHz. The horizontal axis below is log frequency, which makes equal multiplicative jumps look equal-width — perfect for spotting why 915 MHz, 2.4 GHz, and 5 GHz are roughly equidistant on the chart (each is ~2.6× the previous). Higher frequency means shorter wavelength (smaller antenna), more data capacity (wider channels available), but more attenuation by walls and bodies.

What to notice

  • Log axis equalizes ratios — equal pixel distance = equal frequency ratio. Going from 500 MHz to 1 GHz is the same "distance" as 1 GHz to 2 GHz. That's also the same dB cost in path loss.
  • ISM bands are crowded — 2.4 GHz packs WiFi, BLE, Zigbee, BT classic, microwave ovens, baby monitors, and cordless phones into 83.5 MHz. Don't expect a clean channel.
  • Sub-GHz is sparse — 868/915 MHz LoRa has the band nearly to itself. That's part of why range is so good — low noise floor.
  • Higher frequency = smaller antenna, smaller range — 5 GHz λ/4 is 15 mm and convenient on tiny PCBs, but walls eat the signal. 433 MHz λ/4 is 173 mm but penetrates a building easily.
  • GPS L1 (1575.42 MHz) sits right between LoRa and 2.4 GHz on this scale. Its signal is below the noise floor — only correlation against the known PRN code recovers it.

DECISION

  • LoRa — long range, low data-rate (telemetry, fleet).
  • BLE — short range, low power (wearables, sensors).
  • WiFi — high data-rate, close range, mains power.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • 2.4 GHz channels — only 1, 6, 11 are non-overlapping in WiFi.
  • Higher freq = more wall absorption — 5 GHz dies through brick, 433 MHz walks through.
  • ISM ≠ unregulated — duty-cycle and ERP limits still apply per region.
