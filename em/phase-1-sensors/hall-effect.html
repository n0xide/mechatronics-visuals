Hall Effect — Field to Voltage

A current carrying conductor in a magnetic field develops a transverse voltage. That's it. Linear Halls report B directly; switches latch at thresholds; ratiometric current sensors put the conductor onboard and measure its own field.

The Hall effect is the simplest galvanic-to-magnetic interface: drive a small current through a thin semiconductor strip, place it in a magnetic field perpendicular to the current, and Lorentz force separates charges across the strip — producing a small "Hall voltage" proportional to the field strength. The three commercial flavors map onto three jobs. Linear Hall sensors output a voltage proportional to B, with a Vcc/2 zero point for bipolar (ratiometric) sensing. Hall switch integrates a comparator: digital output flips on when |B| exceeds a threshold. Latching Hall needs opposite polarities to flip on and off — the basis of brushless motor commutation, where three latches at 120° track the rotor magnet. Current-sensing Hall ICs (ACS712 family) embed a copper conductor on the die: pass current through it and the chip measures its own self-generated field, giving you a galvanically isolated current sensor.

What to notice

  • Linear Halls are ratiometric to Vcc — zero-field output = Vcc/2, sensitivity quoted in mV/mT (sometimes V/T). If Vcc droops 5%, your zero shifts proportionally. Use a stable reference if precision matters.
  • Bipolar vs unipolar matters — unipolar switches respond only to one pole, bipolar latches need both, omnipolar latches respond to either. Pick the wrong one and your gear-tooth counter doesn't count.
  • Hysteresis prevents chatter — B_op > B_rp typically by 5–10 mT. Without it, a borderline field triggers thousands of ON/OFF transitions.
  • Hall current sensing isolates galvanically — your low-voltage MCU doesn't share ground with the high-current rail. The shunt-based alternative (INA219) is more precise but not isolated.
  • Two Halls at 90° = absolute angle — sine and cosine of θ, atan2 to recover. This is the architecture inside every modern magnetic encoder IC.
  • Magnet selection makes or breaks the design — diametric for rotary, axial for proximity/switch, multi-pole ring for sub-revolution speed sensing. Get it wrong and the curve is unusable.

MEMORIZE

  • Linear Hall zero = Vcc/2 — always ratiometric to supply.
  • Switch B_op > B_rp — hysteresis prevents bounce.
  • 3 latching Halls @ 120° = BLDC commutation, no encoder needed.
  • ACS712 isolates 2.1 kV — safe for line-voltage current sensing.
  • Diametric magnet for rotary, axial for switches.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Wrong magnet pole orientation — unipolar sensor flipped 180° = never triggers.
  • Ratiometric drift with Vcc noise — use a clean LDO if mV matters.
  • Temperature drift — ACS712 ±1.5% over temp; calibrate at install for precision.
  • Nearby ferrous mass — channels and distorts the field. Keep steel ≥ 5× the magnet dimension away.
  • Latching where switch wanted — output stays ON forever until opposite pole, surprising the firmware.
