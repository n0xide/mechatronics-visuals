IMU — Accel + Gyro + Mag

Each sensor measures something fundamentally different and fails differently. Fusion combines them so the failures don't overlap. Tilt the cube, then watch the complementary filter resist gyro drift.

An IMU is a fusion target, not a single answer. Accelerometers measure specific force — gravity plus any linear acceleration the device experiences. They give a tilt reference when stationary, but any motion contaminates that reference. Gyroscopes measure angular rate — they give clean short-term rotation but their integrated angle drifts at the rate of their bias (often degrees per second of unbounded error). Magnetometers measure the local magnetic field; they can give absolute heading but are corrupted by anything ferrous or current-carrying nearby. The complementary filter mixes the gyro's smooth, drift-prone integration with the accel's noisy, drift-free reference: θ = α·(θ + ω·dt) + (1−α)·θ_accel. α near 1 (0.98–0.99) lets the gyro dominate fast motion; the small (1−α) accel term slowly pulls the estimate back to truth.

What to notice

  • Gravity is always pointing somewhere in your accel data — at rest, |a| = 9.81. If you tilt 30°, accel splits into components on two axes. This is the tilt reference.
  • Gyro is honest in the short term, lying in the long term — bias of 0.1 °/s integrates to 6° per minute. Useless after 10 minutes; perfect in the 100 ms window for fast motion.
  • Complementary α is a frequency split — gyro handles high frequencies (fast motion), accel handles low frequencies (slow drift correction). α = 0.98 at 100 Hz ≈ 0.5 s time constant.
  • Magnetometer gives absolute yaw, but only the magnetometer does — accel+gyro fusion cannot distinguish rotations about the gravity axis. Without a magnetometer your robot's heading walks freely.
  • Hard-iron + soft-iron calibration is mandatory — every magnetometer needs you to spin the device in figure-8 patterns to capture an ellipse, then offset and scale to a sphere. Skip this and your heading is wrong by 20°+.
  • Madgwick / Mahony / EKF are upgrades, not replacements — same idea as complementary filter, just more dimensions and adaptive gains. Use them for drones, fast quadrupeds; complementary is fine for slow arms.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Roll from accel
φ = atan2(a_y, a_z)
Pitch from accel
θ = atan2(−a_x, √(a_y²+a_z²))
Gyro integration
θ_k = θ_{k−1} + ω·Δt
Complementary
θ = α·(θ + ω·Δt) + (1−α)·θ_accel
Time constant
τ = α·Δt / (1−α)

MEMORIZE

  • Accel @ rest = g — magnitude 9.81 m/s² distributed across axes.
  • Gyro drifts, accel jitters — fuse complementarily.
  • α = 0.98–0.99 at 100 Hz update rate is the default starting point.
  • Mag = yaw only — and only after calibration.
  • 9-DOF = 3+3+3 — accel, gyro, mag.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Mag near motors — current ≈ magnetic field. Mount far, or disable mag during motor activity.
  • Accel during fast motion — vibration spike → tilt estimate jumps. Trust gyro more (raise α).
  • Forgetting to calibrate mag — see hard-iron/soft-iron offsets. Spin in figure-8.
  • Gyro bias not warmed up — let the IMU sit 5+ seconds after power-up; average the gyro reading and subtract.
  • Wrong axis convention — every chip has its own. Right-hand-rule test on the bench before trusting Madgwick output.
