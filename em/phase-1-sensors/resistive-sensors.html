Resistive Sensors — R(x) into V_out

Four resistive sensors, one circuit: voltage divider. Pick the sensor, pick a series resistor, watch where the curve gets steep — that's your useful range.

Resistive sensors are the cheapest, easiest entry to physical measurement. They change resistance as the world changes — light, temperature, force, strain — and you read that change with a voltage divider into the ADC. The trick is that every resistive sensor is nonlinear, and most are dramatically so: an NTC thermistor can move from 100 kΩ to 100 Ω across its operating range, an LDR from megohms to kilohms, an FSR from "open" to a few hundred ohms. The series resistor in your divider determines where the curve is steep — pick R_series equal to the sensor's resistance at the stimulus you most care about measuring, and you maximize sensitivity there. Pick it wrong and you get a flat, useless signal across your range of interest.

What to notice

  • Pick R_series at your target stimulus — the divider is steepest when R_series equals R_sensor at the value you most care about measuring. Off-target, you lose resolution.
  • All four sensors are nonlinear — LDR and NTC are exponential-ish, FSR is hyperbolic with hysteresis, strain is the only near-linear one (and you still need a bridge to see the change).
  • Strain gauge in a divider is hopeless — ΔR/R ≈ 0.001 at full strain; you'd see microvolts. That's why strain always uses a Wheatstone bridge + instrumentation amp.
  • Self-heating skews thermistors — current through the divider heats the NTC. For ≤0.1 °C accuracy use pulse excitation (sample, then disconnect).
  • FSRs are presence detectors, not scales — ±15–25% repeatability. Use them for "is something there?" not "how heavy is it?".
  • ADC input impedance matters — keep the divider's Thévenin impedance ≪ ADC sample impedance, or sample voltage drifts. Add a unity-gain op-amp buffer if R_series is >100 kΩ.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Divider
V_out = Vcc · R_s / (R_s + R_series)
NTC β
R(T) = R₀ · exp(β·(1/T − 1/T₀))T in Kelvin
Steinhart
1/T = A + B·ln(R) + C·(ln R)³
Strain
ΔR/R = GF · εGF ≈ 2 for foil

MEMORIZE

  • R_series = R_sensor at your target → max sensitivity.
  • NTC drops with heat; PTC rises.
  • FSR is 1 MΩ open, ~kΩ loaded — wide dynamic range, lousy precision.
  • Strain ε ≈ 10⁻³ at full scale → ΔR ≈ 0.2 Ω on 120 Ω gauge.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Wrong R_series — divider flat across your range. Pick at the operating point, not midrange.
  • Self-heating NTC — pulse excitation if ≤0.1 °C matters.
  • High-impedance divider — ADC sample droop. Buffer with op-amp.
  • FSR for weight — wrong sensor. Use a load cell.
