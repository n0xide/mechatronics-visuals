Signal Conditioning — Sensor to ADC

Between the sensor and the ADC sits a chain of stages that decide whether your firmware reads truth or garbage. Each stage adds noise. The art is making the signal big enough that the noise stops mattering before quantization happens.

Every sensor produces a signal at some native amplitude and frequency content. The ADC accepts voltages in a fixed range (e.g., 0–3.3 V) and samples at a fixed rate (e.g., 1 kSPS). The conditioning chain — buffer, amplifier, anti-alias filter, ADC — exists to bridge those two worlds without losing information or adding aliases. Three rules govern the design: (1) match the signal amplitude to the ADC's full-scale to maximize bit usage, (2) low-pass-filter every frequency above Nyquist (f_s/2) before sampling, or risk false low-frequency images, (3) keep total noise below 1 LSB of the ADC. Skip any one and your 12-bit ADC silently degrades to 8 effective bits.

If amp output noise >> 1 LSB, ADC bits past that point are wasted. If signal < 1 LSB, you can't see it at all — boost gain or use a higher-resolution ADC.

What to notice

  • Amplify before the filter — every stage adds noise; gain reduces the impact of downstream noise. Op-amp noise after a ×1000 gain looks 1000× smaller relative to signal than before it.
  • Anti-alias filter is mandatory — any energy above f_s/2 folds into the baseband as a false low-frequency signal. The LPF is the only way to stop it; you cannot fix it in software.
  • Cutoff vs sample rate — set f_c ≤ f_s/2.5 to give the LPF some roll-off room (single-pole RC only drops 20 dB/decade). For a sharp cut, use 2nd or 4th order active filters.
  • 50 Hz mains coupling is your enemy — long sensor wires act as antennas; mains hum can dwarf a 10 mV strain-gauge signal. Twisted-pair, shielded cable, or differential measurement (INA) cancels it.
  • SNR caps your effective bits — a 16-bit ADC behind a noisy 8-bit-effective amp chain is an expensive 8-bit ADC. Spec for ENOB end-to-end, not catalog bits.
  • Match impedance to ADC sample cap — 12-bit SAR ADCs need source impedance < ~10 kΩ to settle in 1 µs. Higher than that → buffer with an op-amp follower (rail-to-rail input, low offset).
  • Quantization noise = V_FS / (2^N · √12) — for 3.3 V, 12-bit: ~232 µV rms. If your signal is 200 µV, you're below the noise floor without gain.

MEMORIZE

  • Amplify first, filter second, sample last.
  • f_s > 2·f_max or you alias. No exceptions.
  • 1 LSB @ 12-bit 3.3 V = 806 µV.
  • ENOB ≈ catalog − 2 bits for most real chains.
  • Twisted pair + shield kills 50/60 Hz pickup.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • No anti-alias filter — aliasing is invisible in firmware. The harmonic at 980 Hz becomes 20 Hz garbage at f_s=1 kHz.
  • Gain too high → amp saturates → signal flat-tops → 3rd harmonic distortion. Pick G so peak signal = ~80% of ADC FS.
  • High source impedance → ADC sample cap doesn't settle → wrong reading. Buffer with op-amp.
  • Op-amp without rail-to-rail → can't reach 0 or Vcc → clipped at 0.2 V from each rail.
  • Ground loops → 60 Hz hum that no filter helps. Single-point ground, separate analog/digital planes.

BUILD ORDER

  • 1. What's the signal: amplitude, bandwidth, source Z?
  • 2. Pick G so signal_pk · G ≈ 0.4·V_FS (some headroom).
  • 3. Pick f_c just above signal bandwidth.
  • 4. Pick f_s > 2.5·f_c for filter roll-off margin.
  • 5. Check: amp noise BW < 1 LSB? If not, lower G or use higher-resolution ADC.
