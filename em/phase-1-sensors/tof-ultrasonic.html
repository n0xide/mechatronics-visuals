ToF + Ultrasonic — Pulse-Echo Timing

Send a wavefront, time the bounce back, divide by two. Light and sound use the same equation — only the propagation speed differs by 875,000×.

Ranging by time-of-flight is the most physically honest distance measurement you can do. Emit a brief pulse at time t=0, wait for the echo from a surface, and the round-trip time multiplied by the propagation speed and divided by two gives the one-way range. With ultrasound the speed is roughly 343 m/s at room temperature, so 1 m round-trip takes 5.8 ms — easy for any MCU's input-capture peripheral. With light (940 nm laser in a VL53L1X) the speed is 3·10⁸ m/s, so 1 m round-trip is 6.7 ns — far beyond any general-purpose timer, requiring a dedicated SPAD array and on-chip TDC (time-to-digital converter) running gigahertz-fast. The trade-offs split predictably: ultrasonic is cheap, works on transparent surfaces, has a wide beam (10–30° cone) that catches obstacles a narrow laser would miss, but is slow and fooled by soft / angled / hot surfaces. Optical ToF is fast, narrow, immune to wind and temperature, but blinded by sunlight, lost on black or glass, and crosstalks with neighbors.

What to notice

  • Divide by two — measured time is round-trip; range is half. Forgetting the ÷2 is the most common code bug.
  • Resolution scales with timer tick — 1 µs MCU timer + 343 m/s sound = 0.17 mm theoretical (in practice you're limited by echo rise-time and threshold, more like 3 mm).
  • Optical ToF needs gigahertz time resolution — that's why you can't build one from a microcontroller. VL53 chips contain a dedicated TDC + SPAD array.
  • Crosstalk between ToF sensors is the gotcha — three VL53L1X on a quadcopter pinging each other will give random readings. Sequence via XSHUT pins or rotate ranging windows.
  • Ultrasonic max rate = range-limited — you can't ping again until the echo arrives. At 4 m max range that's ~24 ms/ping, so ~40 Hz ceiling.
  • Beam width hides obstacles — narrow optical beam misses a thin pole 5° off center; wide ultrasonic cone may pick it up. Use both for redundancy on mobile robots.
  • VL53 sees its own cover glass — bonded plastic/glass in front of the sensor reflects ~4% of the laser back. Apply window-coverglass calibration or use the L1X "cover glass" mode.

MEMORIZE

  • t_µs / 58 = cm — HC-SR04 round-trip shortcut at room temp.
  • ÷2 ALWAYS — round-trip → one-way.
  • Sound = 343 m/s, light = 3·10⁸ m/s. Ratio ≈ 10⁶.
  • VL53 over I²C, HC-SR04 over GPIO pulse timing.
  • XSHUT sequences multiple VL53 sensors.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Forgot ÷2 — doubles the reported distance. Bench-check at known range.
  • Ultrasonic in airflow / temp gradient — bends the path. Don't trust outdoor or near-engine readings.
  • VL53 in bright sun — ambient IR saturates SPADs. Use shrouds or switch to a longer-wavelength chip.
  • Black target → no ToF echo — laser absorbed. Falls back to "max distance" reading.
  • Soft target → weak ultrasonic echo — couch cushions are invisible. Use IR or ToF instead.
