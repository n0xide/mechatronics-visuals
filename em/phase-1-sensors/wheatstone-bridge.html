Wheatstone Bridge — Microvolt Detector

Four resistors in a diamond. When all four match, the output is zero. Nudge one a little — the bridge multiplies that nudge into a measurable signal.

A strain gauge's resistance changes by about 0.2% at full strain — a fraction so tiny no ADC could resolve it directly. The Wheatstone bridge is the trick that made strain measurement practical in the 1840s: arrange two voltage dividers in parallel, take the difference of their midpoints, and the common-mode (everything that doesn't change) cancels out. Now a 0.2% resistance change shows up as a millivolt-scale differential signal that an instrumentation amplifier can boost to volts. Adding gauges in opposing arms doubles or quadruples sensitivity and cancels temperature drift. Every load cell in your kitchen scale, every torque sensor in industry, every pressure transducer — it's this circuit underneath.

What to notice

  • Quarter bridge output is 1/4 of full bridge — adding more active gauges multiplies sensitivity directly: ×1, ×2, ×4 for quarter/half/full. Same ε, same GF, same V_ex.
  • Half bridge cancels temperature — both gauges drift together with temperature; if they're in opposite arms (R1↑ and R3↑), the imbalance cancels. Use this in outdoor / engine-bay environments.
  • mV/V is the load-cell spec — datasheets give bridge output normalized by excitation (typically 2 mV/V at rated load). Multiply by your V_ex to get raw output.
  • Raw signal is millivolts, ADC needs volts — gain of 100–1000× is mandatory. INA125 / AD620 / INA826 are the workhorses; HX711 integrates this + 24-bit ADC into a single 50¢ chip for load cells.
  • Bridge is small-signal linear — formulas assume ΔR/R ≪ 1. For huge resistance changes (LDR, FSR) the bridge breaks; use a divider instead.
  • Common-mode is at V_ex/2 — your INA needs a common-mode input range that covers this. 5V excitation puts both midpoints at ~2.5V; cheap rail-to-rail INAs handle it.

MEMORIZE

  • 1, 2, 4× — quarter, half, full bridge sensitivity.
  • Opposite arms add — same-sign strain in R1 + R3 or R2 + R4 adds; same-arm cancels.
  • 2 mV/V @ rated — typical load-cell output at full scale.
  • HX711 — 50¢ part = bridge amp + 24-bit ADC; default for hobby load cells.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Quarter bridge + temp swing — uncancelled drift dwarfs the strain signal. Use dummy gauge in adjacent arm.
  • INA without rail-to-rail input — common-mode at V_ex/2 may clip. Check input range.
  • Long lead wires — wire resistance enters the divider. Use 3-wire or 6-wire (Kelvin) connections beyond ~1 m.
  • Excitation voltage noise — bridge ratios it directly into output. Use a clean, low-noise reference.

WIRING (INA125 + load cell)

  • E+/E− → V_ex (often 5 V) / GND.
  • S+/S− → INA differential inputs.
  • R_G sets gain: G = 4 + 60kΩ/R_G.
  • REF → mid-rail for bipolar output, GND for unipolar.
  • V_out → ADC. Add 1 kΩ + 10 nF RC for anti-alias.
