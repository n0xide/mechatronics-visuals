Actuator Sizing — Power, Gearing, Thermal Duty

Given a required output torque and speed, find the motor + gearbox combo that lands the operating point near peak efficiency and inside the thermal envelope.

Sizing has three stages. (1) Power requirement: at the load, P = T·ω. You can't deliver more mechanical power than the motor's continuous rating allows, regardless of gearing. (2) Gear-ratio selection: pick N to land the motor's operating point at high efficiency — usually 60–80% of no-load speed for a DC motor. The motor sees torque T_load/N and angular velocity ω_load·N (and reflected inertia J_load/N²). (3) Thermal duty: continuous current is limited by I²R heating. Intermittent operation lets you exceed the continuous limit briefly, with RMS current as the gate: I_rms = I_peak · √(duty), and motor heating is governed by I_rms².

What to notice

  • Gear ratio is the most powerful sizing tool. Doubling N halves the torque the motor must produce and doubles its speed — moving the operating point along the curve without changing motor. Most "torque too low" problems are gear-ratio problems.
  • Sweet spot is 60–80% of no-load speed for typical DC motors, where efficiency peaks. Far below that, you're current-limited (overheating). Far above, you're voltage-limited (can't deliver torque). Gear so your continuous operating point sits in this band.
  • Continuous vs intermittent matters more than peak torque. A motor with 80% duty cycle and 1.2× rated current effectively sees I_rms = 1.2·√0.8 = 1.07× rated — fine. The same motor at 100% duty and 1.2× current is overheating.
  • Reflected inertia drops by N². A 30:1 gearbox makes a 1 kg·m² load look like 1.1 g·m² to the motor — huge for dynamics. This is why robot arms with large reductions accelerate so quickly relative to motor torque.
  • Gearbox efficiency is not free. Spur/planetary: 85–95%. Worm: 40–80%. Cycloidal: 75–90%. Harmonic drive: 70–85% but zero backlash. Account for the drop in your P_motor calculation.
  • 30% torque margin minimum. Real loads have peaks, friction varies, batteries sag. If your calculated motor torque equals exactly the load, you're under-sized. Plan for 1.5×.

SIZING WORKFLOW

  • 1. P_required — from load T·ω; add 30% margin.
  • 2. Pick motor — cont. rating ≥ P/η_gear.
  • 3. Compute N — N = ω_motor_target / ω_load.
  • 4. Check T_motor — must be < cont. torque at chosen speed.
  • 5. Verify thermal — I_rms ≤ I_rated.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Sizing for peak torque — your motor melts in steady state.
  • Forgetting gearbox losses — a 30:1 worm gives 0.5× the torque you expected.
  • Ignoring acceleration torque — for fast motion, T_accel = J·α can dwarf static load torque.
  • Skipping duty-cycle math — 50% duty doesn't mean 2× current is safe; it means I_rms gates.
