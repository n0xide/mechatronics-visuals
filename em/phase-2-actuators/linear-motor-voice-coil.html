Voice Coil & Linear Motor — F = B·I·L

A wire in a magnetic field. Push current through it; it experiences a force perpendicular to both. The voice coil is the simplest, most elegant linear actuator: no commutation, no gears, no friction, no hysteresis. Speakers, hard-drive heads, autofocus lenses, hexapod nano-positioners — all the same equation.

The Lorentz force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field is F = I·L × B; when the wire is perpendicular to the field (the usual case), the magnitude reduces to F = B·I·L, where B is the flux density in tesla, I is the current in amps, and L is the total length of wire in the field (in meters). A voice-coil actuator wraps a coil of length L_eff in the gap of a permanent magnet, so when you push current through the coil, the whole coil (and whatever's bonded to it: the cone of a speaker, the carriage of a hard-drive head, the lens of a phone camera) moves linearly. The force is proportional to current, instantly, with no detent, no cogging, and no friction beyond the spider/spring guidance.

The Lorentz force on a current loop in a radial gap

A typical voice coil sits in an annular gap between a center magnet pole and an outer return iron. The flux is radial in the gap — pointing outward from the center pole. The coil is wound circumferentially, so the current flows tangentially everywhere in the coil. Cross-product I × B is therefore axial — and uniformly so for every loop in the coil. Net force = B · I · L_eff, where L_eff is the total length of wire in the gap (N turns × 2π · r_coil for a circular coil).

On every turn of wire, the radial B and the tangential I produce an axial F. All turns push in the same direction, so the force adds up. Reverse the current and the force reverses — voice coils are inherently bidirectional, no switching circuitry needed.

The electromechanical model (V → I → F → v → x)

A voice coil acts as a simple second-order LR + mechanical mass-spring-damper system. The electrical side: V_applied drives current through coil resistance R and inductance L, fighting a back-EMF e = K_e · v that depends on velocity. The mechanical side: force F = K_f · I accelerates mass m against damping b and spring stiffness k. Crucially, in SI units, K_e (V·s/m) = K_f (N/A) numerically — both are equal to B·L_eff. This is the energy-conservation argument that the same constant ties the electrical and mechanical sides together.

Electrical: V = R·I + L·dI/dt + K_e·v (v = velocity in m/s) Mechanical: F = K_f·I = m·a + b·v + k·x (a = dv/dt) Energy: P_elec = V·I, P_mech = F·v, P_heat = R·I² Conservation forces K_e = K_f in SI (called "motor constant")

Why each one is a voice coil and not a motor + leadscrew

  • Speaker — needs ±5 mm stroke at 100 Hz to 5 kHz, no hysteresis, zero friction. A motor-leadscrew has tens of ms of dead band; a voice coil has microseconds. No competition.
  • HDD head — needs to settle on a track in < 1 ms with sub-µm precision. The voice coil is force-controlled by a high-bandwidth current loop; the head's mass is small, so a few hundred mA of current snaps it to position.
  • Phone autofocus — needs to move a tiny lens ±150 µm in < 20 ms with no audible noise. Voice coil + position feedback (Hall sensor) does this in 4 mm³ of motor volume.
  • Nano-positioner / wafer stage — semiconductor lithography stages run on voice coils because there's no contact friction (no stiction), so position can be servo'd to nm precision with feedback from a laser interferometer.

The common thread: direct force control, no gear backlash, no static friction beyond the suspension. The voice coil's downside is also its defining limit: it generates force per ampere but not per volt — at high speed, back-EMF eats into the headroom, capping the achievable velocity. It's a short-stroke precision actuator, not a long-throw workhorse.

How to size a voice coil for your application

You start with a force and stroke target. The force determines K_f (and through that B · L_eff). The continuous force is thermally limited — too much current, the coil cooks. The peak force is what current the driver can deliver. Stroke determines how big the magnet gap and coil bobbin need to be.

1. Given F_peak, choose B (typical 0.5–1.2 T for NdFeB) and pick L_eff: L_eff = F_peak / (B · I_peak) 2. Coil winding constraint: with fill factor κ ≈ 0.4–0.6, L_eff in volume V_coil → I_max² · R = P_thermal_max 3. Continuous current limited by R·I² heating: I_cont = √(P_max / R) → F_cont = K_f · I_cont 4. Bandwidth from electrical τ = L/R and mechanical √(k/m): pick whichever is slower → trade: more turns = more L_eff = more F per A, but more R = more heat, and more L = slower current rise time

Position feedback

Sub-µm requires position feedback, not open-loop. Common solutions: a Hall-effect sensor with a position magnet (5–10 µm resolution), a glass linear encoder (0.1 µm), or an LVDT (0.5 µm, magnetic immunity). For 0.1 µm, use the encoder.

What to notice

  • K_e = K_f (numerically in SI) — the motor constant ties electrical and mechanical sides. Measure either; the other follows.
  • Force per amp is constant — that's why voice-coil current loops are so well-behaved. Set I, get F, end of story (until you saturate the magnet).
  • Continuous force ≪ peak force — peak force is limited by driver current; continuous force is limited by I²R heating. Typical ratio 3–10×.
  • v_max = V_supply / K_e — back-EMF limits top speed. At v_max, the entire supply voltage is canceling back-EMF and no current flows — zero force. To go faster you need more voltage or a smaller K_e (fewer turns).
  • The coil is the moving mass — every gram of copper has to accelerate. For high-bandwidth voice coils, use aluminum wire or hollow-core composite formers to cut moving mass.
  • Spring suspensions add resonance — speaker spider+surround has k ≈ 1500 N/m, the coil + cone has m ≈ 5 g, giving resonance at ~ 85 Hz. Below this, the spring dominates; above, the mass. The bass response of a speaker is shaped around this resonance.

SHORTCUTS

  • Force is linear in current, instantly. No saturation until the magnet's iron path does.
  • Reverse current → reverse force, no commutation logic.
  • Continuous current ≈ √(P_thermal / R); peak current = driver limit.
  • Bandwidth ≈ min(mechanical, electrical) — speaker mech is the limit; HDD electrical is.
  • Suspension stiffness adds resonance at √(k/m); pick stiff = fast & high-bandwidth, soft = quiet & long-stroke.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Overheating the coil — once enamel insulation cooks (~150 °C), turns short and the coil dies. Always thermal-spec the continuous current.
  • Coil not centered in gap — partial overlap reduces L_eff, reducing K_f, and produces axial side-loads. Use a spider/surround that constrains the coil radially.
  • Forgetting back-EMF — control loops that ignore K_e·v give you no warning before they slam into v_max.
  • Misorienting the magnet — must be radial in the gap, not axial. Reversing N/S flips the force direction (sometimes useful, sometimes a bug).
  • No driver protection for stall — at zero velocity, full V_supply / R can flow forever and burn the coil. Add current limit in software.
