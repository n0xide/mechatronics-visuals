Motor & Actuator Selector — Decision Tree

Pick the wrong actuator and you'll spend the project compensating. The choice is almost always made from four questions: position needed? continuous? high speed? on/off?

Most motion problems break down into a short series of branching questions. Answer them in order and you arrive at a single best-fit family of actuator. The right choice is rarely the most powerful or most precise — it's the cheapest one that meets the actual requirement, plus 30% margin. The four pivotal questions are (1) is precise positioning needed? (2) is rotation continuous or back-and-forth? (3) is speed high or low? (4) is the motion simply on/off? What to notice

  • Steppers default for low-cost positioning — 200 steps × 16 microsteps = 3200 positions/rev, open loop, $15 + $5 driver. Hit them with a closed-loop driver and they become serious servos.
  • BLDC + FOC has eaten brushed DC in everything except disposable toys and cordless tools. Higher efficiency, longer life, smoother torque. The cost difference is now driver IC pennies.
  • "Hobby servo" is a category for cheap PWM toys. If you need any precision under load, jump to bus servos (Dynamixel, LX-224) — same form factor, real feedback, RS-485 daisy-chain.
  • Solenoids are for binary motion only. If you want any positioning, use a motor + lead screw — never try to PWM-modulate a solenoid's position (you can, but it's a thermal nightmare).
  • Match motor sweet spot via gearing. Almost any motor can deliver the right output torque/speed combination with the right gear ratio. "I need more torque" usually means "I need a higher reduction ratio."
  • Duty cycle and thermal envelope matter more than peak rating. A motor rated 50 W "continuous" can do 200 W for seconds; choose your spec for the load profile, not the maximum.

Cheat sheet

QUICK RULES

  • Open-loop position → stepper.
  • Position + load → closed-loop (stepper, BLDC+enc, or bus servo).
  • Just spin → BLDC + ESC.
  • On/off mechanical → solenoid or pneumatic.
  • Nanometer / sub-micron → piezo or voice coil.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Buying for peak, not continuous — your "5 N·m" stepper holds 5 N·m only at zero RPM, full current, full heatsink.
  • Forgetting gear ratio — three orders of magnitude cheaper than buying a bigger motor.
  • Hobby servo for any joint that holds against gravity — it'll droop and overheat.
  • BLDC without a sensor — sensorless ESCs need minimum speed; won't hold position.
