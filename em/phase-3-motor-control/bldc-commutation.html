BLDC Commutation — Six-Step vs Sinusoidal

BLDC = brushless DC = a 3-phase synchronous motor that you commutate electronically. Same magnet+coils as PMSM; difference is how cleverly you drive the phase currents.

A BLDC has three stator phases (A, B, C) wound 120° apart and a permanent-magnet rotor. The driver must energize the right phases at the right time to produce continuous rotation — this is "commutation." Three flavors, in order of sophistication: trapezoidal six-step (turn on two phases, leave one floating; rotate which pair every 60° electrical); sinusoidal (continuously drive smooth sine waves 120° apart); FOC (transform to rotor frame, regulate the torque-producing current directly). Six-step is the hobby-ESC default — simple, works sensorless via back-EMF zero-cross. Sinusoidal is quieter and smoother but needs continuous rotor angle. FOC is sinusoidal commutation done in a smart reference frame; it's how serious robotics drives torque.

Six-step commutation — Hall-sensored

Six discrete states per electrical revolution (360° elec). In each state, one phase is driven HIGH (to V+), one driven LOW (to GND), and the third FLOATS. Hall sensors (or back-EMF zero-cross) tell the driver when to advance to the next state.

Sinusoidal commutation

Drive each phase with a smooth sine, 120° apart. Continuous rotor angle required (encoder, not just halls). Torque is constant — no 6× per-revolution ripple of trapezoidal.

V_A = sin(θ) V_B = sin(θ − 120°) V_C = sin(θ + 120°)

SVPWM (space-vector) adds a 3rd-harmonic injection so the peak line-to-line voltage reaches V_dc — about 1.15× the SPWM limit of 0.866·V_dc (the SVPWM number is relative, not an absolute multiple of the bus). Free torque headroom — almost every modern FOC implements SVPWM internally.

From sinusoidal to FOC (intuition)

Sinusoidal drive sets phase voltages — but you really want to set torque. Torque is produced by current that's perpendicular to the rotor flux. FOC rotates the reference frame to follow the rotor; then "torque-current" becomes a single scalar (i_q) you can PID directly. See foc-overview.html for the full picture.

In the stator frame, the current vector spins. In the rotor frame, it stands still: i_d (flux, regulate to 0) and i_q (torque, regulate to setpoint). Two simple PIs on DC quantities replace three mutually-coupled AC PIs.

What to notice

  • Six-step has 6× per-revolution torque ripple — at each commutation, current jumps to a new phase pair. Rotor sees a ~14% torque dip between steps. Audible "growl" at low RPM. Fine for drone motors at 30 000 rpm; awful for a robot wrist at 10 rpm.
  • Six-step needs rotor position at coarse 60° resolution — three Hall sensors give 6 discrete states per revolution, exactly enough. Sensorless: detect back-EMF zero crossings on the floating phase, time the next commutation 30° later.
  • Sensorless six-step can't start from rest — no rotation → no back-EMF → no zero-cross. ESCs use open-loop "ramp up" (apply rotating field blindly, hope rotor catches), then switch to closed-loop sensorless once spinning.
  • Sinusoidal needs continuous rotor angle — encoder (magnetic AS5048A, optical incremental, resolver) or a high-quality back-EMF observer. Halls alone give 60° resolution — too coarse for smooth sine.
  • FOC = sinusoidal + smart frame — the math (Clarke + Park) is just a rotating-frame coordinate change. Once in the d-q frame, the AC problem becomes a DC problem; PI controllers on DC quantities are easy to tune.
  • SVPWM gives free voltage headroom — 3rd-harmonic injection raises peak line-to-line from 86% to 100% of V_dc. ~15% more usable torque from the same battery, no extra hardware.

MEMORIZE

  • 3 halls = 6 states — Hall pattern → next drive state via LUT.
  • Floating phase shows back-EMF — zero-cross marks 30° before commutation.
  • Sine = 120° apart — V_A, V_B = V_A(θ−120°), V_C = V_A(θ+120°).
  • Pole pairs ≠ stator slots — θ_elec = P · θ_mech, where P = pole pairs (= poles/2). Off-by-P bug → motor spins wrong way or not at all.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Wrong commutation order — motor stutters or runs backward. Swap any two phase wires to reverse, or fix the LUT.
  • Sensorless at low RPM — back-EMF too small to detect; below ~500 rpm switch to open-loop or wait.
  • Six-step at robotic speeds — 14% torque ripple = bad arm motion. Use sinusoidal/FOC.
  • Hall installation off — even a few mechanical degrees → wrong commutation timing → loss of torque + heat.
