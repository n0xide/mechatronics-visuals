Current Sensing — Shunt & Hall

FOC needs phase currents sampled mid-PWM-pulse. Pick the right shunt location, gain it through an INA, sample the ADC at the right instant.

Current is the torque-producing signal in every closed-loop motor drive — FOC, stepper chopper control, overcurrent protection. Two physical methods: drop voltage across a tiny resistor (a "shunt") and amplify, or measure the magnetic field with a Hall-effect IC. Shunts are cheap, accurate, and noisy (PWM commutation noise couples in); Hall sensors are isolated and quiet but more expensive and slower. The shunt-location decision (low-side / high-side / inline) controls cost, accuracy, and how cleverly you have to time the ADC sample. For FOC on a 3-phase inverter, the modern default is inline (one shunt per phase, after the half-bridge, before the motor) with a SAR ADC sampled at the PWM midpoint.

What to notice

  • Low-side shunt is cheap and easy — referenced to GND, standard op-amp works. But you only see current when the low-side FET is conducting, so the ADC must sample during the freewheel interval (the "OFF" of the high-side PWM). Misses overcurrent during the ON time.
  • High-side shunt measures current into the motor leg always — but its common-mode voltage rides on V_supply (12–48 V). Needs a high-side current-sense amp (INA240, MAX4080) with high CMRR. More expensive but always-on.
  • Inline shunt is the FOC default — sits between the half-bridge and the motor phase, so it sees the actual phase current at any duty. CM voltage swings 0↔V_supply at PWM rate — requires fast wideband CSA (INA240 again, ~400 kHz BW). Three shunts for a 3-phase FOC inverter.
  • Sample at the PWM midpoint — the dead-time, FET turn-on/off, and reverse-recovery ringing all happen near the edges. Center-aligned PWM with ADC trigger at TIM_OC4_REF puts the sample in the quiet middle. Blanking window typically 1–3 µs.
  • Hall current sensors give galvanic isolation — no electrical path between motor and MCU. Great for safety / fault tolerance. But slower (BW 100–200 kHz), more drift, and you pay ~$3 per channel.
  • Shunt power = I²·R — at 30 A and 10 mΩ that's 9 W of heat. Use a 4-terminal Kelvin shunt and a copper pour to spread it; precision drops if the shunt itself heats up (TCR matters).

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Not Kelvin-sensed — sense leads on the high-current pad pick up IR drop in copper; use 4-terminal.
  • Sampling near edges — ringing & reverse-recovery glitches; sample mid-pulse.
  • CSA saturation — at peak current G·V_shunt > V_ref → clip; check with worst-case I.
  • Forgotten offset — bipolar current needs V_bias = V_ref/2 reference; calibrate at zero current.
  • TCR drift — 50 ppm/°C shunt at 80 °C rise = 0.4% error.
