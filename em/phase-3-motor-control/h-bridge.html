H-Bridge — Four-Quadrant DC Drive

Four switches in an H shape let you reverse current through a motor without reversing the supply. Click switches to explore states — shoot-through warning fires when both switches in one leg conduct.

An H-bridge is the universal DC-motor primitive. Two legs, two switches each: top (Q1/Q3) and bottom (Q2/Q4). The motor sits between the two switch nodes. Close Q1+Q4 → current flows left-to-right (forward). Close Q3+Q2 → right-to-left (reverse). Close both bottoms (Q2+Q4) → motor terminals shorted to GND = brake. Close both switches in the same leg (Q1+Q2 or Q3+Q4) → V_supply shorted to GND through the FETs = shoot-through. Peak current = V_supply / (2·R_ds(on)) — milliohms of R_ds(on) at 12 V means hundreds of amps for the few microseconds before the FET fails. Every gate-driver IC enforces dead-time (typ 200 ns–1 µs, must be > t_off,max − t_on,min of the FET to guarantee one device is off before the other turns on) when transitioning between states.

SHOOT-THROUGH: Q1+Q2 (or Q3+Q4) both ON shorts V_supply to GND. Current is limited only by FET R_ds(on). Catastrophic in microseconds. Add dead-time.

What to notice

  • Forward and reverse are diagonal pairs — Q1+Q4 (forward) and Q3+Q2 (reverse). Same supply polarity, opposite motor current. That's the whole point of the H.
  • Brake shorts the motor terminals together — both lows ON (Q2+Q4) makes the motor a generator dumping its back-EMF into its own windings. Heavy regenerative drag. All-OFF = coast (motor freewheels).
  • Shoot-through kills FETs — both Q1+Q2 ON puts V+ directly across the FET stack. R_ds(on) is milliohms; current is limited only by FET silicon and parasitic inductance. Dead-time inserted at every direction change prevents this — typ 200 ns–1 µs.
  • Sign-magnitude PWM — one leg holds fixed (say Q4 ON), the other PWMs (Q1 chopping, Q2 complementary). Motor voltage swings 0 → V_supply. Lower ripple, lower switching loss, but firmware tracks direction separately from magnitude.
  • Locked anti-phase PWM — diagonals switch together (Q1+Q4 vs Q3+Q2). Motor voltage swings −V → +V every cycle. Duty 50% = zero average → unified sign+magnitude in one variable, but 2× the ripple and ~4× the loss.
  • Synchronous rectification — when motor current freewheels through a body diode (~0.7 V drop), turn that diode's FET ON for the freewheel interval. Drop becomes I·R_ds(on) ≈ 50 mV. Big efficiency win at high current.

MEMORIZE

  • Diagonals — Q1+Q4 = fwd, Q3+Q2 = rev.
  • Same-leg = death — Q1+Q2 or Q3+Q4 ON = short.
  • Dead-time — 200 ns–1 µs between same-leg transitions.
  • Brake — both lows (or both highs) ON, terminals shorted.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • No dead-time — gate driver IC fixes this; discrete designs must.
  • Bootstrap brown-out — high-side gate cap discharges if PWM stays high too long; force a low pulse occasionally.
  • Body-diode reverse recovery — during dead-time the body diode conducts; when the opposing FET turns on it must remove the diode's stored charge, causing a high-di/dt EMI spike. Mitigate with short dead-time, schottky shunts, or FETs with fast body diodes.
  • Locked anti-phase at 50% duty — looks idle but motor sees full V_supply switching at f_PWM; coils heat with no torque.
