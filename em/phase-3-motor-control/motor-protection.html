Motor Protection — OCP, OTP, UVLO, Stall, Inrush

Smoke is expensive. Build five protection loops into every motor drive: overcurrent, overtemperature, undervoltage, stall, and inrush. Most modern driver ICs do four of them for you — your firmware does the fifth.

A modern motor-driver IC (DRV8316, TMC2209, BTN8982) handles overcurrent (OCP), overtemperature (OTW/OTSD), undervoltage lockout (UVLO), and gate-supply faults internally — when one trips, the driver pulls a nFAULT pin low and you read a status register over SPI/UART. Your firmware adds the two protections the IC can't see: stall detection (mechanically jammed motor — current high, position not moving, back-EMF low) and inrush limiting (huge startup current as the empty bus caps charge through your bridge). Pick a scenario below to watch each trip — and a chip's response (latch off, retry, fold back). For an arm joint at 5 A peak you set OCP at 1.5–2× peak with a 2 µs blanking window; OTSD at 150 °C junction; UVLO at 8.5 V on a 12 V bus; stall after 100 ms of zero motion at > 80% torque command; inrush via slow-start cap or precharge resistor.

Stall detection — back-EMF vs commanded torque

Firmware sees motor not moving (encoder Δ≈0) but current is high. Cross-check with back-EMF: if i·R explains the whole voltage and there's no v_BEMF, rotor is stuck. Trip after ~100 ms.

What to notice

  • Overcurrent trips in microseconds — modern drivers fold back the FET gate within 1–2 µs of sensing > OCP via the shunt amp. Most use cycle-by-cycle limiting (fold-back at PWM-cycle boundary) before they go to latch-off. A short circuit through the H-bridge → die without OCP, because R_ds(on) is milliohms.
  • Overtemperature has two thresholds — OTW (warning, ~130 °C) raises a flag but keeps running so firmware can derate. OTSD (shutdown, ~150 °C) kills the gate drive. T_J is read from a built-in diode; the IC reports °C via SPI.
  • UVLO protects the gate driver, not the motor — when V_supply sags, gate drive amplitude drops; FET R_ds(on) climbs nonlinearly with low V_gs → FET heats → cascade fail. UVLO disables outputs below 8 V on a 12 V part; re-enables with hysteresis (typ 1 V).
  • Stall detection is firmware — IC can't tell "motor stopped because of jam" from "motor stopped because commanded zero." You need an encoder + a commanded-torque comparison. Heuristic: if |i_q| > 0.8·i_q_max for > 100 ms AND |ω| < 0.05·ω_max, raise stall. TMC StallGuard does this in silicon for stepper BLDC.
  • Inrush is the brutal first 10 ms — cold bus caps look like a short to the supply. A 100 µF cap charging from 0→12 V at 1 µs ramp draws hundreds of amps for nanoseconds. Use a precharge resistor + relay (industrial), an NTC inrush limiter (consumer), or a soft-start cap on the driver enable (3D printer).
  • Auto-retry vs latch-off — DRV8316 default: latch-off → clear via nSLEEP toggle. Trinamic default: cycle-by-cycle limiting with retry. Pick latch-off for safety-critical, retry for hobby ESC where shaft might just be stuck for a moment.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • nFAULT never polled — driver tripped, firmware blindly retries setpoints. Always check nFAULT after every command.
  • OCP set too low — normal startup transient trips it on every power-on. Set after measuring actual peak with scope.
  • UVLO too tight — battery-droop during accel falsely trips. Pick hysteresis ≥ 1 V.
  • No inrush limiter — first power-up arcs the connector, weld contacts on relay, sometimes wakes the bench supply protection.
  • Stall detect without encoder — current-only stall always misfires during normal accel. Need ω feedback.
  • Auto-retry into a real fault — driver oscillates trip → retry → trip, parts cook over minutes.
