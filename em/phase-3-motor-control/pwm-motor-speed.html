PWM Motor Speed Control

Chop the supply with a transistor. The motor inductance averages the pulses into a near-DC current. Duty cycle ≈ speed.

A brushed DC motor doesn't care that its supply is being switched on and off thousands of times a second — its winding inductance acts as a low-pass filter, smoothing the chopped voltage into an average current that produces average torque. The duty cycle sets the average voltage (V_avg = D · V_supply), which sets the no-load speed (ω ≈ V_avg / K_e). Pick the PWM frequency high enough to keep current ripple small and noise inaudible, but not so high that switching losses dominate. Standard band for hobby motors: 16–25 kHz (above human hearing, below most MOSFET-comfort limits).

What to notice

  • Duty cycle → speed, almost linearly — once friction is overcome, ω ≈ D · V_supply / K_e. Below a "deadband" duty (motor can't break static friction), the motor sits still and just hums.
  • The motor inductance is the filter — there's no external cap or inductor; the winding's L/R time constant determines how much current ripple you see. Cheap RC-car motors (0.1 mH, 0.1 Ω) ripple wildly; precision gear motors (5 mH, 5 Ω) self-smooth.
  • Ripple peaks at D=50% — formula ΔI ∝ D(1−D), max at half-duty. At 0% and 100%, ripple is zero (DC).
  • Frequency trade-off — too low (<1 kHz): audible whine, big ripple, torque pulsations. Too high (>100 kHz): switching losses cook the FET. Sweet spot: 16–25 kHz (above hearing).
  • The motor draws I_stall at startup — when ω=0, back-EMF=0, so PWM-avg current = V_avg/R and the instantaneous peak during PWM-on is V_sup/R. A 12 V / 2 Ω motor at 50% duty has V_avg/R = 3 A average and V_sup/R = 6 A peak during each on-pulse, even if it draws 0.2 A running once spun up. Size your H-bridge FETs and sense resistor for the peak, not steady-state.
  • Coast vs brake during PWM-off — sync-rectified low-side ON during off-time recirculates current and acts like braking (fast current decay). All-OFF lets the diode handle freewheel and decay is slower. Most drivers default to slow-decay; some let you pick.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Avg V
V_avg = D · V_supply
Avg I
I_avg = (V_avg − K_e·ω) / R
Ripple
ΔI ≈ V_sup·D·(1−D) / (f·L)
Stall I
I_stall_peak = V_sup / R (D=100%)
I_stall_avg = V_avg / R (PWM)
No-load ω
ω_NL = V_avg / K_e

MEMORIZE

  • f_PWM — 16–25 kHz for brushed DC (above hearing).
  • τ_elec = L/R — want > 5·T_PWM for smooth I.
  • Ripple max at D=50% — design ΔI budget there.
  • Size for I_stall — startup >> running current.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Deadband — <5–15% duty often = no motion; firmware should snap to zero or skip past it.
  • 1 kHz audible PWM — sounds like a banshee under load; users hate it.
  • Slow body diode — wastes power & causes reverse-recovery EMI in synchronous rectification.
  • Confusing V_avg with V_RMS — for motor torque the average matters; for resistor heating use RMS.
