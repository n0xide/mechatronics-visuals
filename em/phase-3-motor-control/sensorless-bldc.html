Sensorless BLDC / PMSM — how to run a motor without Hall sensors or an encoder

A "sensorless" motor drive infers rotor angle from electrical signals alone — back-EMF on the open phase (6-step) or by injecting test signals + watching saliency (FOC). It's how fans, drones, e-scooters, and most consumer BLDCs run. The catch: at zero speed there's no back-EMF, so all sensorless drives need a startup ritual. This visual walks both the steady-state detection and the startup hack.

Mode 1 — 6-step trapezoidal sensorless

In trapezoidal BLDC commutation, two phases are energized at any time; the third phase is floating. Because the rotor magnet sweeps past that floating coil, it induces a back-EMF voltage. The zero-crossing of that BEMF — relative to the neutral-point voltage — happens exactly 30° (electrical) before the next commutation should fire. Delay 30°, commutate, repeat. Six commutations per electrical cycle, hence "6-step."

Mode 2 — Sensorless FOC with an observer

Sinusoidal FOC needs continuous angle, not just six discrete events. The drive runs a state observer that takes measured phase currents (iα, iβ) and commanded voltages (vα, vβ), models the stator equation, and outputs an estimated rotor angle θ̂ and speed ω̂. Common variants: sliding-mode observer (SMO), extended Kalman filter (EKF), and flux-linkage integration with high-pass leakage compensation.

The startup problem (and 4 ways to solve it)

At ω = 0 there is no BEMF, no current ripple from rotation, and (often) no saliency you can exploit at standstill. Every sensorless drive needs a startup strategy that ignores rotor-angle feedback until speed is high enough to track:

BEMF zero-cross detection in detail (6-step)

For trapezoidal 6-step, the trick is: during each 60° commutation step, the floating phase's voltage (measured vs. emulated neutral) crosses zero exactly halfway through that step. From the zero-cross, delay another 30° and commutate to the next pattern.

The neutral-point problem

You don't have access to the motor's true neutral point (it's inside the windings, hidden inside the can). Two workarounds:

  • Resistor divider network — three resistors in star from the phase pins create an "emulated neutral." The floating phase BEMF is measured relative to this point. Simple, used in nearly all hobby ESCs.
  • Integrated BEMF comparator — chips like TI DRV10970, ST L6234, MPS MP6531 build it in. Comparator output is a single digital edge.

PWM noise — the real-world killer

The floating phase sits at high impedance, so it picks up PWM switching transients from the energized phases via the motor's parasitic capacitance. Mitigations:

  • Sample only during the PWM-on period (synchronous sampling), avoiding the switching edge.
  • Hardware low-pass filter at the BEMF input (a few kHz cutoff). Adds phase delay that must be compensated.
  • Digital median or majority filter on the comparator output over the last 3-5 PWM cycles.
  • Center-aligned PWM places sampling instant at the predictable mid-point.

FOC sensorless — sliding-mode observer in 6 steps

  1. Model the stator: vα = R·iα + L·diα/dt + eα (and same for β). eα, eβ are back-EMFs to estimate.
  2. Build a current observer: integrate the equation forward to predict îα, îβ.
  3. Compare: error = iαmeas - îα.
  4. Sliding-mode gain: êα = K · sign(error). The discontinuous sign function forces the observer to track even with parameter mismatch.
  5. Low-pass filter êα, êβ to remove chatter.
  6. Extract angle: θ̂ = atan2(-êα, êβ). PLL on this θ̂ gives smooth ω̂.

Why sensorless field-weakening is hard

Field-weakening forces Id < 0 (negative d-axis current), which reduces the resultant flux linkage. The observer sees a smaller "effective Ke," and its angle estimate degrades. Many drives lock out sensorless field-weakening, or add a flux-magnitude estimator. (See the field-weakening visual.)

Common failure modes when bringing up a sensorless drive

  • Spins one direction, stalls the other — startup ramp direction wrong, or motor parameter (pole count, R, L) misconfigured.
  • Stalls under load — BEMF noise threshold too high, drive falls off the back-EMF lock. Lower the noise filter cutoff, increase commutation advance angle.
  • Stutters then runs smoothly — startup ramp profile mismatched to inertia. Increase ramp duration, lower starting current.
  • "Demag" warnings — when commutating, the inductive flyback voltage on the just-disconnected phase is taking too long to clear, masking the BEMF zero-cross. Lower current, or enable damped mode (synchronous rectification).
  • Hot motor at low speed — open-loop or I-f startup is forcing current with no field alignment. Limit startup duration; switch to closed loop ASAP.
