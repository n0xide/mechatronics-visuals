Stepper Drivers — A4988 & TMC2209

A stepper driver is a current-controlled H-bridge per phase. It chops the supply to hold a target coil current, then steps that current through a sine table to microstep.

A stepper motor's torque is set by coil current, not voltage. The driver IC uses a supply >> motor's rated voltage and chops it with a current-controlled PWM (a "chopper") to enforce a setpoint current. The setpoint comes from a sine/cosine table indexed by a microstep counter. Each STEP pulse increments the counter; with 256 microsteps per full step, you sweep the sine smoothly enough that the rotor feels continuous torque instead of detents. Two families dominate hobby/desktop: the classic A4988/DRV8825 (Vref pin sets the current, fixed-decay or fast-decay chopper, audible), and Trinamic TMC2208/2209 (UART configured, silent StealthChop or torque-y SpreadCycle, StallGuard sensorless homing). Modern 3D printers and most arm-joint stepper builds use TMC.

Microstepping — sine-table interpolation

Each STEP pulse advances the microstep counter; the driver indexes a sine table to set coil-A current and a 90°-shifted cosine for coil-B. Smaller microsteps = smoother motion + more position-resolution / step pulse, but the same physical detent torque limit applies.

Setting motor current — Vref method (A4988 / DRV8825)

The driver's reference voltage pin sets the chopper's current setpoint. Turn the potentiometer until V_ref hits target. Wrong V_ref = overheated coils (too high) or skipped steps under load (too low).

Set I_max ≈ 0.85 · I_rated at first (gives margin against thermal). Bump to 100% only if torque is borderline. TMC uses I_RMS in firmware; A4988/DRV8825 set I_PEAK.

Chopper current control — fixed off-time

The coil is an inductor across V_motor >> I·R. Coil current ramps up fast (V/L). When current hits I_target (sensed across R_sense), the driver turns OFF for a fixed t_off (typ 20–30 µs A4988; adaptive for TMC SpreadCycle). Current decays during off-time. Loop runs ~30–50 kHz.

What to notice

  • Microstepping is interpolation, not extra torque — hybrid stepper holding torque scales approximately linearly with coil current (T ≈ K_t·I) up to magnetic saturation, not as I². At 1/256 microstep, the per-microstep incremental torque is sin(0.0070°) · T_full ≈ 0.4% of full-step holding torque — tiny. You get smooth motion + position resolution; you do not get 256× the positioning accuracy under load (the rotor settles in the nearest detent if load exceeds incremental torque).
  • Vref sets the current, R_sense sets the formula — A4988: I = V_ref / (8·R_s). DRV8825: I = V_ref / (5·R_s). TMC2209: configured by UART, but R_sense still scales the headroom. Measure V_ref with a multimeter on the trimpot wiper. Wrong by 2× = coils smoke.
  • The driver runs at high V_motor on purpose — coil V_rated is what it draws when stalled (zero back-EMF). At running RPM, back-EMF + I·R needs >> that to keep up; 24 V driving a 2 V-rated coil is normal. The chopper does the math.
  • Decay mode = current ripple shape — slow decay (recirculate through low-side body diodes) keeps ripple low at high duty, but at the top of the sine it can't decrease fast enough → distortion. Fast decay (reverse the bridge) tracks better but rings audibly. Mixed (start slow, finish fast) is the modern default.
  • TMC StealthChop is voltage-mode PWM — it computes the voltage needed for the target current at the current rotor speed (using a learned coil-R model). Silent because no hard chopper edges. Loses torque above its tuned speed range; switch to SpreadCycle at TPWMTHRS.
  • StallGuard senses load on the motor itself — measures back-EMF drop into the coil during the off interval. As load rises, drop falls. Threshold trips → "sensorless endstop" homing without a switch. Only works in SpreadCycle mode and above a minimum speed (~2 rps).

SET CURRENT (BEGINNER PATH)

  • Read motor I_rated from datasheet (e.g. 1.2 A/phase).
  • Target 85% of rated first → 1.0 A.
  • Compute V_ref from chip formula → set trimpot.
  • Touch coil after 5 min — warm OK, painful = too high.
  • Listen at slow feed — skipped steps = bump up 10%.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Vref on EN pin — wrong pin destroys driver instantly.
  • Hot-unplug stepper — never disconnect motor with power on, back-EMF spike kills driver.
  • Wrong R_sense assumption — fake A4988s ship with 0.05 Ω, original with 0.05/0.1; check the board silkscreen.
  • StealthChop missed steps — high accel + low torque headroom = stall. Enable SpreadCycle above TPWMTHRS.
  • Bipolar = 4 wires — 8-wire motors → wire pairs in series (high voltage, low current) or parallel.
