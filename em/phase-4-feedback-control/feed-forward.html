Feed-forward — pre-emptive control

If you already know what torque is needed to hold a robot arm against gravity at a given pose, why force a PID controller to figure it out from scratch by accumulating error? Feed-forward injects the model-known control action directly, so the PID only has to clean up the leftover modelling error. Example: a 6-DOF arm joint moving against gravity — without FF, the integrator has to wind up to hold each pose.

Block diagram

Feed-forward path (green) computes the expected control effort from the setpoint without waiting for error. Feedback PID (blue) corrects the residual.

Robot arm gravity compensation

One-link arm of length L, mass m, pivot at the shoulder. Joint angle θ measured from the horizontal. Gravity torque τ_g = m·g·L·cos(θ). The motor must supply this just to hold position. With FF on, the controller adds τ_FF = m·g·L·cos(θ_setpoint). PID only sees inertia and disturbance.

Acceleration (inertial)τ_FF = J·α_setpointAggressive trajectories, CNC, 3D printer
Inverse dynamics (full)τ_FF = M(q)·q̈ + C(q,q̇)·q̇ + g(q)High-performance multi-DOF arms (computed torque control)

What to notice

  • FF off + low Ki: huge steady-state droop. Without integral wind-up the arm sags below the setpoint because no controller term knows about gravity. Pure Kp can't hold up a load — the residual is τ_g / Kp.
  • FF off + high Ki: it eventually works. But the integrator has to slowly wind up to carry the gravity load. That's exactly what FF replaces — and it does it instantly, at the setpoint.
  • FF on: PID torque drops to zero in steady state. Watch the orange (PID) trace — once the arm is at the setpoint, the PID has nothing left to do. The model is carrying the load.
  • For ramp motions, gravity FF alone isn't enough. Switch to "ramp" motion + velocity FF and the arm tracks without lagging behind. Without velocity FF, the controller must build up a velocity error to drive velocity — always 1 step behind.
  • FF needs a model. Wrong mass, wrong link length → wrong FF → leftover error for PID. FF + PID is symbiotic: FF handles what you know, PID handles what you don't.
  • FF reduces integral windup. Less work for I means less wind-up risk and snappier disturbance rejection.

Cheat sheet

Combined

Gravity (1 link)

Velocity FF
u_v = K_v · v_sp
Acceleration FF
u_a = J · a_sp
Inverse-dyn FF (multi-DOF)
τ = M(q)·q̈_sp + C(q,q̇)·q̇_sp + g(q)

WHY ADD FF

  • Reaches setpoint faster — no error needs to build up first
  • Less integral windup — I doesn't carry the steady load
  • Better trajectory tracking — velocity FF kills tracking lag
  • Lower Kp possible — more stable loop, less noise amplification

PITFALLS

  • FF without PID = open-loop — disturbances ignored
  • Wrong model → FF pushes the wrong way → fight with PID
  • Mass/inertia changes (gripper picks up payload) → recompute FF online or use adaptive ID
  • Discontinuous FF (Coulomb friction sign flip) → jitter at velocity = 0
