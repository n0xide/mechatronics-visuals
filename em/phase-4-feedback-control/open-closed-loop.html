Open-loop vs closed-loop control

An open-loop controller assumes the plant behaves exactly as modelled — no measurement, no correction. A closed-loop controller measures the output and subtracts setpoint from measurement to compute an error, then drives the actuator to shrink that error. Behaviour on missed stepPermanently lost — error grows foreverDetected by encoder, corrected
Behaviour under load spikeStalls silently, may skip stepsIncreases current until torque limit hit

What to notice

  • Open-loop has no idea anything went wrong. The orange trace shifts by the entire disturbance amount and stays there forever, because there is no measurement to compare against.
  • Closed-loop sees the error and shrinks it. The blue trace dips momentarily when the disturbance hits, then the controller fights it back toward the setpoint. With P-only control there is a small residual offset (covered in the I-control visual).
  • Plant gain mismatch is fatal for open-loop. Slide "plant gain mismatch" away from 100 % — open-loop never reaches the setpoint, but closed-loop barely cares, because it's driven by error, not by a model.
  • Higher Kp shrinks the disturbance dip faster. But push it too far and the closed-loop trace will start to oscillate or overshoot — that's the next visual.
  • Open-loop wins when the plant is perfectly known and untouched by disturbances. Lasers steering galvos in clean rooms, microwave-oven magnetrons, and steppers in benign loads can run open-loop forever.

WHEN TO USE WHICH

  • Open-loop — plant is known, repeatable, disturbance-free, cheap actuator
  • Closed-loop — disturbances, varying load, precision matters, anything safety-critical
  • Hybrid (feed-forward + feedback) — most real motion control; covered in feed-forward visual

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Stepper with skipped steps drifts forever and you only find out at the end of the job
  • Open-loop thermal control on a heater works until ambient changes, then the part is over-baked
  • Closed-loop with bad sensor chases noise; controller now reduces accuracy
  • Loop gain too high turns disturbance rejection into oscillation
