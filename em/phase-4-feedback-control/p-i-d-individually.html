P, I, D in isolation

Same plant, same step input, same noise budget — three controllers, one term each. This is the cleanest way to see what each term contributes (and what each one breaks). Common plant: first-order lag with a tiny dead time, like a small DC motor speed loop.

P only — proportional

u = Kp·e
Fast, but always leaves a residual error (unless plant has free integrator). Push Kp too high → oscillates, then instability.

I only — integral

u = Ki·∫e dt
Eventually drives error to zero. Slow on its own. Pure integral on second-order plants is unstable — needs P or D to behave.

D only — derivative

u = −Kd·(dy/dt)
Acts on rate of change. Without P or I there is no DC gain — output stays near zero. Amplifies sensor noise — visible as jitter on the trace.

Plant + measurements

First-order plant τ·dy/dt + y = u, integrated forward Euler at dt = 5 ms. All three loops see identical white-noise sensor reading. Step setpoint applied at t = 0.5 s. D is computed on the measurement (not on error) to keep the kick out at the step edge.

What to notice

  • P-only never reaches the setpoint exactly. Steady-state error e_ss = r / (1 + Kp·K_plant) for a first-order plant. Doubling Kp roughly halves the residual — until you hit the ringing wall.
  • I-only is too slow to use alone. The integral has to wind up from zero before any control action exists. Pair it with P (PI) and you keep the speed and kill the offset.
  • D-only does nothing in steady state. Once dy/dt = 0 the control output is 0, so the loop has no DC gain. Crank Kd, see jitter, but no useful pull toward the setpoint.
  • Noise is D's enemy. Raise the noise slider — the orange D-only trace gets jagged while P and I stay smooth, because the derivative passes high frequencies straight through.
  • Increase τ to shrink ringing. Slower plants tolerate higher gains. Faster plants need either lower gain or D damping to stay calm.
  • Combining is additive — but the wins compound: u = Kp·e + Ki·∫e + Kd·de/dt. PI kills residual error without ringing; PD damps overshoot without I windup; PID gets all three and most second-order systems need it.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

P term
u_P = Kp · e
I term
u_I = Ki · ∫e dt
D term
u_D = Kd · de/dt
Steady-state err (P only, type-0)
e_ss = r / (1 + Kp·Kplant)

ROLE OF EACH TERM

  • P — speed of correction; sets bandwidth
  • I — eliminates steady-state error; corrects bias
  • D — damps overshoot; predicts; never use alone
  • Sample rate ≥ 10× loop bandwidth or you will not see what you think you see

WHEN NOT TO USE EACH

  • D on noisy speed loops (encoder velocity from finite differences) — amplifies quantization
  • I on plants with hard limits without anti-windup — guaranteed overshoot
  • P only on temperature / level control where offset matters — always combine with I
  • D on slow processes (chem, thermal) — bandwidth too low for D to help
