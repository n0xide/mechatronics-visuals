PID tuning — Ziegler-Nichols, both methods

Two classic tuning methods, both produced by Ziegler and Nichols in 1942 and still the fastest way to get a usable starting point. The step-response method works on an open-loop plant — identify L (delay) and τ (time constant), look up gains. The ultimate-gain method drives the closed loop to the brink of instability and reads off the period of oscillation. Both are aggressive on purpose; back off after.

Apply a unit step to the open-loop plant. Find the inflection point on the response, draw the tangent line. L is the time the tangent crosses zero; L + τ is the time it reaches the final value. Z-N formula: Kp = 1.2τ/L, Ti = 2L, Td = 0.5L. Adjust the plant sliders to see how the recipe shifts.

What to notice

  • L matters more than τ for the recipe. Cut L in half and the suggested Kp doubles. Dead time is the killer of control loops, because the controller is always reacting to old information.
  • Sub-step method failure: plant with no dead time. If L ≈ 0 the formula explodes (Kp → ∞). Z-N step is for plants that have at least some transport delay or measurable rise.
  • Find Ku by ear (or by eye). Push Kp up until peaks neither grow nor shrink — that's Ku. Above it, peaks grow; below, they shrink. Pu is the period of the sustained oscillation.
  • Z-N is aggressive by design. Both methods aim for ¼-decay-ratio response (each oscillation peak is 25 % of the previous). That's faster than most production systems want. Halve the Kp from the recipe and you get a calmer, near-ideal response.
  • Never run Z-N on safety-critical plants. The ultimate-gain method intentionally destabilises the system; that's fine for a bench rig but lethal for HVAC, reactors, or robot arms holding payloads above humans.
  • Modern alternatives: SIMC, Cohen-Coon, IMC tuning, AMIGO — all give less aggressive starting points than Z-N. But for hobby projects on benign plants, Z-N gets you a working loop in five minutes.

Z-N ULTIMATE-GAIN

Identify
Ku = Kp at sustained oscillation
Pu = period of oscillation
P
Kp = 0.5 Ku
PI
Kp = 0.45 Ku, Ti = Pu/1.2
PID
Kp = 0.6 Ku, Ti = Pu/2, Td = Pu/8

PITFALLS & SAFETY

  • Z-N gives an aggressive starting point — back off Kp ~50 % for production use
  • Step-response method needs a true open-loop test — break the feedback loop first
  • Ultimate-gain method is risky — don't run on equipment with people, hot fluids, or expensive payloads
  • Don't trust either method on noisy plants — L and Pu become hard to read
  • Filter D term — Td = Pu/8 with no filter is asking for derivative noise amplification
