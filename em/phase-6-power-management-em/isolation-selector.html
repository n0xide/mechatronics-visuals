Isolation Selector

Galvanic isolation breaks the metallic connection between two circuits. You need it whenever ground loops, mains voltage, or high-side current sensing puts dangerous voltages near low-voltage logic. Three families dominate: optocouplers (slow, cheap, mature), digital isolators (fast, expensive, IC-package), and magnetic isolators (ultra-fast, exotic). The selection comes down to CTR, propagation delay, and CMTI (common-mode transient immunity).

Why isolate?

  • Safety — mains-side circuits (offline switching supply) must be isolated from user-touchable secondary side. Regulatory: IEC 60601, UL 1577.
  • Ground loops — two pieces of equipment plugged into different outlets have ground potential differences (volts at AC, ms-scale). Isolating signal lines between them prevents this current from coursing through your data cables.
  • High-side measurement — current sensing on the high side of a 400 V bus needs to talk to MCU referenced to GND. Isolated amplifier or ADC required.
  • Industrial environments — surge events, ESD, transient ground bounce. Each isolated chunk is a noise firewall.
  • Lightning-prone exposed cables — RS-485 in a barn, Ethernet to a remote sensor. Isolation transformer plus surge clamps survive nearby strikes.

Optocouplers

An LED inside the chip emits light through a transparent gap to a photodiode + transistor on the other side. The two sides are coupled only by photons; the gap is rated for kV breakdown.

Pros: cheap (~$0.20 PC817), well-understood, mature, easy to replace. Robust to ESD on the input. Several thousand volts isolation.

Cons: slow (5–50 µs propagation), aging (CTR drops over years and with high current), poor CMTI (common-mode transients shoot through), highish input current (10–20 mA LED).

Key spec: CTR (current transfer ratio) = I_C(output) / I_F(LED input). PC817 has CTR ~50 % to 600 % depending on bin. Design your input current so the worst-case CTR still saturates the output.

Digital isolators (capacitive / RF)

Modern (Si864x, ADuM, ISO76xx). Inside the chip, two dies face each other across a SiO₂ or polyimide capacitor. The transmitter encodes the digital input as RF pulses; the receiver demodulates. Both sides are still galvanically isolated, but now there's no slow LED in the path.

Pros: fast (10–100 ns propagation), high CMTI (100+ kV/µs typical), no aging, low input current (no LED to drive — just a CMOS input), multi-channel in one package (typical: 4 channels SO-16).

Cons: more expensive (~$2–5), needs power on both sides (you need a separate isolated supply or bus-power scheme), can be sensitive to layout (radiated emissions).

Magnetic isolators

Transformers + integrated digital encoders. ADI's iCoupler uses on-chip transformers; Silicon Labs' Si86xx uses a similar approach. NVE has GMR (giant magneto-resistive) isolators that use magnetic field, not light or RF.

Used in places where extreme common-mode transient immunity is needed (motor drives, EV chargers), or where the bus is itself an isolation transformer (RS-485 with magnetic coupling).

How to pick — decision tree

Data rate < 100 kHz, cost-critical → PC817 optocoupler. Data rate 100 kHz–10 MHz → HCPL-2630/8 fast opto, or any digital isolator. Data rate > 10 MHz → digital isolator only. Need extreme CMTI (motor drive gate signals) → iCoupler/magnetic isolator. Need 4+ channels in one footprint → digital isolator (single SO-16) vs. 4 separate optos. Multi-year reliability essential → digital or magnetic, no LEDs aging.

CTR — the optocoupler design parameter

CTR = I_C / I_F. To saturate the output transistor (low V_CE_sat), you need: I_F × CTR ≥ I_load. Worst-case bin and end-of-life CTR both matter.

PC817 CTR range: 50–600 % (huge bin spread). Worst-case (B-bin, end-of-life): say 50 %. To pull 1 mA collector → need I_F = 1 mA / 0.5 = 2 mA LED. Typical design: I_F = 5–10 mA for margin. CTR drops with LED current (above ~10 mA), with temperature, and with age. Datasheet gives temperature-dependence curves. For long-life industrial: design with 5× CTR margin + thermal derate.

CMTI — common-mode transient immunity

The biggest reason to choose a digital isolator over an opto is CMTI. When the high-side ground bounces (because of a motor commutating, an SCR firing, an arc-welding event), the rate of change of common-mode voltage can be tens of kV/µs. If your isolator doesn't reject this, the high-side spike couples capacitively into your low-side logic and flips a bit.

Opto CMTI: typically 10–15 kV/µs (older parts: 1 kV/µs).

Digital CMTI: typically 50–100 kV/µs.

For motor drives, EV chargers, or any system with hard-switched IGBTs/SiC FETs, you want at least 50 kV/µs.

Worked example — isolated RS-485 between robot base and arm

The setup

Robot base (battery, motors) and arm board (sensors) communicate over RS-485 at 1 Mbps. Common-mode shifts of ±25 V are possible due to motor switching currents in the chassis ground. Need 4 channels: TX, RX, DE, RE.

Optocoupler option

HCPL-2630 dual fast opto × 2 = 4 channels, ~$3 total. Propagation delay ~150 ns. Bit time at 1 Mbps = 1 µs. Two-way comms: 150 ns × 2 = 300 ns end-to-end — works, with margin.

Digital isolator option

ADuM1411 single chip, 4 channels, ~$4.50. Propagation 32 ns. CMTI 100 kV/µs (vs. 5 kV/µs for the optos). Lower bit-error rate in noisy environments.

Power isolation

RS-485 itself can be transformer-coupled (Ethernet-style) or just signal-isolated (separate isolated DC-DC for the transceiver). Add a Si8642 paired with a TI ISOW7841 (isolated DC-DC + 4-ch isolator in one) for the full solution.

Verdict

Digital isolator. The cost premium ($1.50) buys 5× better CMTI and lower bit-error rate. In a robot where motor switching is on the same chassis, that's worth it.

Where you'll see this

Real uses

  • Offline switching supplies — primary-secondary feedback opto.
  • Gate drivers for high-side MOSFETs (especially in motor drives, EV chargers).
  • Isolated USB-to-serial (FT231XS-iso, USB2ISO) — clean serial to mains-side test gear.
  • Industrial IO modules — DI/DO channels isolated to 1500 V.
  • Medical devices — strict regulatory isolation, often basic isolation + reinforced barrier.

QUICK PICKER

  • < 100 kHz, hobby → PC817
  • 1–10 MHz, professional → HCPL-2630 / Si8641
  • 10+ MHz, multi-channel → ADuM1411 / Si8642
  • Motor drive gates → UCC21520 / Si8275
  • SafetyMains → basic + reinforced (IEC 60601)

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Too-little CTR margin — works new, fails in 2 years as LED ages.
  • Forgetting both sides need power — digital iso doesn't bootstrap.
  • CMTI tested at typical, not max — worst-case is what matters in motor drives.
  • Single-channel iso ≠ basic isolation; need to look at safety standards for medical/aviation.
  • Pin-compatible drop-in — opto vs digital often share package but not pin function.
