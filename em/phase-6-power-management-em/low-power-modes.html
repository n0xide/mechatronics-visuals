MCU Low-Power Modes

Active · Sleep · Deep-sleep · Standby. Pick a mode, dial a duty cycle, watch average current drop by 1000×.

Every MCU has a hierarchy of sleep modes that trade current draw for what's retained and how fast it wakes. The trick to long battery life isn't picking the deepest mode — it's spending most of your time there. A sensor node that wakes for 50 ms once a second and sleeps the other 950 ms can outlast its battery shelf life, even if the active draw is 80 mA.

What to notice

  • The y-axis is log. Active to Standby spans 6 orders of magnitude on the same chip — 80 mA vs ~300 nA. You don't ramp current down; you collapse it.
  • Duty cycle is the multiplier. 5% active duty at 80 mA = 4 mA average. Drop to 0.1% active duty and average is 80 µA. Same active current; different runtime.
  • Sleep current dominates at low duty. Once active duty is below ~1%, the sleep current sets the floor — it doesn't matter if your active mode is 80 mA or 800 mA. So pick the deepest sleep that retains what you need.
  • Wake time matters for short tasks. Standby wakes in 1−10 ms (PLL re-lock, RAM init). If you need to act every 100 ms, you can't use Standby — pick Sleep or Deep-sleep with 1−100 µs wake.
  • What's retained matters more than current. Standby on STM32 loses RAM. If your state machine needs context, either keep variables in backup-domain RAM, save to flash, or use a shallower mode.
  • Quiescent of everything else matters too. Killing MCU draw to 1 µA is pointless if your buck converter has 5 mA quiescent. Sleep modes only pay off in tandem with low-Iq regulators and properly powered-down peripherals.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • GPIOs left as inputs — floating pins toggle, leak microamps each. Set as outputs low, or analog.
  • Peripheral clocks left on — ADC/UART eat mA in Sleep if you don't gate them.
  • Pull-ups on idle lines — an I2C pull-up to 3.3V via 4.7 k = 700 µA continuous if line is low.
  • External flash / sensor self-consumption — MCU sleeps fine; the BME280 still draws 1 µA standby, the LCD backlight is 20 mA.
