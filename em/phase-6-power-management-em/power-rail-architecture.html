Power Rail Architecture

From a single battery to the multiple regulated rails a robot actually needs — with star ground for sensitive analog, separate motor rail, and live load aggregation. Regime: steady-state operation, single battery source. Inrush, sequencing, and brownout dynamics are covered on their own pages.

A robot is not powered by a battery. It's powered by a system of rails derived from a battery. The MCU wants a clean 3.3 V at <100 mA, the encoder wants a clean 3.3 V isolated from motor noise, the motor wants 12 V at 2 A peaks, and the gate driver wants 5 V logic. One battery, many converters, one shared ground that must not let motor current contaminate analog measurements. What to notice

  • Motor current dominates everything. 1.5 A at 12 V on the motor rail (18 W) dwarfs every logic load combined (~0.5 W). The battery current is essentially the motor current divided by converter efficiency — logic is rounding error.
  • 3.3 V comes from 5 V via LDO, not from battery via single converter. The LDO cleans up switching ripple from the upstream buck and is cheap because the drop is small (1.7 V at ~100 mA = 0.17 W dissipation). Going straight from 11 V battery to 3.3 V via LDO would dissipate (11−3.3)·0.1 = 0.77 W — needs a heatsink.
  • Analog gets its own LDO, fed directly from battery through a ferrite. The IMU and ADC reference must not see motor switching noise. Spend the extra LDO; the bill of materials cost is dollars, the SNR cost of skipping it is permanent.
  • Star ground = one physical point where all returns meet. Motor return current cannot flow under sensitive analog traces, because it has its own short path back to the battery negative. AGND and DGND tie only at the star.
  • Bulk cap at battery + bulk cap at motor input. Battery wires have inductance; a 1000 µF cap right at the H-bridge is what actually absorbs PWM transients. The battery-side cap stabilizes the bus when the motor cap charges.
  • Fuse sized for sustained load, not peak. A 5 A fuse holds 2 A motor average plus margin, and blows on a dead short. It does not protect against gradual MOSFET cookoff — that's what OCP and TSD inside the driver are for.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

I_batt
Σ (V_rail·I_rail) / (η·V_batt)
LDO dissipation
P = (V_in − V_out)·I_out
Runtime
t = Q_batt / I_avg
Rail headroom
I_rated ≥ 1.5·I_peak

RAIL HIERARCHY

  • Battery → fuse → bulk cap → bus.
  • Buck for big drops (12V→5V) — switching, high eff.
  • LDO for small drops & clean rails (5V→3.3V).
  • Analog rail: separate LDO + ferrite from battery.
  • Motor rail: its own converter, isolated returns.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • One LDO from 12V to 3.3V at 200 mA — 1.74 W dissipated, IC melts.
  • Shared GND trace under analog — motor return injects 50 mV into ADC ground reference.
  • No bulk cap at motor input — PWM edges modulate the 5 V logic rail by 100 mV.
  • Forget converter quiescent current — 5 mA quiescent on a sleeping robot kills battery in days.
