Connector Matrix

Choosing the wrong connector kills more robot prototypes than firmware bugs. Too-small connectors melt under inrush, too-big ones don't fit. Unkeyed connectors plug in backwards. Non-locking connectors vibrate loose. This visual rolls up the dozen most-used hobby and industrial families — XT-30/60/90, JST PH/XH/VH/SH, Molex Micro-Fit, GX12/16 — into one current-rating-vs-pitch table with a use-case picker.

XT-series detail

XT-30/60/90 are 2-pin LiPo battery connectors molded in nylon. The plug is a deeply chamfered bullet contact; the socket grips it. The housing is keyed: positive contact slightly larger than negative.

  • XT30: 15 A continuous, 30 A burst. 1S–3S LiPo packs to ~1500 mAh.
  • XT60: 30 A continuous, 60 A burst. The de-facto RC standard for 3S–4S LiPo (RC cars, drones, e-skateboards).
  • XT90: 60 A continuous. For big EV and e-bike packs. Anti-spark version has a low-value precharge resistor (~5–10 Ω, vendor-dependent — AMASS XT90-S uses ~5.6 Ω) on a longer pre-charge pin that brings the load capacitance up to bus voltage before the main power contacts close — saves the connector from arc erosion. At 60 V the initial precharge current is ~11 A, decaying as the cap charges; with 1 mF bus cap RC ≈ 6 ms so the precharge is brief. (A 10 kΩ resistor would be too large — RC with 1 mF cap ≈ 10 s, far too slow before main contact.)
  • EC3 / EC5 are similar 2-pin power connectors from the same era; less common now.

JST detail (the family of families)

JST is a company; they make hundreds of connector series. The four most-used in hobbies:

  • JST SH (1.00 mm pitch): tiny, for STEMMA-QT, Qwiic, small flex cables. 1 A/contact. Hand-crimping is hard; use pre-made jumpers.
  • JST PH (2.00 mm): the workhorse. Found on Pico W battery jacks, IMU breakouts, sensor modules. 2 A/contact.
  • JST XH (2.50 mm): a step up. Used for balance leads on LiPo batteries (e.g., 4S balance plug = 5-pin XH). 3 A/contact.
  • JST VH (3.96 mm): largest of the four. Power-supply pigtails, larger DC lines. 10 A/contact.

Molex detail

Molex Micro-Fit 3.0 (3.0 mm pitch) is the modern industrial standard for medium-current locked connectors. Positive latch with an audible click; mating force ~10 N; vibration-rated. 2–24 pin configurations; 8.5 A per contact.

Mini-Fit Jr (4.20 mm pitch) is the big sibling — what every PC ATX power connector uses (Molex 39-01-2200 etc.). 13 A per contact.

Round panel-mount (GX, M12)

GX12/16/20 are aviation-style metal-body screw-lock connectors. The pin count is the last digit's intended meaning (e.g., GX16-4 is 4-pin). Inexpensive ($1–3); ubiquitous on cheap CNC and 3D-printer enclosures; available with IP67 seal grommets.

M12 connectors are the industrial sensor standard. 5-pin M12 for Profinet/EtherCAT/CANopen with screw or push-pull lock. Way more expensive ($10–50) but very robust.

What to notice

  • Current rating is per contact, with a derating factor for adjacent pins all carrying current. A 16-pin Micro-Fit at 8.5 A nominal really means ~5 A per pin when fully loaded.
  • Cycle life matters for plugged-in-and-out interfaces (battery, USB-equivalent). XT-60: 200+ cycles. JST PH: 50 cycles (designed for assembly, not service).
  • Locking saves robots from accidental death. A JST PH on a low-vibration bench fixture is fine. The same connector on a flying drone won't survive a vibration test.
  • Polarization is not the same as keying. Polarization means you can figure out which way; keying means the connector won't go the wrong way (asymmetric body). XT-60 has both; Dupont has neither.
  • Don't mix connector families across a system — your future-self will plug XH balance leads into PH battery leads and wonder why your 11 V LiPo cooks the 3.3 V regulator.

Where you'll see this

Real uses

  • Quadrotor — XT60 for battery, JST XH for balance, JST PH for telemetry, M12 for camera gimbal.
  • 3D-printer — Mini-Fit Jr for PSU, GX16 for hotend, JST PH/XH for fans.
  • Robot arm — Micro-Fit for joint motors, M12 for sensors, GX12 for tool-changer.
  • Cyberdeck — JST PH for batteries, JST SH for I²C breakouts, USB-C/JST for charging.
  • Pi/breadboard prototype — Dupont 2.54 mm everywhere; replace with proper connectors in production.

RULES OF THUMB

  • Derate 30 % from rated current for safety.
  • Locking for any robot that moves or vibrates.
  • Anti-spark for any pack > 6S (24 V).
  • Color code your wires — red=+, black=−, signal colors per JIS.
  • Crimp, don't solder connector pins. Soldered crimps fail under flex.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Reverse polarity — Dupont jumpers, twin-cable XT plugs.
  • Connector melt — undersized for inrush.
  • Vibration loosening — JST PH in a quadcopter is a bad idea.
  • Bad crimps — pins push out under load.
  • Mixing pitches — 2.0 vs 2.5 mm visually similar; won't actually mate.
