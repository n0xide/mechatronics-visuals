DFA / DFM Explorer — Design for Assembly + Manufacturability

DFA (Design for Assembly) reduces part count and makes parts easy to handle. DFM (Design for Manufacturability) makes each part easy to produce. The two together can drop a product's cost by 30-70 % AND its defect rate by 10× — same function, half the parts, half the assembly time. The Boothroyd-Dewhurst DFA method

Originated at U. Mass. Amherst (Boothroyd 1980, Dewhurst joined 1985). The method scores each part on:

TestQuestionPass?
1. FunctionDoes the part move during operation?YES → keep as separate part
2. MaterialMust this part be a different material than adjacent parts?YES → keep separate
3. Assembly accessMust this part be separate to allow assembly of others?YES → keep separate

If ALL three answers are NO, the part is a candidate for elimination (merge into a neighboring part or remove entirely). This single rule typically halves the part count.

Worked example — pen redesign

Original ballpoint pen

8 parts: outer barrel, inner barrel, spring, ink cartridge, ball, clip, end cap, label. Assembly time ~25 seconds.

5 parts: barrel (with integral clip + print), spring, cartridge, ball, end cap. Assembly time ~12 seconds — half. Cost ~40% lower.

DFM principles (per-part)

ProcessKey DFM rule
Injection moldingUniform wall thickness ±25%; 1-3° draft on all walls; ribs ≤ 50% of wall thickness; no undercuts
Sheet metalMin bend radius = 1× material thickness; flanges > 4× thickness; no narrow features < 2× thickness
CNC millingMax depth = 6× tool diameter; internal corners need fillet (no sharp internal corners); pocket floors are flat (not rounded)
CNC turningL/D ratio < 5 for unsupported; no internal corners narrower than smallest available tool
FDM (3D print)45° self-supporting overhang; bridge max 5 mm; first layer wide; orient strong axis along load
CastingUniform thickness, draft angles, no thin features (sand) or sharp corners; gating + risers needed

The 10× rules of thumb

  • Eliminating a part saves 10× the cost of optimizing one — focus on part-count reduction first.
  • Mistakes caught at design save 10× compared to manufacturing fix, which saves 10× compared to field recall — design phase is 1, manufacturing is 10, field is 100.
  • 1 fewer part = 1 fewer fastener + 1 fewer alignment step + 1 fewer inventory SKU + 1 fewer point of failure.
