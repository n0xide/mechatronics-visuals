Complementary Filter (IMU tilt)

Gyroscopes give clean short-term angle changes but drift over seconds. Accelerometers measure gravity for absolute tilt but get jumpy with vibration. Combine them with a 1-line α blend: θ = α·(θ + gyro·dt) + (1−α)·θ_accel. The gyro half is a high-pass; the accel half is a low-pass; together they cover the whole frequency range. This is the workhorse of every quadcopter, every self-balancing robot, every PoseEstimation library.

Two sensors, complementary noise

Most low-cost IMUs (MPU6050, BMI160, BNO055, ICM-42688) bundle a 3-axis gyro + 3-axis accel in one package. For tilt estimation, we use one axis pair:

  • Gyro: gives angular velocity (deg/s or rad/s). Integrate over time to get angle. Two issues: (a) zero-rate drift — even when stationary, the gyro outputs ~0.5°/s, and (b) integration accumulates that drift into seconds-to-minutes-scale absolute-angle error. Strengths: fast response, no noise from vibration, no need to assume gravity is the only acceleration.
  • Accel: when stationary, measures gravity. Convert (a_x, a_y, a_z) to a tilt angle via atan2(a_y, sqrt(a_x² + a_z²)). Issues: assumes the only acceleration is gravity; any sudden motion (a kicked robot, a propulsion event, vibration from motors) overwhelms gravity. Strengths: drift-free; correct absolute angle.
  • Magnetometer (optional 9-DOF): heading reference. Not used in tilt-only filters but added for full attitude.

The α blend

One line of code:

θ_new = α · (θ_old + gyro · dt) + (1 − α) · θ_accel Where α is typically 0.95–0.99 — close to 1, but not 1. α = 0 → just accelerometer (jittery). α = 1 → just gyro integration (drifts). α = 0.98 → mostly gyro (smooth), but slowly corrected by accel (drift-free).

The filter has a single equivalent time constant τ that controls how fast it forgets the gyro-integrated estimate and trusts the accelerometer:

τ = α · dt / (1 − α) For α = 0.98, dt = 10 ms: τ = 0.98 · 0.01 / 0.02 = 0.49 s → The filter "forgets" the gyro estimate over ~½ second; that's how long a vibration spike biases your tilt before the accel anchor pulls it back.

Why it works — frequency-domain interpretation

The complementary filter is two transfer functions that sum to 1:

Gyro path = high-pass: H(s) = τs / (τs + 1) Accel path = low-pass: L(s) = 1 / (τs + 1) Sum: H(s) + L(s) = 1 → perfect across all frequencies (if both inputs are perfect) Low frequencies (slow tilts) → accel dominates → drift-free. High frequencies (fast motions) → gyro dominates → noise-free.

That's why it's complementary: each sensor's strength covers the other's weakness, and their combined response is unity gain across the band.

Worked example — self-balancing robot

The robot

A two-wheel inverted-pendulum balancer. MPU6050 reads accel + gyro at 100 Hz (dt = 10 ms). Goal: estimate pitch angle θ within ±1° accuracy so the PID can hold balance.

Choosing α

Slow drift (gyro bias): 0.5°/s typical for MPU6050 after calibration. Want this corrected within 1 second → τ ≈ 1 s.

τ = α·dt / (1−α) → 1 = α · 0.01 / (1 − α) → α / (1 − α) = 100 → α = 0.99

Vibration handling

Motors spin at 50–200 Hz. Wheel hubs (which mount the IMU) shake at twice that. With α = 0.99, the filter rejects vibration above 0.16 Hz crossover — so any spike longer than ~6 s would leak through. Real spikes are tens of milliseconds; they're absorbed cleanly.

Initialization

At startup, set θ = θ_accel (use accel reading as initial estimate). Wait 500 ms for the IMU to stabilize. Calibrate gyro bias by averaging 1000 stationary samples and subtracting.

The full code

gx = (gx_raw − gx_bias) * deg_per_lsb; ax_g = ax_raw * g_per_lsb; ay_g = ay_raw * g_per_lsb; theta_accel = atan2(ay_g, sqrt(ax_g² + az_g²)) * RAD_TO_DEG; theta = 0.99 * (theta + gx * dt) + 0.01 * theta_accel;

Tuning in field

Tap the robot: filter should snap back within 0.5 s. If too slow → reduce α (e.g., 0.97). If too noisy → increase α (e.g., 0.995). Trade-off is fundamental: faster response means more accel noise leaks in.

What to notice

  • One line of code, no matrices. Compare to a Kalman filter (10–20 lines of matrix algebra for the same accuracy). Often the complementary filter is "good enough" — and it's what most quadrotors actually use under the hood.
  • Calibrate gyro bias first. Average gyro readings while the IMU is stationary. Subtract that mean from every subsequent gyro sample. Bias drift over temperature is a slower issue — some IMUs (BNO055) self-calibrate; cheaper ones (MPU6050) don't.
  • The accel needs gravity. If the robot is accelerating (translational, not rotational), the accel reading includes that as well. Filter assumes accel ≈ gravity; if not, that period accumulates error.
  • Pick α once, don't try to vary it dynamically without good reason. Adaptive α schemes exist (sense vibration, lower α when high) but are tricky to get right.
  • 2-D vs 3-D: for full attitude (roll + pitch + yaw), you need three filters — one per axis — plus a magnetometer to anchor yaw. Madgwick and Mahony filters extend the complementary idea to quaternions.

Where you'll see this

Real uses

  • Self-balancing robots (Segway, MIP, Boston Dynamics' early Atlas balance) — complementary or Kalman.
  • Quadcopters — Madgwick/Mahony (which are complementary filters in quaternion form).
  • VR headsets — complementary filter with magnetometer to fight yaw drift.
  • Stabilized gimbals — IMU on the camera, complementary filter for tilt, fast inner-loop motor controller.
  • Mobile phone screen rotation — accel + gyro fusion (heavily filtered to be slow and stable).

RULES OF THUMB

  • α = 0.96–0.99 typical.
  • Update rate ≥ 100 Hz for balance applications.
  • Calibrate gyro bias at startup; subtract.
  • Filter accel (10–50 Hz LPF) before atan2.
  • Initialize with accel value, not zero.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Wrong units — gyro in deg/s but θ in radians; mismatch.
  • Forgetting dt — gyro · 1 ≠ gyro · dt.
  • Vibration spikes overwhelming accel — add 50 Hz LPF on accel input.
  • Translational accel at takeoff/braking — accel ≠ gravity for several seconds.
  • α not constant — variable dt creates inconsistent τ; pin dt or recompute α.
