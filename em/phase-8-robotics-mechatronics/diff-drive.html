Differential Drive

Two driven wheels, one passive caster (or nothing — many small robots just drag a bumper). Set wheel speeds independently, and the robot translates and rotates simultaneously. The math is two-line: v = r(ωL+ωR)/2, ω = r(ωR−ωL)/L. Symmetric wheels give straight lines; asymmetric speeds curve the path; opposite-direction wheels spin in place.

Forward kinematics

Each wheel has an angular velocity ω (rad/s) and a radius r. Wheel linear speed is v = ω·r. With two wheels on a fixed axle of length L (track width):

Each wheel's ground speed: v_L = ω_L · r, v_R = ω_R · r Robot center linear speed: v = (v_L + v_R) / 2 = r·(ω_L + ω_R) / 2 Robot angular speed: ω = (v_R − v_L) / L = r·(ω_R − ω_L) / L Sign convention: + means forward, + ω means CCW (left turn) Instantaneous Center of Rotation (ICR) sits on the axle line at distance: R = v / ω from the robot center

Three special cases worth remembering:

  • Straight line: ω_L = ω_R → ω = 0, ICR at infinity.
  • Pivot turn: ω_L = −ω_R → v = 0, ICR at robot center, pure rotation.
  • Bank turn: ω_L = 0 (or ω_R = 0) → ICR at one wheel, robot pivots around that wheel.

Inverse kinematics

Solve for the wheel speeds given the robot's desired linear and angular velocity:

From the forward equations, subtract and add: v_R = v + ω·L/2 v_L = v − ω·L/2 Then divide by wheel radius: ω_L = (v − ω·L/2) / r ω_R = (v + ω·L/2) / r

This is the formula your PID-driven motion controller uses every iteration: high-level path planner says "go 0.5 m/s, turn left at 0.3 rad/s," and the kinematics layer translates that into wheel-speed setpoints.

Worked example — TurtleBot following a circle

The robot

Differential-drive TurtleBot-style platform. Wheel radius r = 0.066 m (66 mm). Track width L = 0.230 m (23 cm). Target: drive a 1 m radius circle at 0.3 m/s linear velocity.

Required angular velocity

For circular motion: ω = v / R = 0.3 / 1.0 = 0.3 rad/s (turn left)

Wheel speeds (inverse kinematics)

ω_L = (v − ω·L/2) / r = (0.3 − 0.3·0.115) / 0.066 = 0.2655 / 0.066 = 4.022 rad/s ω_R = (v + ω·L/2) / r = (0.3 + 0.3·0.115) / 0.066 = 0.3345 / 0.066 = 5.068 rad/s Or in RPM: ω_L = 38.4 RPM, ω_R = 48.4 RPM (multiply rad/s by 60/(2π))

Verify with forward kinematics

v = 0.066 · (4.022 + 5.068)/2 = 0.066 · 4.545 = 0.300 m/s ✓ ω = 0.066 · (5.068 − 4.022)/0.230 = 0.066 · 4.547 = 0.300 rad/s ✓

How the controller uses this

Top-level path planner emits (v, ω) at 20 Hz. Kinematics converts to wheel ω setpoints. Per-wheel PID converts wheel ω error to motor PWM. Encoder feedback closes the loop at 100+ Hz. If real robot ends up curving differently than expected, calibrate wheel radius and track width — these constants are surprisingly hard to measure precisely. ROS calibration tools (e.g., turtlebot3_navigation calibration) systematically tune r and L.

What to notice

  • L is the "track width" — the distance between contact patches, not the chassis width. If your wheels are wider than the chassis, L is larger than visual chassis width.
  • r dominates the speed, L dominates the agility. Bigger wheels = faster top speed for the same motor RPM. Wider track = slower turns for the same wheel-speed difference.
  • Calibration matters more than the math. Real wheels have slip; tires deform under load; encoders drift. Robotics calibration routines spin a known path and adjust effective r and L to match.
  • Caster wheel doesn't affect kinematics in theory — it's passive. In practice, caster wobble adds noise, and a sticky caster on carpet can prevent the robot from turning correctly.
  • Velocity is local, not global. The robot moves in its body frame; integrating (v, ω) to get a world-frame pose is a separate step (odometry). With perfect encoders and no slip, odometry is exact; in reality it accumulates error.

Where you'll see this

Real uses

  • Floor robots — Roomba, Neato, modern vacuum cleaners.
  • Warehouse AGVs — Kiva (Amazon), MiR, Locus.
  • Educational platforms — TurtleBot, Husarion ROSbot.
  • Differential-drive forklifts — Crown, Toyota; same kinematics with larger wheels.
  • Combat / fighting robots — almost all "wedge bots" are differential drive.

RULES OF THUMB

  • Calibrate r and L empirically; nominal values are 5–10 % off.
  • Encoder ticks/rev × wheel-circumference = mm/tick.
  • Pivot turn = ω_L = −ω_R; stresses caster, avoid on carpet.
  • Max ω is set by max wheel-speed: ω_max = 2·v_wheel_max / L.
  • Always set v and ω limits to avoid motor saturation.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Sign convention drift — left/right vs CCW/CW. Pick one and stick to it.
  • Wheel diameter, not radius — datasheet says diameter; formula wants radius.
  • Track width measured wrong — center of tire contact, not outside-to-outside.
  • Slip on slippery floors — odometry drifts; need IMU or external sensor fusion.
  • Symmetric calibration — left and right wheels may have different effective r; calibrate each.
