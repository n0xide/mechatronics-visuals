Forward kinematics — 2-link planar arm

Drive joint angles θ₁ (shoulder) and θ₂ (elbow). The end-effector position (x, y) is computed by a chain of rotations and translations. The traced workspace envelope reveals the reachable set: an annulus between |L₁−L₂| and L₁+L₂. This is the same logic that takes joint encoders on a 6-DOF arm and produces a base→tool transform — just with more matrices.

The math

For a planar arm with joints at θ₁, θ₂ and link lengths L₁, L₂, propagation by chained rotations gives:

x = L₁·cos(θ₁) + L₂·cos(θ₁ + θ₂)

y = L₁·sin(θ₁) + L₂·sin(θ₁ + θ₂)

Equivalently in matrix form, each joint contributes a rotation R(θᵢ) and a translation T(Lᵢ); the base→tool transform is the matrix product. Stretch the same idea to 6 joints + 4×4 homogeneous transforms and you have the forward kinematics of a real industrial arm. The DH-parameters visual generalises this.

What to notice

  • θ₂ is the elbow angle, NOT the absolute angle of link 2. The angle of link 2 in the world frame is θ₁ + θ₂ — joint angles are local to the previous link's frame.
  • Sweep θ₁ alone — the end-effector traces a circle of radius √(L₁² + L₂² + 2L₁L₂cos θ₂), centered on the base. The "elbow snapshot" determines the radius.
  • Workspace is an annulus. Reachable points lie between inner radius |L₁−L₂| (arm folded) and outer radius L₁+L₂ (arm extended). Outside this annulus, FK has no solution because IK has no solution.
  • The base frame is fixed; every link adds a local rotation + a translation along its own length. Walking from base to tool you accumulate transforms: T_base→tool = T_1 · T_2 · … · T_n.
  • Multiple joint configurations reach the same point. Try (θ₁=30°, θ₂=60°) and (θ₁≈80°, θ₂≈−60°) — both can reach the same (x,y). This non-uniqueness is what makes IK a multi-solution problem.
  • For a 6-DOF arm, FK is unchanged in spirit: chain six 4×4 transforms built from DH rows. tf2 in ROS 2 publishes exactly these transforms between joint frames each tick.

MENTAL MODEL

  • Joint angles are local — θᵢ is measured against link i−1, not the world.
  • FK is always solvable — given joint angles you get exactly one tool pose.
  • IK is the hard direction — multiple solutions or none.
  • Singularities show up in IK and J — FK still produces a number even at full extension.
  • Workspace = annulus (planar) or torus-like volume (3-D arm) — sweep all joints to visualise.

PITFALLS

  • Mixing world vs joint angle — link-2 angle in world = θ₁+θ₂, not θ₂. Most beginner FK bugs.
  • Degrees vs radians — JS Math.cos eats radians; servo APIs often speak degrees. Convert at the boundary, once.
  • Forgetting link offsets — real arms have non-zero link "twists" and "offsets"; DH parameters capture all four.
  • Assuming planar covers 3D — the 6-DOF arm needs full 4×4 transforms with rotation matrices, not just trig.
