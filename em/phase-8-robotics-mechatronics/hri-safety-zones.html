HRI Safety Zones & Stop Categories

When robots share space with people, the safety architecture is everything. ISO 10218 + ISO/TS 15066 define the rules: stop categories (Cat 0/1/2), speed-and-separation monitoring (SSM), and power-and-force limiting (PFL). This visual maps the zones, the standards, and the math that ties speed to allowed-distance.

ModeHow it worksExample

Hand-guidingHuman directly moves the robot through teach modeLead-through programming, surgical co-manipulation
Speed and separation monitoring (SSM)Robot slows or stops as human approaches; resumes when human moves awayModern cobots with vision / scanning lidar
Power and force limiting (PFL)Robot continuously limits force + torque so contact is non-injuriousUR cobots, Franka, KUKA iiwa

The ISO 13855 separation-distance formula

S = K · (T_s + T_d) + C S = minimum separation distance (mm) K = approach speed (mm/s) — typically 1600 mm/s for hand reach, 2000 mm/s for arm T_s = robot stopping time (s) T_d = system reaction time (sensor + processing + signal) C = intrusion distance (855 mm typical for hand reaching through opening)

Example: with K = 1600 mm/s, T_s = 200 ms, T_d = 100 ms, C = 855 mm:

S = 1600 × (0.2 + 0.1) + 855 = 480 + 855 = 1335 mm — the minimum distance the safety zone must reach.

Cat 0 emergency stop typically requires PLd or PLe. The safety chain (sensor → safety PLC → motor disconnector) must achieve the same PL end-to-end. The weakest link sets the system PL.

Common pitfalls

  • Single E-stop button on a cable is PLb at best — single point of failure. PLd requires dual-channel monitoring (two independent contacts + cross-checked by safety PLC).
  • "Cobot" + heavy payload ≠ safe — ISO/TS 15066 PFL limits depend on contact location (head most sensitive). A "cobot" carrying 5 kg is no longer collaborative — speed-rated separation required.
  • Sensor blind spots — scanning lidar misses things below knee height. Always supplement with mat or light-curtain at floor level.
  • Estop test frequency — ISO 13849 requires Diagnostic Coverage. Most safety PLCs auto-test estop circuit at boot + periodically (every 24 hours).
  • Mode-switching gaps — when switching between teach-mode and run-mode, ensure no window where safety is briefly disabled.
  • Cycle-time-vs-safety tradeoff — slower robots are safer but less productive. ISM/SSM dynamically slows only when humans are near, recovering throughput.
