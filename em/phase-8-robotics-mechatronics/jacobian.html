The math

For a 2-link planar arm, the Jacobian relating [θ̇₁, θ̇₂]ᵀ to [ẋ, ẏ]ᵀ is:

J = [ −L₁ sin θ₁ − L₂ sin(θ₁+θ₂) −L₂ sin(θ₁+θ₂) ] [ L₁ cos θ₁ + L₂ cos(θ₁+θ₂) L₂ cos(θ₁+θ₂) ] det(J) = L₁ · L₂ · sin(θ₂)

When θ₂ → 0 (full extension) or θ₂ → ±π (full fold), det J → 0 and the matrix is singular. Inverting it produces joint velocities that diverge for any non-radial ẋ. In practice you either avoid the singularity in planning, or use the damped pseudo-inverse: J⁺ = (JᵀJ + λ²I)⁻¹ Jᵀ, which trades accuracy for stability.

What to notice

  • Joint velocities ARE finite when J is non-singular. Pick a tool velocity, read off θ̇₁, θ̇₂. Both are bounded.
  • θ₂ near 0° → singularity. Slide θ₂ to ±5° and watch the required θ̇ explode. Same near ±180°.
  • Direction matters at singularity. Even at θ₂ = 0, motion along the arm's length (radial) is still feasible — the loss of rank kills only tangential motion. The "blow-up" is direction-dependent.
  • The Jacobian is also the force map. By duality, joint torques τ = Jᵀ · F_tool. Near singularity, tiny joint torques produce huge tool forces in the lost direction — a benefit for impedance control but a hazard for safety.
  • Condition number κ(J) measures "near-singularity." Large κ = bad. Most planners throttle Cartesian speed by 1/κ near the boundary.
  • For a 6-DOF arm, J is 6×6. Wrist singularities (alignment of two wrist axes) and elbow singularities (full extension) are common. MoveIt 2 detects them via Jacobian singular-value decomposition.

Cheat sheet

JACOBIAN ESSENTIALS

Velocity
ẋ = J(θ) · θ̇
Inverse
θ̇ = J⁻¹ · ẋ (square, full rank)
Pseudo-inverse
θ̇ = J⁺ · ẋ (rect., min-norm)
Damped
θ̇ = (JᵀJ + λ²I)⁻¹ Jᵀ · ẋ
Static force
τ = Jᵀ · F
Determinant (2-link)
det J = L₁ L₂ sin θ₂

SINGULARITY TYPES (6-DOF ARM)

  • Boundary singularity — arm fully extended; lost direction is radial outward.
  • Interior singularity — wrist axes align (joints 4 and 6 colinear); lost direction is rotation about that axis.
  • Shoulder singularity — wrist on top of shoulder axis; joint 1 spins freely.
  • Manipulability index — w = √det(JJᵀ) → 0 at singularity.

PITFALLS

  • Computing J⁻¹ near singularity — numerically explodes. Always use damped pseudo-inverse for Cartesian control.
  • Ignoring direction — at a singularity some directions are still reachable. Don't blanket-stop.
  • Confusing analytical vs geometric J — geometric uses ω; analytical uses Euler-angle rates (problems with own gimbal lock).
  • Forgetting τ = JᵀF — joint torques to hold a payload depend on pose. Far from base = high shoulder torque.
