Motion profiles — trapezoidal vs S-curve

A point-to-point move with limits on velocity, acceleration, and jerk. Trapezoidal honours v_max and a_max only — jerk is impulsive at each corner (infinite for one sample). S-curve respects a jerk ceiling: acceleration ramps in and out of the cruise. Vibration in a stiff servo system tracks jerk closely, not acceleration — switching to S-curve is usually a free upgrade in settling quality.

How each profile is computed

Trapezoidal: the controller commands a = +a_max until velocity reaches v_max (or until the midpoint, whichever first — "triangular" if the move is short), holds at v_max, then ramps with a = −a_max to a stop. Three segments. Position is parabolic on the ramps, linear in the middle. Jerk is impulsive (Dirac spikes at the three corners), which is what excites mechanical resonances.

S-curve: seven segments. Jerk steps between +j_max, 0, −j_max, 0, +j_max, 0, −j_max. Acceleration is piecewise linear (smooth ramps), velocity is piecewise quadratic (no kinks), position is piecewise cubic. The move takes a bit longer but the servo settles much faster — net throughput is often higher.

What to notice

  • Acceleration corners on trapezoidal cause the plant to overshoot. Watch the green trace ring after each corner.
  • S-curve plant response settles cleaner with the same v_max and a_max — at the cost of slightly longer total time.
  • Shrink the distance below the "triangular" threshold (where v_max never reached): both profiles degrade gracefully into shorter shapes.
  • Increase ζ (damping) on the plant and the difference shrinks — both profiles settle quickly. Stiff, lightly-damped servos benefit most from S-curve.
  • Jerk limit is the new bottleneck on S-curve. Raise j_max and the profile starts to look trapezoidal again. Lower it and the move stretches in time.
  • For ultra-smooth moves (vision-guided, scanning): drop accel and jerk hard; the plant response becomes nearly identical to the command.

Cheat sheet

TRAPEZOIDAL (TRIANGULAR FALLBACK)

Cruise reached?
Δp > v_max² / a_max
Ramp time
t_a = v_max / a_max
Cruise time
t_c = Δp/v_max − v_max/a_max
Total time
T = t_a + t_c + t_a
If triangular
T = 2·√(Δp/a_max)

S-CURVE TIMING

Jerk time
t_j = a_max / j_max
Constant-accel time
t_a = v_max/a_max − t_j
Cruise time
t_c = Δp/v_max − v_max/a_max − t_j
Total time
T = 4 t_j + 2 t_a + t_c

PICK A PROFILE

  • Trapezoidal — quick, simple. OK when servos are well-damped or motion is slow.
  • S-curve — default for industrial / precision servos. Smoothness > extra ~10% move time.
  • Quintic / cubic spline — for chained waypoints with C¹ or C² continuity.
  • Input shaping (ZV, ZVD) — alternative: filter the command at resonance frequencies. Use when you can't tune jerk.
