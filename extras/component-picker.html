SMD chip-resistor numbering (printed code, no colors)

Chip resistors don't have color bands — they have a 3- or 4-digit code printed on top, or an EIA-96 alphanumeric on tiny parts.

What to watch for

  • Gold vs orange multiplier band — gold is ×0.1 (decimal place), orange is ×10³. On a small part with worn paint, easy to mistake. Verify with a meter.
  • Red as 1st digit vs red as tolerance — on a 4-band part, red is "2" if it's the leftmost band; on a 5-band, red is also "2" — but trailing red is ±2 % tolerance. Read direction matters: bands cluster toward one end.
  • Power rating isn't in the code — resistor power is set by physical size (0402 ≈ 1/16 W, 0603 ≈ 1/10 W, 0805 ≈ 1/8 W, 1206 ≈ 1/4 W, 2512 ≈ 1 W). Compute I² · R before picking the package.
  • Tempco band (6-band) — brown=100 ppm/°C, red=50, yellow=25, orange=15, blue=10. Matters for precision dividers in instrumentation; ignored elsewhere.

Class I — NP0 / C0G

  • ±30 ppm/°C drift — flat with temperature
  • No DC-bias derating
  • Tight tolerance (±5 % or better)
  • Limited capacitance (up to ~10 nF in 0805)
  • Use for: oscillator timing, anti-alias filters, charge-pump caps, RF tank circuits

Class II — X7R / X5R

  • ±15 % over rated temperature range
  • DC-bias derating: 100 nF rated 10 V can lose 40–60 % at 10 V DC
  • Aging: small capacitance loss over time (1–3 %/decade-hour)
  • Affordable in larger values (1–100 µF easily in 0805)
  • Use for: decoupling, bulk filtering, LDO output, general digital

Tantalum / electrolytic markings

Tantalum and aluminum-electrolytic caps usually print the value directly in µF along with voltage rating. Voltage derating is required:

TypeMarking styleVoltage derating rulePolarity
Aluminum electrolytic10µF 25V on the canUse ≤ 80 % rated; reliability halves per 10 °C above 85 °CMarked stripe = negative
Solid tantalum106 25V = 10 µF / 25 VUse ≤ 50 % rated; tantalum failures are shorts that catch fireMarked stripe / + symbol = positive
Polymer (solid Al / Ta)Same as wet, but ESR much lower≤ 80 % rated; less catastrophic failuresAs above

What to watch for

  • Tantalum derating is mandatory — a 10 V solid tantalum cap used at 9 V in a 5 V circuit will fail short, smoke, and possibly ignite. Use 25 V tantalum on a 5 V rail (≤ 50 % rated). Polymer tantalum is more forgiving but still derate.
  • MLCC DC-bias derating — X5R 4.7 µF rated 10 V can be only 1.5 µF at 5 V DC. Datasheets have a curve; always check for bypass caps near power devices.
  • NP0 above 10 nF is rare — if you need 100 nF NP0 you'll pay 10× the price of X7R; for high-Q filtering use polypropylene film instead.
  • Aluminum electrolytic ESR + temp — bulk caps in switching supplies have specified ESR at 100 kHz / 20 °C. ESR can rise 3–5× at −20 °C, causing ripple voltage to spike. Use polymer or hybrid caps in cold-environment designs.

Wire selection rules

  • Chassis vs power-transmission — chassis assumes short runs in open air (a chassis wire bundle), power assumes a long bundled run with derating. Real applications fall between; use power numbers for safety margin.
  • Skin effect — at high frequency, current flows only in the outer skin. Above ~10 kHz, large solid wire has effective AWG much smaller than its nominal. Use Litz wire (many tiny insulated strands) for high-frequency power.
  • Voltage drop limit — even if a wire passes ampacity, V drop = I · R · L can exceed your budget. Rule of thumb: keep DC supply drops below 2–3 % of bus voltage.
  • Aluminum is 1.6× more resistive than copper for the same gauge — and won't tolerate the same connector design without antioxidant compound. Stick to copper unless cost forces you to Al for very large bus.
  • Stranded vs solid — solid is cheaper, easier to terminate, better for breadboards. Stranded flexes (silicone-jacketed stranded for moving cables), terminates with ferrules or crimped lugs. Same gauge ≈ same ampacity.

Reading rules

  • Same code, different parts — markings are not unique across manufacturers. 1A is BC847A from NXP but could be a different code on a knockoff. Cross-reference against the datasheet and the package style (SOT-23 vs SOT-323 vs SOT-89).
  • Suffix letter = bin — BC847A/B/C differ only in β grade. The bin letter is part of the marking on bigger packages.
  • SOT-23 pinout convention — pin 1 is bottom-left looking at the marking; pin 2 is bottom-right; pin 3 is top. For BJTs: 1=Base, 2=Emitter, 3=Collector. For MOSFETs: 1=Gate, 2=Source, 3=Drain.
  • Logic-level vs standard MOSFET — 2N7002 (standard) starts conducting at ~2.5 Vgs but barely on at 5 V; AO3400 (logic-level) is fully on at 4.5 Vgs. Use logic-level when driving from a 3.3 V or 5 V GPIO.

NTC thermistor R(T) curve

Negative temperature coefficient (NTC): R drops as T rises. Most embedded thermistors are characterized by R₂₅ and a β (Beta) value over 25–85 °C. The Beta equation gives R at any T from R₂₅.

Beta equation + the better Steinhart-Hart

Two equations, ascending in accuracy.

EquationFormAccuracy
Beta (B)1/T = 1/T₀ + (1/β)·ln(R/R₀)±1 °C over 0–70 °C if β rated for that range
Steinhart-Hart1/T = A + B·ln(R) + C·(ln R)³±0.05 °C over full range; need 3-point calibration

For most embedded applications (battery temp, room thermostat, motor temp), Beta with the manufacturer's published value is good enough. For battery-fuel-gauge or precision instrumentation, calibrate at 3 points and solve Steinhart-Hart.

Voltage-divider reading + linearization

NTC thermistors are usually measured with a series-divider (R_pullup // ADC // NTC). The output voltage is non-linear in T — the divider's non-linearity actually partially cancels the thermistor's non-linearity over a narrow range, but only with a well-chosen R_pullup.

  • R_pullup = R₂₅ for symmetric response near 25 °C. Maximum dV/dT happens right around that temperature.
  • R_pullup < R₂₅ shifts the linear region toward higher temperatures.
  • R_pullup > R₂₅ shifts toward lower temperatures.
  • Self-heating matters at high R_pullup or low R₂₅: I_NTC · V_NTC · θ_th ≈ ΔT. Keep I_NTC under 100 µA for tight measurements. Use the divider-explorer in EE 1 to size R_pullup.
