Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W — RP2350 dual Cortex-M33 / 150 MHz

RP2350 dual-core Cortex-M33 @ 150 MHz (or RISC-V Hazard3) 520 KB SRAM + 4 MB QSPI flash 4 ADC channels, 12-bit, 0–3.3 V (noisy on Pico, see pitfalls) 2× UART, 2× SPI, 2× I2C on flexible GPIO mux 3× PIO blocks (12 SMs total) — bit-bang anything USB 1.1 device + host (host needs external Vbus switch) 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi 4 + BT 5.2 (Infineon CYW43439)

Decoupling, reset & clock

  • Decoupling: On the bare RP2350 chip, eight 100 nF caps — one per VDD pin (IOVDD, DVDD, USB_VDD, ADC_AVDD, VREG_VIN). The Pico 2 W module already has these. If you build your own RP2350 board, add a 1–10 µF bulk cap and a 100 nF on every supply pin under 5 mm.
  • ADC_AVDD deserves a ferrite-bead + 100 nF filter from 3V3 to keep digital noise out. Pico's design ties ADC_AVDD directly to the buck-converter rail — explains the well-known noise floor (see pitfalls).
  • RUN (pin 30) is the reset input — active low with internal pull-up. Optional: 1 µF cap to GND for power-up delay + RC. Tie to a button to GND for hard reset.
  • BOOTSEL button shorts QSPI_SS to GND on the Pico module; on a custom board, expose a button for flashing via USB mass-storage mode.
  • Clock: the RP2350 has an internal ROSC, but every production board uses a 12 MHz crystal on XIN/XOUT (Pico module: 12 MHz, ±50 ppm). Load caps ≈ 15 pF each, matched to crystal CL.

Worked example — Pico 2 W with UART + I2C + SPI + ADC

Project: a portable data-logger with a OLED screen (I2C), GPS module (UART), SD card (SPI), and a battery-voltage divider (ADC). Allocate pins to avoid mux conflicts; verify with the table below.

UART0 TX = GP0 / RX = GP1 → to GPS module RX/TX (cross over)

I2C0 SDA = GP4 / SCL = GP5 → OLED (SSD1306 @ 0x3C)

SPI0 CS=GP17, SCK=GP18, MOSI=GP19, MISO=GP16 → SD card

ADC0 = GP26 → battery voltage / 2 (via 100k:100k divider)

3V3 + GND + GP25 (onboard LED)

That uses 8 GPIOs of 30 available. Check the peripheral boxes above to highlight these pins on the pinout.

Common pitfalls

  • ADC noise — Pico/Pico 2 wires ADC_AVDD directly to the SMPS rail. RMS noise ≈ 5 LSB on 12 bits; for precision (12 effective bits) you must enable PSM (power-save mode) or bypass the buck with a low-noise LDO into 3V3.
  • ADC reference is ~3.3 V system rail — drifts with USB voltage. For ratiometric sensors (voltage dividers) this is fine. For absolute measurements use an external 3.000 V reference into ADC_VREF (pin 35).
  • QSPI pins are tied up — GPIO 12–17 are reused on certain Pico variants. Don't assume those pins are free without checking the schematic.
  • 5 V tolerance: none. GPIO max = 3.6 V. Use a level shifter (TXS0108E / BSS138 for slow signals) for any 5 V peripheral. Direct 5 V on a GPIO will latch up the RP2350.
  • USB pin pair — D+/D- are dedicated pins on the Pico module; on the bare RP2350 they're GP24/GP25 in some packages but should be left for USB only.
  • UART vs USB serial — picotool and MicroPython both expose stdio over USB CDC. UART0 (GP0/GP1) is a separate physical UART; both can run simultaneously.

STM32G474RET6 — Cortex-M4F @ 170 MHz (LQFP-64)

STM32G4 Cortex-M4F @ 170 MHz + FPU + DSP 512 KB flash, 128 KB SRAM HRTIM high-res 184 ps PWM (motor control flagship) 5× ADC 12-bit @ 4 Msps + 7× opamp + 7× comparator FDCAN (CAN-FD), 3× I2C, 4× SPI, 5× USART, 1× USB FS VDDA separate analog supply pin (critical filter)

Decoupling, reset & clock

  • Decoupling: per ST AN4206, place one 100 nF on every VDD pin (LQFP-64 has VDD1..VDD4 + VDDA). All within 5 mm with a short return. Add one 4.7 µF bulk cap close to the regulator output, and a 100 nF + 1 µF combo on VDDA. VBAT needs 100 nF + a tied or RTC-battery network.
  • VDDA = analog reference. Connect to VDD through a ferrite bead (BLM18 series, 600 Ω @ 100 MHz) for noise isolation. VREF+ defaults to VDDA; for precision use an external 3.0 V buffered reference (REF3030).
  • NRST (pin 7): active low, internal 40 kΩ pull-up. Datasheet recommends a 100 nF cap to GND to filter glitches. Optional external pull-up (10 k) plus reset button to GND.
  • BOOT0 (pin 60 on LQFP-64): selects boot region. BOOT0=0 → flash, BOOT0=1 → system memory (DFU bootloader). Wire 10 kΩ pull-down + button to 3.3 V for DFU access, or a 2-pin jumper.
  • Clock: 8/16/24/25 MHz HSE crystal on OSC_IN / OSC_OUT (pins 5/6). Load caps ≈ 18–22 pF for typical 18 pF CL crystals; tune for ±20 ppm. Internal HSI16 (16 MHz ±1 %) works without crystal but is too rough for USB or CAN-FD.
  • SWD debug: PA13 = SWDIO, PA14 = SWCLK. Don't repurpose these unless you have a backup debug method — see pitfalls.

Common pitfalls

  • PA13 / PA14 are SWD — remapping them to GPIO during early-init bricks debug. If you must use them, expose a "factory reset" pad on the board and re-enable SWD via NRST + bootloader.
  • PB4 / PB3 = NJTRST / JTDO — JTAG remnants. On boot they're pulled up for JTAG; if you use them as GPIO outputs, drive carefully or disable JTAG in MCU options bytes.
  • USB DM/DP are on PA11/PA12 — fixed, no remap. CAN-FD shares PD0/PD1 or PB8/PB9; pick at sysclk init.
  • VBAT must be powered or tied to VDD — floating VBAT is undefined; tie to VDD if you don't use the RTC backup domain.
  • 5 V tolerance: most GPIOs are 5 V-tolerant when configured as inputs (marked "FT" in the datasheet). Analog pins and a few others (PC14/15, PF0/1) are not. Always check the Table 14 pin list — wrong assumption blows the GPIO.
  • OPAMP/DAC pin sharing — the G4 has 7 opamps and 4 DACs on dedicated pins. Don't allocate those to GPIO if you might use the analog block; pins like PA1/PA2/PA3 are PGA-capable.

ESP32-S3 DevKitC — dual Xtensa LX7 @ 240 MHz

ESP32-S3 dual Xtensa LX7 @ 240 MHz + AI vector ext 512 KB SRAM + 8 MB PSRAM (N8R8 variant) Wi-Fi 4 2.4 GHz + BLE 5 USB-OTG + native USB-Serial-JTAG on GPIO 19/20 45 GPIOs, 2× 12-bit SAR ADC (20 channels), LCD/CAM/SDMMC peripherals 3× UART, 4× SPI, 2× I2C, 2× I2S, RMT (IR), MCPWM, TWAI (CAN 2.0)

Decoupling, reset & clock

  • Decoupling: the ESP32-S3 module already has 100 nF on every VDD pad plus a 10 µF bulk cap. On your motherboard, put another 10 µF + 100 nF within 5 mm of the module's 3V3 pin — Wi-Fi TX peaks pull 400 mA bursts. Undersized supply → brown-out resets at TX.
  • EN / nRESET: active high enable (with internal pull-up on the module). The DevKitC ties EN through 10 k to VDD and a 100 nF cap to GND for power-up delay. Add a reset button to GND if missing on your board.
  • Clock: 40 MHz crystal on XTAL_P / XTAL_N — already on the module. RTC sleep can use a 32.768 kHz watch crystal on dedicated pins.
  • USB-OTG vs USB-Serial-JTAG: GPIO 19 = D-, GPIO 20 = D+. The USB-Serial-JTAG block is built-in and used for flashing/debug via the on-chip ROM. No external USB-to-UART chip required (the DevKitC still ships one on UART0 as backup).
  • Strapping pins (sampled at reset): GPIO 0, GPIO 3, GPIO 45, GPIO 46. They set boot mode, JTAG vs USB, ROM message logging, and flash voltage. Never drive them externally before boot completes.

Common pitfalls

  • Strapping pins are TIME BOMBS — GPIO 0 (pulled low → bootloader, high → run). GPIO 45/46 set flash voltage and ROM logging. If your sensor pulls GPIO 0 low at reset, the chip won't run. Solution: choose any pin except 0, 3, 45, 46 for signals that may be asserted at power-on; if you must, gate with a buffer that releases after reset.
  • USB-JTAG vs USB-CDC arbitration — both share GPIO 19/20. If you want native USB serial, configure USB-OTG mode; the USB-Serial-JTAG block is then unavailable. The DevKitC sidesteps this with a separate UART-to-USB chip on the second connector.
  • ADC2 channels are blocked while Wi-Fi is on — ADC2 (GPIO 11–20 channels) shares hardware with the Wi-Fi PHY. Use ADC1 (GPIO 1–10) if you sample during Wi-Fi TX/RX.
  • SPI flash + PSRAM occupy SPI0/SPI1 — GPIO 26–32 area is taken by internal flash/PSRAM on most modules. Don't route signals to those pins; the symbols are not exposed on DevKitC headers either.
  • 5 V tolerance: none — same as RP2350. 3.3 V max. Many DevKitC carrier boards include a 5 V pin (VIN) — that's the input rail, not a GPIO level.
  • GPIO 35–37 (S3 module: not present) — referenced in older ESP32 docs but absent on S3 due to flash repurposing. Pin numbering shifted from classic ESP32 to S3; always check the S3-specific datasheet.

Teensy 4.1 — i.MX RT1062, Cortex-M7 @ 600 MHz

i.MX RT1062 Cortex-M7 @ 600 MHz (single + double precision FPU) 1 MB tightly-coupled RAM + 8 MB external PSRAM optional 8 MB QSPI flash on-board, expandable to 16 MB 55 GPIOs on the board (more on chip), 18 PWM channels 8× UART, 3× SPI, 3× I2C hardware peripherals 3× CAN (2× CAN 2.0B + 1× CAN-FD), USB host + device, 480 MHz HS 2× I2S + S/PDIF, 100 Mbit ethernet PHY pads, SDIO for microSD

Decoupling, reset & clock

  • Decoupling: the Teensy 4.1 board is fully decoupled — every i.MX VDD has a 100 nF + a shared 22 µF bulk. As a user you only need to bypass your peripheral chips. If you wire anything that pulls >100 mA, add a local 10 µF + 100 nF at that chip's supply.
  • Reset: a momentary button on the board grounds the program-mode pad ("PROG" line) which triggers a USB bootloader entry. Software reset via SCB_AIRCR = 0x05FA0004 from Teensyduino.
  • Clock: 24 MHz crystal on-board feeds the PLL up to 600 MHz. RTC uses a separate 32.768 kHz crystal (already populated). VBAT pad accepts a 3 V CR2032 for backup.
  • USB: the micro-USB jack on top is USB 2.0 device. The board also has D+/D- pads on the underside for USB host mode (used in audio shields, MIDI controllers). Add a 5 V power switch (TPS2065) if you supply VBus to a device.
  • Boot: no BOOT pin to set — bootloader is in ROM, triggered by the PROG button. To re-flash, press the button while connected over USB; Teensyduino picks up the bootloader and writes flash.

Common pitfalls

  • Teensy pins are 3.3 V, NOT 5 V tolerant — common newbie mistake on a board the size of an Arduino Nano. Putting 5 V on a digital input damages the i.MX permanently. Use level shifters.
  • Underside pads matter — five pins (54–57) and the SD card / Ethernet / USB-host pads are only accessible on the bottom of the board. If you mount on a header strip, you lose access to those without a flying lead.
  • PaulStoffregen's pin tables are authoritative — the i.MX datasheet labels pins by their iMX number; the Teensy 4.1 schematic re-numbers them. Always cross-reference Paul's pinout card (built into Teensyduino).
  • Hardware Serial vs USB Serial — Serial is the USB virtual port (always on). Serial1..Serial8 are the hardware UARTs on physical pins. Don't confuse the two.
  • CAN-FD transceiver still required — the Teensy has the FlexCAN controller, but you still need a TJA1042 / MCP2562FD / SN65HVD230 between the CAN pins and the differential CAN_H/L bus.
  • Ethernet is PHY-less — Teensy 4.1 brings out the RMII pads for a 100 Mbit ethernet PHY (LAN8720) plus magnetics + jack. Without those external parts, no Ethernet — there's no PHY on the Teensy itself.

Pick a chip in 60 seconds

  • You need Wi-Fi and budget is < $10 → ESP32-S3.
  • You need precision motor control or analog → STM32G474 (HRTIM + opamps).
  • You need bit-bang flexibility / DIY peripherals → Pico 2 W (PIO is the killer feature).
  • You need raw horsepower / DSP / multi-bus → Teensy 4.1.
  • You need 5 V GPIO tolerance for legacy logic → STM32G474 (most GPIOs are FT).
  • You need CAN-FD → STM32G474 or Teensy 4.1.

Decoupling memory hooks

  • 100 nF per VDD pin, within 5 mm — always.
  • 1–10 µF bulk near the regulator — once per board.
  • VDDA / ADC_AVDD gets a ferrite + 100 nF — analog isolation.
  • Big inductive loads (motors, relays) — 100 µF electrolytic at the load, not at the MCU.

Pitfall headlines

  • ESP32-S3 strap pins 0/3/45/46 — never drive at reset.
  • STM32 SWD = PA13/PA14 — repurpose carefully.
  • Pico ADC noise — shared rail with SMPS.
  • Teensy is 3.3 V only — looks like an Arduino, isn't.
  • BOOT0 + NRST — STM32 needs both right to enter DFU.

Reset RC sizing

  • R = 10 kΩ, C = 100 nF → τ = 1 ms typical.
  • R = 10 kΩ, C = 1 µF → τ = 10 ms (slow supplies).
  • Always check the MCU's t_RSTL spec (typ 20 µs minimum).

Crystal load caps

  • C_L per side = 2 × (CL_crystal − C_stray)
  • Typical CL = 18 pF, C_stray ≈ 3 pF → C_L = 30 pF each
  • NEVER copy load caps from a different crystal — datasheet specific.
