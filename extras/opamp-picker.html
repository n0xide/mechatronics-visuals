Worked example — photodiode TIA selection

Selecting an op-amp for a 100 kHz photodiode transimpedance amplifier (TIA)

Photodiode is reverse-biased; its photocurrent Ipd = 0–10 µA; want Vout = 0–2 V across 0–100 kHz bandwidth. Rf = 200 kΩ. Photodiode capacitance Cpd = 50 pF. Stable TIA requires GBW » signal bandwidth (industry rule: 100×), low input bias (else Vos-like errors), and very low input current noise (else you build a current-noise amplifier).

1. GBW required
100× signal BW = 100× 100 kHz = 10 MHz minimum
2. Stability pole
fz = 1/(2π·Rf·Cpd) = 1/(2π · 200k · 50pF) = 15.9 kHz. Need fp from Cf at √(fz·GBW); GBW = 10 MHz ⇒ Cf ≈ 0.4 pF.
3. Input bias
10 µA signal » bias error. Want Iib < 1 nA — rules out BJT-input (LM358 has 250 nA, fatal here). JFET or CMOS required.
4. Voltage offset
Vos = 200 kΩ · (DC-bias-current-difference) + Vos,intrinsic. Want <5 mV total → Vos,intrinsic < 5 mV is fine. (Bigger concern is bias-current symmetry.)
5. Noise
Total output noise = en(1+Rf/Rs) + in·Rf. JFET-input has tiny in — the right pick for Rf=200k.

⇒ Filter: GBW ≥ 10 MHz, Iib ≤ 1 nA, family = JFET-input. Candidates: OPA1656 (premium, $4–6), OPA350 (CMOS RRIO, $2–3), LMC662 (vintage CMOS, $2). Click the "Photodiode TIA" card above to pre-apply these filters.

Common pitfalls

  • LM358 has crossover distortion — the class-AB output stage handles off near 0.6 V. Output settles to dead-zone around the crossover point on low-side single-supply use. Symptom: a sine wave with a flat spot every cycle. Fix: pull-down resistor (10 kΩ to VEE) on the output or use AD8541/MCP6001.
  • RRIO vs RRI vs RRO — RRIO means rail-to-rail input and output. RRO alone is common (output swings to within 50–200 mV of rails); RRI alone is rare. Common cap: input can swing from VEE-0.3 V (BJT-input "rail-to-rail" via PNP-input stage) but not over VCC-2 V. Read the input-CM-range curve, not the marketing line.
  • Iib × Rsource » Vos — bias-current-induced voltage offset can dwarf intrinsic Vos. With LM358 (250 nA typ Iib), a 100 kΩ source impedance creates 25 mV of "offset" before you've even measured Vos. Match impedance on V+ and V− pins to cancel bias-current effects (works only when Iib+ ≈ Iib−, which is true for BJT-input but NOT for JFET-input).
  • Don't use an op-amp as a comparator — op-amps' output saturates rather than recovers cleanly when their inputs differ by > the input-stage range. Slew-to-rail can be a 100× slower than a real comparator. Use LM393, TLV3201, or LMV7271.
  • JFET-input has high in, low en; BJT-input has low in, low en but high Iib; CMOS has zero Iib but moderate-to-high en. For high source impedance (> 10 kΩ), JFET wins. For low source impedance (< 1 kΩ), BJT wins. The crossover impedance Rcrit ≈ en/in; for LT1028 (BJT) it's ≈ 30 kΩ, above which JFET noise is lower.
  • GBW is for unity-gain-stable parts only — "decompensated" op-amps (LT1028, OPA637) have higher GBW but require G ≥ 5 for stability. Reading the datasheet's minimum stable gain is essential.
  • Vos drift dwarfs initial Vos in real installations — OP07 has 75 µV initial offset and 1.3 µV/°C drift → 130 µV over a 100 °C range. Use auto-zero parts (AD8551 has 0.005 µV/°C) for thermal-stable instrumentation.
