Schematic Symbol Reference

The visual vocabulary of electronics — IEEE/ANSI vs IEC (European) variants. Knowing the symbols speeds up datasheet reading, schematic capture, and code review of someone else's PCB. This card is a cheat-sheet for every common passive, active, and source element you'll see in EE schematics + a few annotations on the conventions different EDA tools use.

IEEE / ANSI: USA + most modern EDA (KiCad, Altium default) IEC 60617: European, simpler shapes (rectangular resistor) JIS: Japanese, mostly aligned with IEC

Passive components

Resistor (IEEE zigzag)
USA convention. Annotation: R1, 4.7k
Resistor (IEC rectangle)
European. Same component.
Capacitor (non-polar)
Two parallel lines. C1, 100nF
Capacitor (polarized)
Curved = negative plate; + marked. Electrolytic, tantalum.
Inductor (air core)
Loops. L1, 10µH
Inductor (iron / ferrite core)
Bar = core. Power-supply inductor.
Variable capacitor (trimmer)
Arrow or screw through symbol
Potentiometer (3-terminal)
Wiper arrow. R_top, R_bottom, R_wiper.
Diode (general)
Triangle + bar. Anode → cathode.
Zener diode
Bent bar = "Z" shape on cathode
LED
Diode with arrows out (emitting light)
Photodiode
Diode with arrows in (absorbing light)

Active components — transistors

NPN BJT
Arrow OUT of emitter. Base left, collector top.
PNP BJT
Arrow IN to base. "Pointing iN Permits".
N-channel MOSFET (enhancement)
3 separate lines (channel off at zero Vgs); arrow in = N-channel
P-channel MOSFET (enhancement)
Arrow out = P-channel
Op-amp
Triangle. − above + (or vice versa). V+/V− power.

Power + ground symbols

Earth / Chassis ground
"GND". Three decreasing lines.
Signal ground / 0 V
Triangle. Often distinguished from chassis.
DC source
Battery or constant voltage. Polarity matters.
AC source
Sine inside circle. Mains, AC bench supply.
Current source
Arrow inside circle. Constant I.
Power rail (Vcc, +5V, +3.3V)
Vertical line with rail name. Convention: top of schematic.

Switches + connectors

SPST switch
Single Pole Single Throw. Open in this view.
SPDT switch
Single Pole Double Throw. Selector.
Relay (coil + contact)
Box = coil. Adjacent contact pair switches.
Connector (2-pin)
Dots = pins. Header, terminal block.
Antenna
Triangle out. RF.
Wire junction (4-way)
DOT means connected. No dot = wires cross but don't connect.

Reading vs drawing schematics — best practices

  • Power flows top-to-bottom: Vcc rail at top, GND rail at bottom. Signal flows left-to-right: inputs left, outputs right.
  • Use net labels for any wire crossing a sheet boundary or going more than 5cm. A short wire is easier to read than a net label.
  • Crystal load caps go RIGHT AT the crystal pins on the schematic AND in PCB layout. Don't separate them.
  • Each IC gets a decoupling cap symbol drawn RIGHT NEXT to its power pin, even if the cap will be physically routed via the plane.
  • Reference designators are sequential across the entire schematic — don't restart R1 on every sheet.
  • Annotate the value AND the package on each part: R1 4.7kΩ 0805 not just R1.
