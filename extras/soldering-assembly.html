Soldering & PCB Assembly

Soldering is a craft, not a science — but it has very specific physics underneath it (alloys, fluxes, surface tension, intermetallic growth, thermal mass). This reference covers the gear, the technique, the defects you'll see, and the reflow profile every SMD component asks for. Designed for hobbyists going from breadboard to first-board and engineers reviewing other people's joints.

Through-hole technique

The 5-second cycle

  1. 0:00 — Tin the iron tip (small bead of solder + flux). Wipe on damp sponge or brass.
  2. 0:01 — Touch the tip to both the pad and the lead simultaneously, heating both for ~1.5 s.
  3. 0:02 — Feed solder to the joint (not the tip). Wait until it flows into the joint and forms a smooth concave cone.
  4. 0:04 — Remove solder, then remove iron. Don't move the joint for 2 s while it cools.
  5. 0:06 — Inspect. Should look smooth, shiny, concave. If matte, dull, or cracked, it's a cold joint — reheat with fresh flux.

Common mistake: applying solder to the iron first then transferring to the joint. The solder oxidizes; the joint stays cold. Always heat the joint, then add solder.

SMD soldering — hand vs reflow

Hand soldering (drag + paste)

  • 0805 + bigger: pad-by-pad with iron + 0.5 mm solder. Tack one pad, position part, solder opposite pad, then finalize.
  • 0603 / 0402: tweezers + fine tip. Or paste + hot air.
  • SOIC / TSSOP: drag soldering — solder one corner, position, solder opposite corner, then drag solder across all pins with flux.
  • QFN / DFN: NEED hot air or reflow oven. Hand soldering thermal pad on bottom is impossible.
  • BGA: only with reflow + paste stencil. Cannot hand-solder.

Reflow (paste + oven + stencil)

  • Apply paste through stencil onto pads.
  • Place components with tweezers or pick-and-place.
  • Run reflow profile (preheat → soak → reflow → cool).
  • Best for: many SMD parts, fine-pitch (0.4 mm QFN), BGA.
  • Equipment: T-962 toaster oven ($150) or modified toaster oven + thermocouple.
  • JLCPCB / Macrofab can assemble for you — usually cheaper than buying parts + your time.

Reflow profile (SAC305 lead-free, typical)

Total: 4–5 minutes from start to room temp. Going too hot (>270 °C peak) damages MLCC capacitors (cracks under thermal shock) and silicon chips (Tj exceeded).

Defects — visual gallery

Cold joint: gray, matte surface, often cracked or "lumpy". Cause: insufficient heat or contaminated pad. Fix: reheat with flux until solder reflows.
Solder bridge: solder spans two adjacent pads. Cause: too much solder or fine-pitch package without flux. Fix: wick + flux. For QFN, drag a fresh solder bead through the bridge with the iron tip.
Tombstoning: SMD chip stands up on one end (looks like a tombstone). Cause: uneven solder paste, uneven heat. Fix: re-reflow with equal paste on both pads.
Insufficient wetting: solder beads up instead of spreading. Cause: oxidized pad/lead, weak flux. Fix: scrub pad with abrasive (or solder wick to remove oxide), apply fresh flux.
Solder ball: spherical bead of solder near a joint (not part of it). Cause: solder paste splatter at reflow. Mostly cosmetic but can short under vibration. Fix: clean with IPA.
Head-in-pillow (BGA only): ball connects to chip but not to PCB (or vice versa). Caused by warpage or under-temperature. Requires X-ray to detect. Fix: rework with full reflow.
Whisker: long thin filament of pure tin growing from a joint over months/years. Lead-free only. Can short adjacent pads. No reliable fix; just inspect critical assemblies.
Dewetting: solder spreads, then retracts. Cause: contaminated pad surface (typically gold-flash plating dissolving into solder). Fix: re-clean pad, fresh paste.
Solder bridge under thermal pad: invisible from above. QFN/BGA only. Cause: too much paste. Diagnose: open-circuit between pad and adjacent pin, or short to ground. Fix: rework — remove chip, clean, re-apply paste.

Inspection — what to look for

  • Shiny + concave = good. Smooth meniscus from pad to lead.
  • Matte + grey = cold joint. Reheat with flux.
  • Round + ball-shaped = poor wetting. Pad is contaminated or oxidized.
  • Pad lifted = excessive heat ripped the trace. Pad is destroyed — rework with wire jumper or DPS pad.
  • Crack between joint + pad = mechanical stress. Reflow it.
  • Component missing = obvious. Re-stuff.
  • Component crooked = uneven paste or shifted during reflow. Re-stuff if functional impact.

Worked example — assembling a Pico W cyberdeck PCB at home

Setup

Mixed-tech board: SMD (0805 + SOIC + QFN-32), 0.4-mm SOT-23 MOSFETs, a Pico W module on castellated edges, USB-C connector, headers.

Order of operations

  1. Stencil + paste (if using paste): apply solder paste to all SMD pads through a 4 mil stainless stencil. Wipe excess. Clean the stencil after.
  2. Place SMDs by hand with tweezers, smallest to largest. Tack passives first. Then SOIC, QFN, USB.
  3. Reflow in toaster oven: ramp to 200 °C in 90 s, peak 235 °C for 30 s, cool to room temp.
  4. Inspect: under microscope, check every joint. Reflow any tombstones or bridges with hot air + flux.
  5. Add Pico W module: solder castellated pads by hand with iron. Pads are 0.5 mm, so 0.5 mm solder + tinned-tip works.
  6. Add headers: through-hole, easiest. Use a breadboard to hold headers vertically; solder one pin first, check angle, then do the rest.
  7. Add bulk caps + connectors: through-hole, biggest thermal mass — save for last.
  8. Clean: IPA + soft brush. Remove flux residue. Inspect again.
  9. Smoke test: current-limited PSU at 0.1 A on 5 V input. If current draw > expected baseline + 10 %, you have a short. If not, ramp up.

This sequence keeps the most thermally-sensitive parts (SMDs) on the hottest reflow event, and adds hand-solder parts after when thermal stress is bounded.

Practical advice: learn on Sn63/Pb37 (easier). For products you'll ship commercially, switch to SAC305 + appropriate flux + 30–40 °C higher iron temp.

Safety

  • Ventilation: solder smoke (especially flux fumes) is irritating + slightly toxic. Get an exhaust fan or fume extractor with activated-carbon filter.
  • Wash hands after soldering, especially with lead solder. Don't eat at the workbench.
  • Hot iron is 350–400 °C. It WILL set things on fire if dropped on paper or fabric. Use a metal stand.
  • Eye protection: solder occasionally pops + sprays microdroplets when feeding into a flux-rich joint. Safety glasses always.
  • ESD: wrist strap to ESD mat to ground. Sensitive MOSFETs (gate-source) can die at 50 V; a synthetic carpet shoe scuff can reach 10+ kV.
