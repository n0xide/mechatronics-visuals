Engineering Standards Reference

A guide to the standards bodies + their numbered standards that govern electronics, PCB assembly, automotive parts, ISO process compliance, environmental regulations, and safety. Knowing "IPC-A-610 Class 3" or "AEC-Q100 Grade 2" or "IEC 60601" is shorthand that lets you specify a product's quality + market without 50 lines of prose.

IPC: PCB assembly + manufacturing JEDEC: Semiconductors (memory, ESD) AEC-Q: Automotive electronics ISO / IEC: International generic UL / CE / FCC: Safety + emissions compliance

IPC — Association Connecting Electronics Industries

The PCB-and-assembly standards body. Every contract manufacturer references IPC. Their classes determine cost: IPC-A-610 Class 1 = consumer toys; Class 3 = medical / aerospace; Class 3A = space.

IPC-A-610 Class breakdown

Class 1 — General electronics: toys, consumer products. Cosmetic defects allowed. Cheapest.

Class 2 — Dedicated service: communications, industrial controls, instruments. Higher reliability requirement.

Class 3 — High-reliability: military, medical, aerospace. Most stringent — no allowed defects on solder joints + critical dimensions.

Class 3A — Space (added later). Ultra-high reliability; full traceability + microscopy + X-ray verification.

When you spec "IPC-A-610 Class 3", the contract manufacturer's solder process must meet that bar. Class 3 typically adds 30-50 % to assembly cost.

AEC-Q — Automotive Electronics Council Qualification

Components destined for automotive use have to survive harsher conditions than consumer parts. AEC-Q qualification is shorthand: cheaper to spec "AEC-Q100 Grade 1" than to write out 40 thermal cycling + humidity tests.

How to navigate which standard applies

Decision tree

  1. What market? US (FCC + UL), EU (CE + EN), Japan (VCCI + JEITA), Canada (ICES).
  2. What industry? Automotive (AEC-Q + ISO 26262), medical (IEC 60601 + ISO 13485), aerospace (DO-160 + MIL), industrial (IEC 61508), consumer (UL/CE basic).
  3. What enclosure / environment? IP rating (60529), thermal (IEC 60068), drop / vibration (60068, JESD22).
  4. What signal? EMC (IEC 61000, EN 55032), wireless (FCC Part 15 / EN 300-328 / EN 301-893).
  5. What chemistry? RoHS, REACH, Prop 65, conflict minerals.

Standards rabbit-holes

You can spend forever in standards. Practical advice:

  • Don't try to read all of them. They're hundreds of pages of bureaucratic text.
  • Outsource compliance testing. Labs like UL, TÜV SÜD, SGS, Intertek handle the testing + paperwork. Budget $10k-$50k+ for a small product.
  • EU CE self-declaration is the cheapest path if you can do your own testing — you sign a DoC saying you meet the directives. Audits happen if there's a complaint.
  • The standards aren't actually optional. Selling a non-RoHS product in EU = €10-100k fine. Non-FCC product in US = $11k/day per device.
