Study path — 26-phase ladder for self-taught robotics

The 9 EE + 9 ME + 8 EM phases (plus math prerequisites) make 26 modules. Strict linear order is wrong — too much foundation before any payoff. This is the dependency-aware sequence: six ladders that get you to a working robotic arm fastest. Click any card to open the phase markdown.

EE — Electrical ME — Mechanical EM — Electromechatronics Math prerequisites

Active buildWhat to study next for arm-6dof-pa612

Your sole active long-arc is a 6-DOF industrial-prototype arm in PA612-CF with ROS 2 and CAN-FD. The companion pedagogical build (arm-6dof-so100-mega, hobby tier) shares the same skill stack. The arm needs kinematics, motor control, sensor fusion, feedback loops. These five are the immediate critical path:

  • EM 2 — Actuators
    stepper / BLDC / servo selection for joints
  • EM 3 — Motor control
    FOC, current loops, position loops
  • EM 4 — Feedback (PID)
    tuning each joint controller
  • EM 1 — Sensors
    encoders, IMU, limit switches
  • EM 8 — Robotics & mechatronics
    DH params, jacobians, trajectory planning

CAD/material (ME 1, 2, 4) is mostly done — you've picked PA612-CF and have the printers. Defer EE 9 (RF), ME 9 (thermal), and EM 6 (power mgmt) until the arm moves. EE 3 (AC) can wait permanently if no mains-driven subsystem.

How to use this page

  • Ladders run in number order. You can be in L1 and L2 at the same time, but don't skip L1.
  • Inside a ladder, top to bottom = rough order. Tracks within a ladder are parallel — you can work EE and ME in the same week.
  • Click any card to open the phase markdown in your editor (relative link).
  • Each phase has a visuals folder linked from visuals-index.html. Open the visual when the phase doc references it.
  • EM = where the disciplines meet. L5 is the goal. Everything before is scaffolding.
