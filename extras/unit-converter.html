Force

1 N ≈ weight of a 100-gram apple 1 kgf = 9.807 N = weight of 1 kg 1 lbf = 4.448 N 1 dyne = 10⁻⁵ N (CGS)

Notes

  • kgf and lbf are weights, not masses — they include g built in. Confusion: a "kg" sticker on a scale is its weight (kgf at Earth surface). On the moon, the kg of mass is the same but the kgf weight is ~1/6.
  • Newton is the SI force — all serious mechanical analysis uses N. Convert to kgf or lbf only for the user-facing display.
  • For pull-test specs (e.g., "10 lbf min insertion force") use the converter to get N for your FEA.

Energy / Work

1 J = 1 N·m = 1 W·s 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ (utility billing unit) 1 cal = 4.184 J (chemistry); 1 kcal = 1 food-Cal 1 BTU = 1055 J (HVAC, heating) 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Battery capacity shortcut

Battery capacity is usually rated in mAh, which is current × time, not energy. To convert to energy:

Energy (Wh) = Capacity (Ah) × Nominal voltage (V)

So a 5000 mAh 3.7 V LiPo = 5.0 × 3.7 = 18.5 Wh = 66.6 kJ. A 18650 cell at 2500 mAh / 3.7 V ≈ 9.25 Wh.

Power

1 W = 1 J/s 1 hp (mech) = 745.7 W (US/imperial) 1 hp (metric) = 735.5 W (also: PS, ch, CV) 1 BTU/h = 0.2931 W 12 000 BTU/h ≈ 1 ton (refrigeration)

Watch the hp variants

  • Mechanical hp (745.7 W) — US convention, also called "imperial" or "brake" hp.
  • Metric hp / PS / ch / CV (735.5 W) — European convention, ~1.4 % less than US.
  • Electrical hp (746 W) — slightly different again, for AC motor nameplate.
  • Boiler hp (9.81 kW) — only for steam — completely different definition.
  • When buying motors across borders, the same "5 hp" can mean 3.68 kW (metric PS) or 3.73 kW (US). Always cross-check kW for clarity.

Notes

  • Tesla and Gauss are by far the most common — every datasheet for Hall sensors, magnets, motors uses one of them. 1 T = 10⁴ G, easy memorize.
  • Weber rarely appears outside transformer-design math — it's flux integrated over a surface. 1 Wb = 1 V·s = 10⁸ Mx.
  • Permeability link: B = μ₀(H + M); in air, B ≈ μ₀ H so 1 A/m gives ≈ 1.257 µT.

Linear speed

1 m/s = 3.6 km/h = 2.24 mph = 3.28 ft/s 1 mph = 0.447 m/s 1 knot = 1.852 km/h (aviation/marine)

Volume flow rate

1 L/min ≈ a kitchen tap dribble 1 GPM = 3.785 L/min 1 m³/h = 16.67 L/min 100 CFM ≈ small PC fan high speed

Rotational speed

1 RPM = 2π/60 rad/s ≈ 0.1047 rad/s 60 RPM = 1 rev/s = 6.28 rad/s 3000 RPM ≈ idle car engine 30 000 RPM ≈ drone outrunner BLDC

Notes

  • Mechanical RPM vs electrical Hz — for a BLDC, electrical frequency = (RPM / 60) × pole pairs. A 14-pole-pair motor at 6000 RPM has 1400 Hz electrical, which is why PWM gate-drive bandwidth matters.
  • Angular velocity ω = 2π · f in mechanical rad/s. Torque · ω = power: P [W] = T [N·m] × ω [rad/s]. Use rad/s in any power equation, not RPM.
