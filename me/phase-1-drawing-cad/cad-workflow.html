A parametric model is a chain of operations: sketch a 2D profile, constrain it, dimension it, push it into 3D, then refine. Click any step to see what it does and the most common way it bites you. Below: pick an export format and see which downstream uses it actually supports.

What this teaches

Every CAD model is a recipe. The pipeline is the recipe; the timeline records every step. Change a sketch dimension and everything downstream updates — that's the value. Reorder steps or rename geometry and downstream references break — that's the gotcha. The export step is its own minefield: STL discards parameters forever, STEP keeps them, DXF only carries 2D, and Gerber isn't a mechanical format at all.

What to notice

  • The timeline is the model. Fusion's bottom-bar timeline records every step in order. Re-running the timeline (after edits) re-derives all geometry from the original sketches — that's why a dimension change propagates instantly.
  • Constrain first, dimension second. Sketches with no constraints can rotate or shear when you drag any point. Apply geometric constraints (coincident, horizontal, perpendicular) until the sketch *holds its shape*, then dimension. A fully-constrained sketch goes blue in Fusion (green in FreeCAD).
  • FreeCAD's topological naming. Fillet edge "Edge12" — if you add an earlier feature that renumbers edges, the fillet references a different (or vanished) edge and the model breaks. FreeCAD 1.0 begins fixing this; until then, prefer Pattern + sketch references over manual edge picks.
  • Pattern saves your sanity. Four M2.5 mounting holes is one sketch + one Rectangular Pattern, not four sketches. Reorder them later by changing one parameter (47×11.4 mm spacing) instead of editing four sketches independently.
  • STL is one-way. Once exported, the parametric history is gone — the file is a triangle soup. Always keep the source .f3d (Fusion) or .FCStd (FreeCAD) file for future edits. STL is the deliverable, not the source.
  • STEP is the universal handoff. Going to a supplier you don't know? Export STEP. It carries solid geometry, assembly structure, and surfaces, and every CAD tool reads it. 3MF is newer and better for 3D printing specifically.

Cheat sheet

SKETCH STATE COLORS

  • Blue — fully constrained; safe.
  • Yellow / red — under-constrained; drag-warps.
  • Named parameters survive iterations; hard-coded values don't.

TOOL + FORMAT DECISIONS

  • Fusion 360 — better assemblies + drawings; free for hobby.
  • FreeCAD — open-source, Linux-friendly.
  • STEP CAD interchange · STL 3D print · DXF laser/CNC · 3MF modern print.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • FreeCAD topological naming — edge refs break on timeline reorder.
  • STL is lossy — no parameters survive export; keep source file.
  • Timeline reorder breaks downstream feature references.
