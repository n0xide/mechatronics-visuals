Shaft & Hole Fits — clearance, transition, interference

Hold a Ø10 mm H7 hole fixed; switch the shaft grade (h6, g6, p6, s6) and watch the tolerance bands shift along the nominal line. Whether the bands separate, touch, or overlap tells you whether the part slips in, presses in, or refuses entirely.

What this teaches

In the ISO hole-basis system, the hole is the fixed partner. Lowercase = shaft, capital = hole. Pick the shaft grade to engineer the relationship: h6 slides, g6 runs free, p6 is a light press, s6 is a tight press. Picking the wrong grade either lets a bearing wobble or splits an aluminium boss when you press the pin in.

FDM caveat: ISO fit tables assume machined parts. An FDM-printed hole at nominal Ø10.0 typically measures ~9.8 mm because plastic squishes inward as the perimeter cools. If you model an H7 hole and print it, the real fit drifts toward interference by ~0.2 mm. Compensate by either oversizing the hole in CAD by 0.2 mm or by drilling/reaming the printed hole to nominal before assembly.

What to notice

  • Hole-basis system. The hole stays H7 (always above nominal); you change the shaft grade to engineer the fit. Saves on tooling — drills and reamers are discrete sizes; shafts can be turned to any value.
  • Lowercase = shaft. H7/h6 → uppercase H is the hole, lowercase h is the shaft. The letter shifts the band up or down relative to nominal; the number sets the band's thickness (IT grade — IT6 tighter than IT7).
  • h6 is the dividing line. Shaft band sits *just below* nominal — minimum interference is zero, so it's a clearance fit but barely. Anything tighter (j6, k6) starts to overlap.
  • g6 runs freer. Whole band shifted further down so the shaft is always smaller — used for spinning shafts where lubrication is needed.
  • p6, s6 invert the geometry. Shaft band sits *above* the hole band entirely — the shaft is always too big. Assembly requires force (press fit, sometimes thermal: heat the boss or freeze the shaft).
  • Transition fits (k6, n6, m6 — not shown) have overlapping bands. Some assemblies clearance, others interference, depending on actual sizes — used for precise alignment where you'll tap parts together but want repeatability.

DECISION

  • Clearance — assembly + service (bearings, sliding shafts).
  • Transition — locating but separable (dowel pins).
  • Press — permanent, no lockring (gears, bushings).

COMMON PITFALLS

  • FDM holes — print ~0.2 mm undersized; design with offset or ream after.
  • Over-tight press — cracks aluminum hubs; calibrate or step down a grade.
