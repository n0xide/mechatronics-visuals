Surface Finish Symbols — Ra, Rz, Rt & ISO 1302

A nominal dimension says "the bore is Ø25 mm". The surface-finish callout says "and the wall is allowed to be this rough at most". Get it wrong and bearings drag, O-rings leak, seals tear, fluids stick, and fatigue cracks find every machining mark. This visual decodes the ISO 1302 / ASME Y14.36 surface-finish symbol, anchors Ra/Rz/Rt to numbers you can feel, and maps machining processes to the Ra ranges they realistically deliver.

Decoding the symbol — ISO 1302 fully populated

The symbol is a checkmark-style "V" with optional bar across the top. Six positions around it carry information; you only fill in the ones that constrain the surface.

What Ra, Rz, Rt actually mean

Three numbers, three pieces of information about the same profile trace.

Ra — arithmetic average roughness: average of the absolute deviations from the mean line over the sampling length. → Ra = (1/L) × ∫0L |y(x)| dx Rz (ISO) — average of the five largest peak-to-valley distances within the sampling length. Roughly 4–7× Ra. Rt — maximum peak-to-valley over the full evaluation length. Captures the worst single bump. Old Rmax / Ry — same idea as Rt; you'll meet it on legacy drawings.

Why three? A surface that looks the same Ra-wise can have very different Rz / Rt:

  • Lapped surface — Ra 0.2 µm, Rz 1.5 µm. Smooth and consistent; nothing sticks out.
  • Honed bore — Ra 0.4 µm, Rz 2.5 µm. The cross-hatch holds oil — ideal for hydraulic cylinders.
  • Sharply turned surface — Ra 0.8 µm, Rz 8 µm. Same Ra but a few deep grooves — will cut an O-ring.

Rule of thumb: if sealing or fatigue is involved, specify Rz as well as Ra. For appearance only, Ra alone is fine.

The turning-roughness formula (closed-form)

For single-point turning with a round-nose tool, the geometric residual roughness is:

Theoretical Ra ≈ f² / (32 · r) Theoretical Rz ≈ f² / (8 · r) where f = feed per revolution (mm/rev), r = tool nose radius (mm) → halving the feed → 4× smoother surface (geometric only) → doubling the nose radius → 2× smoother

Real surface is rougher than the theoretical formula because of tool vibration, edge buildup, and material spring-back. Use the formula to estimate; rely on a contact profilometer to verify.

Worked example — spec for a bearing journal

The function

A Ø20 mm shaft runs in a ball bearing inner race (light press fit, k6/H7 slide combo). The shaft surface seats the bearing inner ring; surface roughness determines press-fit retention force and prevents bearing damage during install.

The Ra spec

Ra ≤ 0.8 µm — bearing manufacturer's minimum for press-fit seats (e.g., SKF, FAG general catalog) Rz ≤ 4 µm — cap the deep grooves; otherwise bearing inner ring may not seat consistently Lay = C (circumferential / lathe-turned) — press fit relies on contact area; circumferential lay is fine

The process choice

Turn to Ra 1.6 µm, then cylindrical-grind to Ra 0.4 µm. The Ra 0.8 spec is comfortably met. f = 0.1 mm/rev, r = 0.8 mm → theoretical Ra = f²/(32·r) = 0.1²/(32·0.8) ≈ 3.9×10−4 mm ≈ 0.39 µm. Real Ra is higher due to vibration and tool wear; finish-grind makes the number predictable.

The drawing callout

"V_bar Ra 0.8 / Rz 4 / C / 0.8" — reads "machined; Ra max 0.8 µm; Rz max 4 µm; circumferential lay; 0.8 mm sampling length"

What to notice

  • Ra is the default but not always the right number — for seals, dynamic contact, and fatigue, specify Rz too. Ra averages out lone deep scratches that may dominate function.
  • Surface finish costs money exponentially — going from Ra 3.2 to Ra 0.8 is one extra pass. Going from Ra 0.8 to Ra 0.1 needs a grinder. Going from Ra 0.1 to Ra 0.025 needs lapping. Spec the loosest finish your function tolerates.
  • Lay is functional, not cosmetic — perpendicular lay across a sliding contact accelerates wear (each ridge is a tiny saw); parallel lay along the slide is fine. Honed cross-hatch is the canonical example: ridges retain oil.
  • Casting / printing skin has limits — sand cast surfaces below Ra 12.5 are aspirational. If you spec Ra 1.6 without "removal required", the part will fail inspection because the casting skin can't meet it.
  • FDM layer height ≈ roughness ridge height — a 0.2 mm layer print has ridges 200 µm apart and ~100 µm tall; Ra is in the 6–12 µm range. Smooth it post-process (sanding, vapor smoothing, paint) for any contact / sealing function.
  • Specs you didn't write — a drawing with no surface-finish symbol implies the general note ("all surfaces Ra 6.3 unless otherwise specified") in the title block. If there's no general note, the part is uncontrolled — bad news for inspection.

SHORTCUTS

  • Ra 3.2 ≈ rough turn / mill, no finish pass.
  • Ra 1.6 ≈ standard milled face.
  • Ra 0.8 ≈ turned + bearing-press fit OK.
  • Ra 0.4 ≈ ground sliding seal.
  • Ra 0.1 ≈ honed, polished, optical.
  • R3.2 numeric series doubles: 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.3, 12.5, 25.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Spec'ing Ra without Rz on sealing surfaces — a single deep groove will leak.
  • Tight Ra on cast / printed faces without specifying "removal required" — the as-formed skin can't meet the spec.
  • Forgetting lay direction — perpendicular lay across a slide cuts the mating surface.
  • Using imperial µin mixed with µm — 32 µin = 0.8 µm. Watch the units on US drawings.
  • Confusing RzISO with RzDIN (old) — the old DIN Rz is the max peak-to-valley (today's Rt). Modern Rz averages the 5 largest. Different number; check the date on the drawing.
