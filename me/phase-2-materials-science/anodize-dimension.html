Anodize Dimensional Allowance

Anodizing converts aluminum surface into a hard oxide layer — but unlike paint, that layer doesn't sit on top. It grows half outward and half into the metal. For tight-tolerance parts (pins, shafts, holes) you must pre-machine to compensate, or the part won't fit after the anodize bath. The 50/50 rule: where the oxide grows

Anodizing is an electrolytic process. The aluminum surface is converted into Al2O3 (aluminum oxide), but the conversion happens at the original surface plane. New oxide forms by oxidizing the metal below; that oxide is less dense than the parent aluminum, so it occupies more volume.

1. Bulk density: Al ≈ 2.70 g/cm³ • Al₂O₃ ≈ 3.95 g/cm³ 2. Pilling–Bedworth ratio for Al → Al₂O₃ ≈ 1.28. Volume grows by 28% as Al converts. 3. If thickness t of oxide forms, ~ t/1.28 of aluminum is consumed below the original surface. 4. Net: of total layer thickness t, about 0.5·t grows outward and 0.5·t grows inward — the classic "50/50 rule." 5. → A hole of Ø D shrinks to D − t (the layer enters from both walls); a pin grows from D to D + t. 6. Most shops cite 50/50 as average. Real values vary 40/60 to 60/40 with alloy and bath chemistry; specify the deviation if it matters.

Worked example — Ø5 H7 pin in Ø5 H7 hole, Type III hard-coat

Starting fits (raw 6061-T6, no coating)

Hole Ø5 H7 = 5.000 / 5.012 mm (ISO H7 bore) Pin Ø5 h6 = 4.992 / 5.000 mm (ISO h6 shaft) Clearance band = 0.000 / 0.020 mm (sliding fit)

After Type III, 50 µm spec

Hole shrinks: D_after = 5.000 / 5.012 − 0.050 = 4.950 / 4.962 mm Pin grows: D_after = 4.992 / 5.000 + 0.050 = 5.042 / 5.050 mm Interference: pin is 5.042 min, hole is 4.962 max → 0.080 mm interference, will not assemble.

Compensation strategy

Pre-anodize hole: 5.000 + 0.050 = 5.050 mm + H7 tolerance → spec 5.050 / 5.062 mm Pre-anodize pin: 5.000 − 0.050 = 4.950 mm + h6 tolerance → spec 4.942 / 4.950 mm After anodize: hole 5.000 / 5.012, pin 5.000 / 5.008 → restored clearance band 0.000 / 0.012 mm Drawing note: "ALL DIMENSIONS BEFORE ANODIZE — HARD-COAT MIL-A-8625 TYPE III, 50 µm, MASK FACES TO ±0.025"

Mask or machine after

Two options to avoid the math: (1) mask the critical surface so oxide doesn't form there (cheap, but mask creep adds ±0.02 mm); (2) anodize over-thick, then grind / hone the precision surfaces to spec (expensive, perfect tolerance). Production hydraulic cylinders use (2); robotic-arm prototype linkages usually use (1).

What to notice

  • The hole always shrinks by the same amount as the pin grows — they're symmetric. Forget which way each goes by remembering: oxide grows everywhere normal to the metal surface. For a hole, "outward" is inward. For a pin, "outward" is outward.
  • Type III at 50 µm shifts dimensions by 0.05 mm — that's significant for an H7/h6 fit (band is 0.020 mm). Always compensate or post-machine.
  • Type II at 15 µm shifts by 0.015 mm — also significant for press fits, ignorable for through-hole bolts.
  • Thread fits get tight — internal threads (tapped holes) lose 2·t on minor and major diameters; external threads (studs) gain 2·t. Mask threads or chase post-anodize.
  • Sharp corners build extra oxide — anodize wants ≥ 0.5 mm radius on outside corners (electric field concentrates). Burn-marks and pitted surface on sharp edges.
  • Wave the part rack with care — racking contact points leave un-anodized spots. Hide them on non-critical faces or post-coat-touch with cold-cure.
  • Color depth ≠ coating thickness — dyed parts use absorption, so a thicker coating absorbs more dye and looks darker. If color uniformity is critical, hold ±2 µm on the coating spec.

SHORTCUTS

  • Type II 15 µm → shifts each face by ~0.008 mm
  • Type III 50 µm → shifts each face by ~0.025 mm
  • Hole loses, pin gains — same magnitude
  • Spec MIL-A-8625 with type + class
  • Mask bores you need exact, or post-machine
  • Radius all outside corners ≥ 0.5 mm

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Forgetting threads — internal M5 threads tighten 0.025 mm per side after Type III 50 µm. M5×0.8 nominal becomes hopeless. Mask or chase.
  • Anodizing pre-press-fit parts — the oxide can chip during press-in, leaves bare Al + galvanic risk. Press first, anodize last (mask the bore).
  • Mixing alloys on the same rack — 6061 takes color uniformly; 7075 goes blotchy; cast 380 is nearly black no matter what. Group by alloy.
  • Assuming color matches between runs — dye lots vary. Order all parts in a single batch if cosmetic.
  • Sealed vs unsealed — sealing closes the pores. Unsealed is more dye-friendly but absorbs grease. For functional parts, always seal.
  • Hand-soldering after anodize — oxide is non-conductive. You can't solder to anodized surface; either mask the pad or use a dielectric strain relief.
