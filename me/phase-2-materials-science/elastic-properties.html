What to notice

  • Only two of E, G, K, ν are independent for an isotropic material. Move the ν slider and watch G and K rebalance — E stays fixed.
  • ν = 0.5 means incompressible. Push ν toward 0.5: K diverges (denominator 1−2ν → 0). Volume change ΔV/V → 0. Real rubber is ~0.499.
  • ν = 0 means no lateral contraction. Cork has ν ≈ 0 — push it down, the sides don't bulge. Useful for seals. Thermodynamic stability requires −1 < ν < 0.5; negative ν is real (auxetic foams contract sideways when squeezed) but rare.
  • Polymers have higher ν than metals. PETG ≈ 0.40 vs. steel 0.29 vs. alumina 0.22. The chain-mobility lets them squeeze sideways more readily.
  • G is always less than E. For ν > 0, G = E / (2(1+ν)) < E/2. Shear is "easier" than tension for the same material.
  • Anisotropic warning. These relationships hold for *isotropic* materials. FDM prints and rolled metals are NOT isotropic — properties differ by direction; one ν is not enough.

Cheat sheet

ISOTROPIC RELATIONS

shear
G = E / (2(1 + ν))
bulk
K = E / (3(1 − 2ν))

POISSON ANCHORS

  • most metals — ν ≈ 0.25–0.35.
  • rubber — ν → 0.5 (incompressible).
  • cork — ν ≈ 0 (no lateral bulge).
  • FDM — anisotropic; E differs in X, Y, Z.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • rolled steel ≠ isotropic — grain direction matters.
  • FDM Z-axis — E is only 30–60% of in-plane.
  • one ν is not enough for anisotropic materials.
