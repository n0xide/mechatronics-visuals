What to notice

  • HDT is the real "hot car" gate. PLA at HDT 55°C fails the dashboard test; PETG at 75°C is marginal; ASA at 98°C and PA12 at 100°C pass comfortably.
  • UV stability separates ASA from ABS. They print similarly (both warp without enclosure), but ABS yellows and crazes in months; ASA holds for years. Outdoors → ASA, always.
  • Nylon is the toughest filament — and the most annoying. PA12 absorbs water from the air aggressively; wet nylon prints poorly and the part's mechanical properties drop ~30%. Dry box mandatory.
  • TPU 95A bridges the rigid/flexible gap. Stiff enough to print without slumping but flexible enough for gaskets, bumpers, cable strain relief. Print slow (≤30 mm/s) and direct-drive only.
  • PLA wins on print difficulty, loses on everything functional. Use it for indoor display models and printer benchmarks; don't put it in your car or your hand luggage.
  • "Higher temp = harder to print" is not always true. PA12 has lower bed/nozzle than ABS in many formulations — the issue is moisture, not heat. Always check filament humidity before blaming the slicer.

DECISION RULES

  • hot car — PETG/ASA minimum.
  • outdoor / UV — ASA.
  • impact / fatigue — PA12.
  • flexible / gaskets — TPU.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • PLA in hot car — fails at ~60°C dashboard temps.
  • ASA without enclosure — warps and cracks.
  • wet nylon — must dry PA12 12–24h before printing.
