What to notice

  • Indenter geometry tells you the use case. Sharp diamond = hard material + small impression. Large ball = soft material, big visible impression, averages over surface defects.
  • Rockwell is fast and direct-reading. Depth-of-penetration is measured under load; no microscope needed. Why HRC dominates the shop floor.
  • Vickers is the universal scale. Same indenter and formula at any load → micro to macro. Slow because you measure the impression diagonals under a microscope.
  • Shore is for polymers because metal scales would just punch through. Shore A and D overlap (~Shore A 95 ≈ Shore D 45) — use A for soft, D for rigid.
  • The 3.45·HB rule is a steel-only crutch. Aluminum work-hardens differently; brass is too soft. The same HB value means a different UTS for each metal.
  • Don't measure thin coatings on Brinell. The 10 mm ball averages through the substrate. Use Vickers microhardness for thin layers (anodize, electroplate).

COMMON PITFALLS

  • 3.45·HB rule — STEELS only; not Al, not brass.
  • thin specimen — use HV, not HB (ball averages substrate).
  • Shore A vs D — overlap zone; don't mix scales.
