What to notice

  • Each class clusters. Metals dominate the upper-right (stiff and dense), polymers sit lower-left (light and compliant), ceramics top-center (stiff and moderately dense).
  • Performance-index lines slope by index exponent. For tension members (E/ρ) the line slopes 1:1 on log-log; beams (E^0.5/ρ) slope 2:1; plates (E^0.333/ρ) slope 3:1. Materials *above* the line beat materials below it for that loading.
  • Wood and CFRP beat metals on beam stiffness per weight. Switch to "beam stiffness" overlay and notice wood sits above 1018 steel — the reason fishing rods and aircraft spars are not made of solid steel.
  • FDM plastics (PLA / PETG / ABS / PA12) cluster tightly. They differ in HDT and toughness, not stiffness. Don't pick a filament by E.
  • Strength chart (Sy vs ρ) reshuffles winners. Titanium Ti-6Al-4V and CFRP dominate; PLA looks competitive only because we plot bulk specimens, not layer-bonded prints.
  • Cost vs strength reveals "engineering reality." 6061-T6 sits in the value sweet spot; titanium is 30× more expensive per MPa of Sy.

Cheat sheet

CLASS SIGNATURES

  • metals — high E, ductile, yield before fracture.
  • polymers — low E, viscoelastic, HDT matters.
  • ceramics — high E, brittle, fail at elastic limit.
  • composites — anisotropic; strong along fibre.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • FDM ≠ bulk — printed PETG strength ≪ injection-molded.
  • CF-filled — changes E, Sy, fatigue dramatically.
  • anisotropy — bulk values assume isotropic; FDM/CFRP aren't.
