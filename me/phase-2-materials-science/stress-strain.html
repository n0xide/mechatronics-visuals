Stress-strain curve

The shape of the σ vs ε curve tells you the failure mode: a ductile metal warns you with yielding and necking, a brittle ceramic snaps at the elastic limit, a polymer drags through a rubbery plateau. Drag the marker along the strain axis to walk through the regions, and switch materials to see how the story changes.

σ–ε curve elastic slope = E key points

What to notice

  • Shape = failure mode. Long plateau before fracture → ductile; near-vertical line that snaps → brittle; rubbery shelf and cold-draw → semi-crystalline polymer.
  • E is just the slope of the elastic region. Toggle the slope helper line (green dashed) — it matches the initial straight segment. Higher slope = stiffer material.
  • Yield is the curve's first knee. For metals without a sharp yield, use the 0.2% offset construction: parallel-to-elastic line shifted by ε=0.002 intersects the curve at Sy.
  • UTS is the peak, not the end. After UTS, engineering stress drops because cross-section is necking — but the true material is still strain-hardening locally.
  • Ceramic curves have no plastic region. Move the marker past ~0.4% strain on the alumina trace — fracture happens before any plastic flow. No warning.
  • Polymer "cold draw" is a real plateau. Move the PETG marker into the long flat region after yield — the bar is pulling out into a neck at constant load, not strain-hardening.

Cheat sheet

CORE EQUATIONS

eng. stress
σ [Pa] = F [N] / A₀ [m²]   A₀ = original cross-section, not the necked one. 1 MPa = 1 N/mm².
eng. strain
ε = δ [m] / L₀ [m]   Dimensionless — m/m or %. 0.002 = 0.2 % (the offset for Sy).
elastic
σ = E · ε   Hooke's law — straight-line portion only.
ductility
%EL = (L_f − L₀)/L₀ × 100

"Engineering" stress/strain use the original dimensions throughout the test. "True" stress/strain use the instantaneous (necked) dimensions and are larger past UTS. For small strains the two agree; design codes use engineering stress.

STIFFNESS ANCHORS

  • steel — E ≈ 200 GPa.
  • aluminum — E ≈ 70 GPa.
  • PETG — E ≈ 2–3 GPa.
  • brittle — fails at elastic limit, no plastic region.
  • ductile — long plastic region before fracture.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • engineering vs true — σ_eng uses A₀; σ_true uses instantaneous A.
  • no sharp yield? — use 0.2% offset method.
  • UTS ≠ fracture stress — necking drops stress past UTS.
