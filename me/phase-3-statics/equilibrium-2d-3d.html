What to notice

  • Three equations, three unknowns in 2D. ΣFx=0, ΣFy=0, ΣM=0 about any point. Pin+roller gives 3 unknowns (RAx, RAy, RB). Solvable.
  • Pick your moment center wisely. Summing about a support kills that support's reactions from the moment equation — leaving one equation in one unknown. Much easier algebra.
  • Cross-check with ΣFy. RA + RB should always equal the total downward load. If it doesn't, you made an arithmetic error.
  • Load near the right → right reaction grows. Intuitive: the support nearest the load takes most of the weight (lever arm logic).
  • Centered load → reactions are equal. RA=RB=P/2. Symmetry is your friend.
  • 3D needs six equations. A cantilever bracket loaded at its tip produces a force and a bending moment at the wall — both must be reacted. Notice My=Fz·Lx (bending about y) and Mx=−Fz·Ly (bending about x, from the Y-offset of the load). Both are bending — pure torsion about the bracket axis would need an applied moment, not a tip force.

Cheat sheet

EQUATIONS

2D (3 eqs)
ΣFx=0, ΣFy=0, ΣM=0
3D (6 eqs)
ΣFx=ΣFy=ΣFz=0ΣMx=ΣMy=ΣMz=0

SPECIAL BODIES

  • two-force body — forces equal, opposite, collinear along the line joining them.
  • three-force body — forces are concurrent (lines meet at a point) or all parallel.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • sign drift — fix +x, +y, +CCW once; don't flip mid-problem.
  • wrong moment point — pick the point that eliminates the most unknowns (typically a support).
