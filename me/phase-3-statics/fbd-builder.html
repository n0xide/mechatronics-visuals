Pick a support type at each end, drop loads on the beam, and watch the reaction forces and moments appear. The determinacy check tells you whether ΣF=0, ΣM=0 are enough to solve the system — or whether you need deformation compatibility too.

What to notice

  • Each support type removes a fixed number of DOF. A pin removes 2 (no translation), a roller 1, a 2D fixed support 3 (no translation, no rotation). 3D ball-socket removes 3; built-in removes all 6.
  • Reactions appear only along DOFs that are constrained. A roller can only push perpendicular to its surface — it has no horizontal reaction, no moment.
  • Determinate = unknowns equal equations. 2D rigid body has 3 equilibrium equations (ΣFx, ΣFy, ΣM); 3D has 6. A pin+roller beam in 2D has 2+1=3 unknowns — perfectly determinate.
  • Indeterminate = more unknowns than equations. Two pins at both ends of a 2D beam gives 4 unknowns vs. 3 equations — redundant, but stiffer. To solve you also need a compatibility equation (e.g. equal length).
  • Mechanism = fewer unknowns than equations. Insufficient supports → the body can move freely under load. Not a useful structure.
  • Loads don't change determinacy — only the count of reactions does. You can pile any number of forces and moments onto a beam without changing the unknown count.

DECISION

  • determinate — unknowns = equations (3 in 2D, 6 in 3D).
  • indeterminate — more unknowns than eqs; need compatibility.
  • mechanism — fewer unknowns; body moves freely.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • missing weight — always include W at the CG.
  • missed reactions — every constrained DOF needs an unknown.
  • internal forces — never double-count; FBD is external only.
