Moments & Couples — Lever Arm and Free Vectors

A moment is force times perpendicular distance. The left panel shows a single force about a pivot you choose; drag the pivot to change the lever arm. The right panel shows a pure couple — two equal-and-opposite forces — whose moment is the same about every point. That's what makes a couple a free vector.

Single Force — M = F·d

Drag the green pivot dot. The perpendicular distance d (dashed) drops a normal from the pivot onto the force's line of action.

Pure Couple — M = F·d (free vector)

Two equal-and-opposite forces separated by d. Net force = 0. The moment is the same regardless of which pivot you measure it about — change the pivot above and watch.

Right-Hand Rule for Moment Direction

Curl your right hand's fingers from r toward F. Your thumb points along M⃗. For 2D moments (CCW positive in screen plane), M⃗ points out of the page (+z); CW moments point into the page (−z).

What to notice

  • Lever arm is the perpendicular distance. Not the straight-line distance from pivot to where you push — only the component perpendicular to the line of action counts. M = F·d_⊥ = |r||F|sin(θ).
  • Move the pivot along the line of action and d stays the same. A force is a sliding vector: you can slide it anywhere along its own line without changing the moment about any external point.
  • A couple has zero net force. Equal-and-opposite, so ΣF = 0. But the two forces aren't collinear, so they twist the body — pure rotation tendency, no translation.
  • A couple's moment is independent of reference point. Change the pivot (the dropdown above): the moment from the couple stays at F·d. That's what "free vector" means — it acts the same anywhere.
  • Right-hand rule gives the moment's vector direction. r × F: curl fingers from r into F, thumb is M. In 2D, CCW = +ẑ (out of page); CW = −ẑ.
  • Varignon's theorem. If you resolve F into components, M = M_x + M_y. The moment of a resultant equals the sum of moments of its components — handy when d is hard to read directly but the components' lever arms are obvious.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

scalar
M = F · dd ⊥ to line of F
vector
M⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗
magnitude
|M| = |r|·|F|·sinθ
couple
M = F · dfree vector — same about any point

MEMORIZE

  • right-hand rule — curl fingers r → F, thumb is M direction.
  • Varignon — M of resultant = ΣM of components.
  • 2D sign — CCW = +, CW = −.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • d ≠ line-of-sight — use the perpendicular distance from pivot to line of F.
  • couple is free — its moment is independent of pivot; apply anywhere on the body.
