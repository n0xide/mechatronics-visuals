Zero-force member rules

  • Rule 1. If only two non-collinear members meet at an unloaded joint, both must carry zero force. (Their components in two independent directions must each sum to zero — only zero satisfies that.)
  • Rule 2. If three members meet at an unloaded joint and two are collinear, the third (the non-collinear one) is zero-force. The two collinear members will then have equal and opposite forces.
  • Use zero-force members to simplify first. Eliminate them before solving so you have fewer unknowns to chase joint-by-joint.

Method of joints vs. method of sections

  • Method of joints. Cut around one joint at a time. Each joint gives 2 equilibrium equations (ΣFx, ΣFy). Solve outward from a joint with at most 2 unknown members. Sequential and complete: gives every member's force.
  • Method of sections. Cut through the whole truss (typically 3 members at once). The two halves are each in equilibrium under ΣF and ΣM. Three equations → solve 3 unknowns in one step. Best when you only need the force in one or two specific members.
  • Sign convention. Assume every member is in tension initially (force points away from the joint along the member). A positive answer confirms tension; a negative answer means the member is actually in compression.
  • Reactions first. Always solve external reactions before starting joints. The truss as a whole is a 2D rigid body — 3 equations, 3 unknown reactions for a pin-roller setup.
  • Pratt truss top chord is in compression; bottom chord is in tension. Under a downward load, the top chord shortens (compresses) and the bottom chord lengthens (tensions). The diagonals in a Pratt point inward toward the load and are in tension.

Cheat sheet

ZERO-FORCE MEMBERS

  • rule 1 — 2 non-collinear members at an unloaded joint: both = 0.
  • rule 2 — 3 members, 2 collinear, joint unloaded: the third = 0.

WHICH METHOD?

  • joints — solve every member; pick joints with ≤2 unknowns, work outward.
  • sections — cut 3 members, 3 eqs (ΣFx, ΣFy, ΣM); fast for one specific member.
  • reactions first — always solve external reactions before either method.

ASSUMPTIONS & SIGN

  • ideal pins — loads at joints only; no self-weight; no bending in members.
  • sign convention — assume tension (+); negative result = compression.
