Pick a beam type and a load. V(x) and M(x) appear instantly. The two rules: dV/dx = −w(x), dM/dx = V. So V is the slope of M; M is maximum where V crosses zero. Click any cross-section to see the bending stress distribution σ = M·y/I.

Cross-section bending stress σ = M·y/I

Stress varies linearly across the depth: compression on the convex side, tension on the concave side, zero at the neutral axis. Magnitude scales with distance y from the neutral axis. σ_max = M·c/I at the outer fibre. Click the beam to pick a section.

What to notice

  • dV/dx = −w(x). No load → V constant. Point load → V jumps. UDL → V slopes linearly.
  • dM/dx = V. So M is the area under V. Constant V → linear M. Linear V → parabolic M.
  • M_max occurs where V crosses zero. Always. That's where dM/dx = 0. Build the V diagram first, find the zero crossing, then evaluate M there.
  • Cantilever (end-loaded) vs simply supported (midspan-loaded), same P: M_max at the fixed end of a tip-loaded cantilever = PL, vs PL/4 at midspan of an SS beam — 4× larger. That's the cost of having only one support. (A cantilever's M_max is always at the fixed end for any downward load along its length, not the midspan.)
  • UDL spreads the load. A simply-supported beam with total load wL has M_max = wL²/8, vs PL/4 if it's all at the centre. Same total load, half the peak moment when distributed.
  • Applied moments cause M jumps but no V jump. A concentrated couple shifts the M diagram by M₀ at its location. V is unaffected by pure couples.
  • σ_bending = M·y/I. Halve I (slimmer beam) → double the stress. Halve y (closer to NA) → half the stress. To survive bending, get the material *far* from the neutral axis — that's why structural beams have flanges at top and bottom.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

bending σ
σ = M · y / I
extreme fiber
σ_max = M · c / I = M / S
transverse τ
τ = V · Q / (I · b)
relations
dV/dx = −w · dM/dx = V

MEMORIZE

  • M is max where V = 0 — sign change in shear.
  • Positive M = sagging — compression on top, tension on bottom.
  • S = I / c — section modulus; bigger S → less bending stress.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Sign convention drift — pick one (sagging+ or hogging+) and hold it through both diagrams.
  • Jumps — V jumps at point loads, M jumps at applied couples; don't draw them smooth.
