Mohr's Circle & Failure Theories

Any 2D stress state (σ_x, σ_y, τ_xy) can be rotated to a coordinate system where shear vanishes — the principal axes. Mohr's circle is a graphical map of every rotated state. Then ductile and brittle materials have different rules for when "enough stress" means yield or fracture: Von Mises, Tresca, and Coulomb–Mohr / Modified Mohr.

Failure criteria comparison

Three classical theories. Von Mises (distortion energy) is the most accurate for ductile metals — the gold standard in FEA. Tresca is slightly more conservative (always predicts yielding sooner). The brittle envelope drawn here is Coulomb–Mohr (the simpler straight-line theory connecting S_ut on the tension axes and −S_uc on the compression axes); the closely-related Modified Mohr theory adds inset corners at (S_ut, −S_ut) and (−S_ut, S_ut) and is slightly less conservative in the mixed-sign quadrants. Use either for brittle materials (cast iron, ceramics, resin prints) that fail in tension at a different stress than in compression.

What to notice

  • Mohr's circle is geometry, not physics. Center = (σ_x + σ_y)/2, radius = √(((σ_x − σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy²). The circle plots every rotated coordinate system, so you can read off principal stresses (where it crosses the σ axis) and max shear (= radius).
  • Principal stresses come for free. σ_1 = center + radius, σ_2 = center − radius. The in-plane maximum shear is τ_max = radius = (σ_1 − σ_2)/2. For plane stress, the true 3D max shear includes σ_3 = 0 and may be larger: τ_max,3D = (σ_max − σ_min)/2 over {σ_1, σ_2, 0}.
  • Von Mises ellipse is the safe envelope for ductile metals. σ_VM = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ_xy²). When σ_VM ≥ S_y, the material yields. In (σ_1, σ_2) space, this traces a rotated ellipse.
  • Tresca is the inscribed hexagon — always inside the Von Mises ellipse, so always predicts failure first. Use it when you want a conservative quick check by hand.
  • Pure shear is the worst case for Tresca-vs-Von Mises disagreement. At pure shear (σ_x=σ_y=0, τ ≠ 0), Tresca says you yield at τ = S_y/2, but Von Mises lets you go to τ = S_y/√3 — about 15% more.
  • Coulomb–Mohr for brittle: a brittle material is much weaker in tension than compression. The failure envelope is asymmetric — large negative principal stress is okay, but small positive tension fractures it. In Q4 (σ₁ > 0, σ₂ < 0), the envelope is the straight line σ₁/S_ut − σ₂/S_uc = 1 from (S_ut, 0) to (0, −S_uc); Q2 is the mirror. (Modified Mohr is similar but inset, slightly less conservative in mixed quadrants; the difference matters only at high biaxial mixed-sign stresses.) Resin prints, cast iron, ceramics: don't pull on them.
  • Factor of safety n = S_y / σ_eff. If n < 1, the part fails. n ≥ 2 is typical for static loads, ≥ 3 for fatigue.
  • Plane stress means σ₃ = 0 is one of the three principal stresses. Always check whether σ₃=0 is the algebraic min or max of {σ_1, σ_2, σ_3}: that's the principal stress Tresca uses to compute σ_max − σ_min. For example, when σ_1, σ_2 are both positive, σ_min = σ_3 = 0, so Tresca = σ_1 (not σ_1 − σ_2).

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Mohr center
C = (σ_x + σ_y) / 2
Mohr radius
R = √[((σ_x − σ_y)/2)² + τ_xy²]
principals
σ_1,2 = C ± R, τ_max(in-plane) = R
Von Mises
σ_VM = √(σ_x² − σ_x·σ_y + σ_y² + 3τ²)
Tresca (plane stress)
σ_max − σ_min ≥ S_y, where σ_min, σ_max are taken from {σ_1, σ_2, σ_3 = 0}

MEMORIZE

  • Ductile → Von Mises (accurate) or Tresca (conservative).
  • Brittle → Coulomb-Mohr (or Modified Mohr) — VM/Tresca don't apply.
  • τ_max (in-plane) = R — radius of Mohr's circle directly. For plane stress with σ₃=0, the 3D max shear may be larger.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Tresca over-predicts failure — that's why it's the "safer" design choice.
  • Never use VM/Tresca on brittle materials — cast iron, ceramics, glass need Mohr-type criteria.
  • Mohr's circle uses 2θ. A physical 30° element rotation is 60° around the circle. Skip the factor of 2 and you'll mis-identify the principal plane.
  • Mohr τ sign convention varies. "τ positive down" vs "τ positive up" both appear in textbooks. Pick one and stay with it for the entire problem.
