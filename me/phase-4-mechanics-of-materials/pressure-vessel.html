Thin-Walled Pressure Vessel

When pressure pushes on the inside of a thin-walled cylinder or sphere, the wall stretches both around the circumference (hoop stress) and along the axis (longitudinal). For a cylinder, hoop is exactly twice longitudinal — which is why pressure cylinders rupture lengthwise, not crosswise. Slide pressure, radius, wall thickness; watch the stresses scale and the failure direction reveal itself.

Why σ_hoop = 2·σ_long (cylinder)

Two free-body cuts:

Cut #1 — longitudinal half-shell. Slice the cylinder along its axis. Pressure on the projected rectangle (2r · L) is balanced by hoop stress acting on two wall strips (2 · t · L). ΣF: p · 2r · L = 2 · σ_h · t · L → σ_h = p · r / t Cut #2 — cross-section. Slice perpendicular to axis. Pressure on the circle (π r²) is balanced by longitudinal stress on the ring (2π r · t). ΣF: p · π r² = σ_l · 2π r · t → σ_l = p · r / (2t) Ratio: σ_h / σ_l = 2. Always. Independent of pressure, radius, thickness. → A cylindrical vessel always fails along a longitudinal seam (perpendicular to the higher hoop stress) before it fails circumferentially.

Sphere — uniform stress

For a sphere, any cut through the center produces the same FBD by symmetry. Pressure on πr² is balanced by stress on the cut ring 2πr·t.

p · π r² = σ · 2π r · t → σ_sphere = p · r / (2t) Same stress in every direction. → No preferred failure direction; failure is by general yielding or a defect-driven crack. For the same p, r, t, a sphere has half the maximum stress of a cylinder. → Spherical tanks are inherently more efficient with material.

Worked example — compressed air receiver tank

The spec

Working pressure: 10 bar = 1.0 MPa Internal diameter: 400 mm → r = 200 mm Material: ASTM A537 Class 2 carbon-manganese-silicon steel, σ_y = 415 MPa, σ_UTS = 550 MPa min (per ASTM A537/A537M-13) Code: ASME BPVC Section VIII, n_min = 3.5 (or use 4× for static air)

Required wall thickness

Allowable σ = 415 / 4 = 104 MPa Set σ_h = pr/t ≤ 104 → t ≥ pr/104 = 1.0 · 200 / 104 = 1.92 mm Add corrosion allowance 1 mm + manufacturing tolerance 0.5 mm → spec t = 4 mm Actual hoop @ t = 4: σ_h = 1 · 200 / 4 = 50 MPa (below 104, n = 8.3 healthy) r/t = 200/4 = 50 → comfortably thin-walled

End caps

End cap stresses (hemispherical) at σ = pr/(2t) = 25 MPa. Flat plates would need ~3× thickness for same stress. ASME-coded vessels almost always use ellipsoidal or hemispherical ends.

Burst pressure

At t = 4 mm, burst when σ_h reaches σ_UTS ~ 550 MPa p_burst = σ_UTS · t / r = 550 · 4 / 200 = 11 MPa = 110 bar (11× nominal — pass)

What to notice

  • Hoop wins, always — cylinder failures are along the long axis, "unzipping" the seam. Every burst boiler, propane tank, or carbonated soda bottle fails this way.
  • Sphere is half-stressed — spherical LNG tanks, mercury thermometer bulbs, and gas cylinders on Mars rovers use the sphere shape exactly because it's half-stressed for the same material.
  • Thin-wall breaks at r/t < 10 — below that ratio, radial stress isn't negligible and the formulas are off by 20%+. Use Lamé thick-wall equations for pipes with r/t < 10.
  • Three principal stresses (3D) — σ_h, σ_l, σ_r (radial). In thin-wall, σ_r ≈ −p at inner, 0 at outer; the average is small. For yielding analysis, max shear is (σ_h − σ_r) / 2 = (pr/t + p) / 2.
  • Von Mises lower than max principal — because hoop and longitudinal stresses are both tensile, they partially "cancel" in von Mises. For cylinder: σ_VM = √(σ_h² − σ_hσ_l + σ_l²) = σ_h · √(3)/2 = 0.866 · σ_h.
  • Stress doesn't depend on length — a 1 m boiler and a 10 m boiler have the same wall stress at the same p, r, t. Long thin tubes don't get weaker per the standard formula (though they get harder to support).

SHORTCUTS

  • Sphere always half the stress of cylinder at same p, r, t
  • Failure parallel to axis on cylinder
  • n = 3.5–4× for ASME pressure vessels
  • r/t < 10 → use thick-wall (Lamé)
  • Add corrosion allowance 1–2 mm always
  • Hemispherical ends beat flat plate by 3×

PITFALLS

  • Forgetting r/t check — at r/t = 5, thin-wall is off by 30%+. PVC pipes are often r/t = 5.
  • Outer vs inner radius — use INNER for thin-wall; the formulas are derived on the inner.
  • Ignoring end caps — flat heads ≠ hemispherical heads. ASME spec gives detailed shape factors.
  • Welded seams — joint efficiency factor E reduces allowable stress by 0.7–1.0.
  • Cyclic fatigue — vessels with frequent pressure cycles need fatigue analysis, not just yield.
  • Hydrostatic test — actual vessels are pressure-tested to ~1.5× operating to find defects. Don't just analyze, also pressure-test.
