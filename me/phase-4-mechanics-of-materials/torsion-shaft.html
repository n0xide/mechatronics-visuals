Torsion in Circular Shafts

Twist a circular shaft (solid or hollow) in the linear-elastic regime with torque T. Inside, shear stress is zero on the axis and rises linearly to τ_max = T·c/J at the outer radius. The shaft twists through an angle φ = TL/(GJ). Hollow shafts ditch the low-stress core — almost the same J for much less mass. P = T·ω links torque to power for sizing motor shafts. (Non-circular cross-sections warp and require torsion constant C instead of J — see Saint-Venant torsion.)

τ(ρ) = T·ρ/J. Zero at center, max at outer surface. In a hollow shaft, the inner surface still has nonzero stress (= T·r_i/J), but the wasted low-stress material near the axis is removed.

Power-torque-speed

P = T·ω = T·2π·n. Pick any two, the third follows. For a NEMA17 stepper at 0.5 N·m holding torque: even 0 RPM means full torque (and zero power). Most motor failure modes are at low speed and high torque — that's where the shaft has to be sized.

What to notice

  • τ scales linearly with radius. Material at the axis carries no torque. Material at the outer surface carries the maximum stress. That's why hollow shafts are popular — you cut out the lazy material at the centre.
  • J scales as d⁴. Double the diameter → 16× the polar moment of inertia → 16× the torsional stiffness for the same length. Going one wire-gauge thicker on a drive shaft is a *big* upgrade.
  • Hollow shaft trade-off: a tube with d_o = 20, d_i = 16 has only ~60% the J of a solid 20 mm shaft, but only ~36% the mass. Pound-for-pound, hollow wins by ~1.7×.
  • Angle of twist φ = TL/(GJ) is a stiffness equation — longer or softer shaft twists more for the same torque. Sub the shaft into a control loop and you'll see this as backlash or compliance.
  • Power doesn't care about diameter, torque does. A 100 W motor at 3000 RPM only needs ~0.3 N·m. The same motor geared down 10:1 needs to deliver 3 N·m at the output — and the output shaft now needs to be sized for that.
  • Stress concentrations multiply τ. Keyways, shoulder fillets, and threads can give Kts of 1.5–3×. Real machine design uses Kts × τ_nominal and compares against allowable.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

shear stress
τ_max = T · c / J
J solid
J = π · d⁴ / 32
J hollow
J = π(d_o⁴ − d_i⁴) / 32
twist
φ = T · L / (G · J)
power
P = T · ω

MEMORIZE

  • τ is linear in r — 0 at center, max at OD.
  • Hollow ≈ same J, much less mass — inner core barely contributes.
  • J ∝ d⁴ — doubling diameter → 16× torsional stiffness.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Keyways & fillets — stress concentrations multiply local τ; don't ignore K_t.
  • Misalignment adds bending — combined stress is worse than pure torsion.
