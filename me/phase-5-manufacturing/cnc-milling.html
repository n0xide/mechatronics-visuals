CNC milling — speeds, feeds, chip flow

Three numbers govern every CNC cut: RPM (spindle), feed rate (table travel), and chip load (material per cutting edge per revolution). Get them right and the chips look like cornflakes; get them wrong and the tool either rubs (heat, wear) or breaks. The calculator below ties them together.

Climb vs conventional milling

Climb milling rotates the cutter in the same direction as the feed at the entry point — chip starts thick and thins to zero. Conventional goes opposite — chip starts at zero (rubbing) and thickens. Modern rigid mills: climb. Old gantries with backlash: conventional.

Climb pushes the chip into the cut path and lifts the workpiece — needs a rigid machine and no backlash. Conventional rubs at entry (poor surface, heat into the tool) but tolerates loose machines. Hobby CNCs: climb if your spindle holder is tight; conventional if you have a Shapeoko-class gantry with measurable backlash.

Depth-of-cut rule of thumb

For a full-slot cut (cutter engaged on both sides), keep axial depth-of-cut ≤ 0.5 × tool diameter. For a side cut (climb, < 30% radial engagement), you can push axial to 1.0× tool diameter or more.

What to notice

  • SFM is a material constant, not a machine setting. RPM is derived: RPM = (Vc × 1000) / (π × D) with Vc in m/min and D in mm. Carbide tools push SFM ~2× higher than HSS.
  • Chip load is the real diagnostic. Too low → cutter rubs, generates heat, dulls quickly. Too high → cutter breaks. The right band for aluminum + 6 mm 2-flute carbide is ~0.03–0.08 mm/tooth.
  • Climb is the default on rigid mills, but on a loose hobby gantry the table can pull into the cut. If you hear chatter or see backlash play, switch to conventional.
  • Full-slot ≤ 0.5D, side-cut ≥ 1D. If you need a deep pocket, take multiple shallow passes — total cycle time stays similar because each pass runs at the full feed rate.
  • Flood coolant or compressed air is mandatory for aluminum. Without it, chips re-weld to the tool flutes ("built-up edge") and surface finish collapses.
