Three properties of FDM determine whether your print survives load: layer adhesion (weakest direction is between layers), print orientation (rotate the part to keep load in-plane), and infill geometry (pattern and density of the interior lattice). Switch tabs to explore each.

Tensile strength: in-plane vs between-layer

For every common filament, between-layer (Z-axis) tensile strength is a fraction of in-plane (XY) tensile strength: roughly 50–65% for PLA/PETG/ASA/PA, and as low as ~45% for ABS (its layer adhesion is famously weak without an enclosure). The polymer chains within a layer entangle strongly during extrusion; between layers, they only fuse where freshly extruded plastic re-melts the previous layer's surface — that's a thinner, weaker bond zone.

Same part, two orientations, same load

A cantilever bracket loaded at the tip. The bending moment causes tension along the bracket's length (top fibres stretch, bottom fibres compress). Orient so that the layer stacking direction is across the thickness — then the length-wise tension runs in-plane, not between layers.

Infill pattern comparison

Patterns differ in how strength varies with load direction. Gyroid is the only common pattern that's truly isotropic (similar stiffness in X, Y, Z). Rectilinear is faster to print but anisotropic. Adjust the sliders to see how perimeters and density combine.

Perimeters dominate bending stiffness — they're the outer fibres of the beam. Doubling perimeters from 2→4 typically gains more strength than doubling infill 20→40%.

What to notice

  • Z-axis is always your weakest direction. Between-layer bonds are ~50–65% of in-plane strength for PLA/PETG/ASA/PA, and ~45% for ABS; design loads to run along, not across, layer lines.
  • Orientation matters more than material. A PLA part loaded in-plane often outperforms a PETG part loaded between layers.
  • Gyroid is the isotropic infill. Use it when load direction is unpredictable or rotational; rectilinear is fine when you know the load axis.
  • Perimeters > infill for bending stiffness — the outer fibres of any beam carry the most stress. 4 walls and 20% infill beats 2 walls and 40% infill in most bending cases.
  • Honeycomb is a print-time trap. Pretty, but slower than gyroid for similar stiffness; gyroid wins on speed + isotropy.

Cheat sheet

MEMORIZE

  • Z-axis strength ≈ 50–65% of XY (PLA/PETG/ASA/PA); ~45% for ABS
  • Gyroid = only isotropic infill
  • Structural: ≥3 perimeters + ≥40% infill
  • Self-supporting overhang (angle of the surface from vertical): PLA ~50°, PETG ~45°, ABS ~40° — steeper than this needs supports

DECISION

  • Orient critical loads parallel to layers
  • Tree supports for organic geometry
  • Grid supports for flat overhangs

PITFALLS

  • 100% infill ≠ strong — perimeters dominate
  • Cooling fan >50% on PETG/ABS/PA kills layer adhesion (PLA wants full fan)
