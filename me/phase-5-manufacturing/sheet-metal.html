Sheet metal — bend allowance & flat patterns

When you bend a sheet, material on the outside stretches and material on the inside compresses. The neutral axis (where length is unchanged) sits at fraction K through the thickness — typically K ≈ 0.4. Bend allowance is the arc length of that neutral axis. To order a laser-cut + bent part, you need the flat-pattern length = straight legs + bend allowances.

What to notice

  • Bend allowance < sum of straight legs. The neutral axis is shorter than the outside surface but longer than the inside; net effect is that the flat blank is shorter than naive "leg + leg + thickness" math suggests.
  • K depends on r/t. 0.33 for very tight bends, 0.40–0.45 for typical sheet metal, 0.50 for large radii. K=0.4 is a safe default.
  • Minimum bend radius depends on temper, not just alloy. 6061-T6 needs r ≥ 3t; 6061-O (annealed) bends almost flat. SendCutSend uses 6061-O internally then re-tempers — that's how they offer tight bends.
  • Always sanity-check the flat pattern in CAD. CAD tools (Fusion, SolidWorks) compute the flat from your model — but if you've used the wrong K-factor in your sheet-metal style, the laser-cut blank will be ~1–3 mm wrong per bend. For 4 bends in series, that's 12 mm of error.
  • Spring-back exists. Sheet metal bent to exactly 90° releases to ~91°. Over-bend by 1–2° if precision matters.
