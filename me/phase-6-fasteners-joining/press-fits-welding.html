Press fits & welding overview

Two ways to join two parts without bolts: (a) interference fits where the shaft is slightly bigger than the hole and friction holds it; (b) welding/brazing, which makes the joint metallurgically permanent. The press-fit calculator below uses the thin-walled hub approximation; the welding table compares MIG / TIG / spot / brazing / soldering for typical hobbyist work.

Equations used (simplified Lamé for thin-walled hub): p ≈ E_eff·δ / d with E_eff = (E_shaft · E_hub) / (E_shaft + E_hub) · F ≈ π·d·L·p·f · σ_hoop ≈ p·(d_hub² + d²)/(d_hub² − d²) (hub OD assumed 3×d). When shaft & hub share a material, E_eff = E/2 (compliance adds in series). Fit boundaries (ISO): δ < 0 = clearance, 0–5 µm = transition, > 5 µm = interference (light to heavy press).

What to notice

  • p ∝ E_eff·δ/d — interface pressure scales linearly with both Young's modulus and interference. For same-material pairs E_eff = E/2 (shaft and hub compliances add in series), so a 20 µm interference on a steel-on-steel fit gives ~p ≈ 200 MPa; the same on PETG-on-PETG gives ~p ≈ 2 MPa.
  • Press-in force scales with d, L, AND p. Doubling the engagement length doubles the press force. A 10 mm × 15 mm steel-on-steel light press typically wants several kN — arbor press territory.
  • Hoop stress is the limit, not friction. Push interference too high and the hub yields radially before the friction limit is reached. The Lamé equation says σ_hoop ≈ p × geometry factor; check against hub yield, NOT just bolt loads.
  • FDM press fits behave differently. Effective interference must be much larger (0.2–0.4 mm) because surface asperities collapse and the PETG hub creeps. Too tight and the hub splits along layer lines.
  • Bearings: outer race clearance, inner race press. The rotating ring (inner) gets the press fit so it doesn't creep relative to the shaft; the stationary outer ring just sits in its housing.
  • Welding is permanent and changes parent properties. 6061-T6 loses ~50% strength in the heat-affected zone (HAZ); a "welded T6 frame" is structurally a different alloy at the joint. Post-weld heat-treatment is required to recover.
  • Brazing fills the dissimilar-metals gap. Bike frame lugs, carbide tool tips, and copper pipes are joined by brazing because the parent metals don't melt — you can join steel to brass, copper to silver, etc.

DECISION — welding

  • MIG — fast, cheap, mild steel
  • TIG — precise, clean, all metals (Al, SS, Ti)
  • Spot — auto body, battery tabs
  • Braze — dissimilar metals, low temp

PITFALLS

  • Welding 6061-T6 → HAZ drops to ~T4 (−50% strength)
  • FDM press-fit needs 0.2–0.4 mm interference
  • Bearing: inner race press, outer race clearance
  • Hoop stress limits press, not friction
