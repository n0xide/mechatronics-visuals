Property classes — decode the number on the head

Metric bolt property class is two numbers separated by a dot. X.Y says UTS = X·100 MPa and yield = X·Y·10 MPa. So 8.8 = 800 MPa tensile, 640 MPa yield. Stainless uses A2-70 / A4-80 — the last number is UTS / 10. Pick a class and thread size to see the proof load and the 70%-of-proof target preload.

Head marking examples

Numbers stamped on the bolt head identify class. Manufacturer mark (initials, e.g. "FF", "BMW") is also present on the head — same flat. Inch grades use radial slashes instead of digits; metric is always digits.

What to notice

  • Class 8.8 is everywhere because the math is everywhere. A 640 MPa yield in M6 stress area = 12.9 kN clamp at proof. That's a lot of clamp from a hardware-store bolt.
  • 10.9 vs. 12.9 is a 20% gain you usually don't need. Unless you're space-constrained, use 8.8 with a bigger diameter — far cheaper and less brittle.
  • Stainless is weaker than carbon-steel bolts. A4-80 (800 MPa UTS) ≈ class 8.8 in tensile but only ~600 MPa yield and softer threads. Galling risk is real — use anti-seize.
  • Proof load < yield load. ISO 898 sets proof at ~90% of yield to give a safe margin against the lower bound of yield scatter.
  • 70% of proof is the workhorse target preload. Leaves 30% margin for torque scatter and external loads. Critical joints (engine head) go to 90%+ with controlled tightening (torque + angle).
  • "X.Y" decomposes as UTS / yield-fraction. If you forget this, mark the bolt body with σ_uts = X·100 MPa and you can derive yield from the dot.
