What to notice

  • Dry friction K varies ±25%; preload varies almost the same ±33% because F = T/(K·d). Two identically-torqued bolts can have 1.5× different clamp loads.
  • Lubrication shrinks scatter AND raises mean preload. Going from K = 0.20 → 0.15 at the same T raises F by 33%. Read the spec: torque values are usually quoted for one specific K.
  • Star/cross pattern in 3 passes (1/3 → 2/3 → full) keeps flange flat. Tightening one bolt fully before others always warps the joint and de-preloads earlier bolts.
  • Preload changes after the first cycle on a new joint. "Embedment" (gasket compression, thread bedding) loses 5–10% — re-torque after some hours/cycles is common for critical joints.
  • Hand-feel torque is ±50%. A torque wrench is mandatory for anything important. Calibrated wrenches start around $30 and are essential for any class 10.9+ work.
  • T·F·d is a friction-dominated approximation. ~50% of input torque overcomes head friction, ~40% overcomes thread friction, ~10% becomes useful preload. Improvements come from cutting friction, not from harder tightening.
