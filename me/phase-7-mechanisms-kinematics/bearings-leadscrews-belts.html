What to notice

  • L₁₀ is "90% of bearings reach this life." Half will far exceed it; 10% fail earlier. Multiply by service-factor judgement (2–5×) when sizing for a long-life machine.
  • L₁₀ ∝ (C/P)³. Doubling C/P gives 8× life; halving the load gives 8× life. Tiny load reductions matter a lot.
  • Lead screw efficiency ≈ 30–50%; ball screw ≈ 85–95%. The 3× efficiency advantage of a ball screw is the entire reason CNC chose them — same motor, 3× the load capability.
  • Self-locking when λ ≤ arctan(μ). Required for Z-axis on power-off (your part won't crash down). Lost on a ball screw — needs a brake.
  • Belts give resolution for free. A 20T GT2 + 1/16 microstep NEMA17 → 40 mm / 3200 steps = 12.5 µm theoretical resolution. Real accuracy is much worse (belt stretch, backlash at idlers).
  • Don't over-tension belts. Excess tension wrecks the small bearings inside the stepper motor; loose belts skip and lose position. Aim for ~1–3 mm deflection at midspan under ~5 N finger pressure on a typical 200 mm 3D-printer span.

MEMORIZE

  • 608 bearing: 8 mm bore × 22 mm OD × 7 mm
  • Fits: inner H7/p6 (press), outer H7/h6 (slip — one ring must float)
  • GT2 belt: 2 mm pitch × 20 T pulley = 40 mm/rev
  • L₁₀ ∝ (C/P)³ — halve P → 8× life

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Press-fit both rings = no thermal/misalignment slip → bearing dies fast
  • Ball screw back-drives (η too high to self-lock) → vertical axis needs a brake on power-off
  • Lead screws self-lock when λ ≤ arctan(µ) — intended for Z-axes without brakes
  • Over-tensioned belts crush stepper bearings; aim ~1–3 mm midspan deflection
