ω = cam angular velocity, β = rise duration in radians.

Pressure angle: ϕ = arctan( v / ((r_b + s) ω) )

Pressure angle is the angle between the follower-motion direction and the common normal at the contact point. Higher pressure angle → more side force on the follower stem (binding, wear). The classical limit for translating roller followers is ϕ_max ≤ 30°. To reduce ϕ: (a) use a larger base-circle radius, (b) use a smoother profile (cycloidal has lower peak v than constant acceleration), (c) increase β (slower rise).

What to notice

  • Constant velocity has infinite acceleration at the corners. Mathematically clean rise, mechanically violent — every rise/fall transition is a hammer-blow.
  • Constant acceleration is the textbook compromise: jerk is non-zero only at the transition points (step changes), peak acceleration is moderate.
  • Cycloidal is the smoothest of the three — velocity, acceleration, and jerk are all continuous. Peak acceleration is ~1.57× higher than constant-acceleration's, but jerk is finite, so the follower-train sees no shock loading.
  • The pressure-angle limit drives the base circle. Want a fast rise on a small cam? You'll hit ϕ > 30°. Either grow the cam or stretch the rise duration.
  • Roller follower > flat follower > knife-edge. Roller has lowest friction, flat is robust for steep cams, knife-edge is theoretical-only because the tip would wear instantly.
  • Undercutting happens when the cam profile curves tighter than the roller radius — the roller can't physically follow. Check by computing the radius of curvature ρ; require ρ > r_roller everywhere.

Cheat sheet

MOTION PROFILES

  • CV (constant velocity): infinite a at endpoints → impact, do not use
  • CA (constant accel): parabolic, finite a but jerk steps
  • SHM: cosine profile — v continuous but a steps to non-zero at boundaries (infinite jerk at start/end of rise)
  • Cycloidal: smoothest (continuous v, a, jerk). Peak a = 2π·hω²/β² ≈ 1.57× CA — preferred at high speed despite higher peak a

DECISION POINTS

  • Pressure angle ϕ ≤ 30° practical limit (force-flow into follower)
  • Larger base circle → lower ϕ, smoother contact, bigger envelope
  • Roller follower > flat > knife-edge for life and friction

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Undercutting: profile curves back into itself → ρ_cam < r_roller; roller skips
  • Use cycloidal at high RPM — CV causes impacts at rise/return ends
  • Pressure angle peaks mid-rise on harmonic motion; check there, not at start
