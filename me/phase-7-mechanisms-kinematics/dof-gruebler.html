Mechanism DOF — Gruebler's equation

Pick a preset mechanism (or build your own by adding links and joints) and the calculator applies Gruebler's planar equation F = 3(n−1) − 2·J₁ − J₂ to verify whether the mechanism is a structure, a one-DOF machine, underconstrained, or overconstrained.

What to notice

  • Gruebler counts ground. The fixed frame is link #1. A four-bar has 4 links: ground + crank + coupler + rocker.
  • F = 1 is the sweet spot — one input drives one output deterministically. Most machines target this.
  • F = 0 is a structure. A triangle truss has 3 links + 3 pins: F = 3(3−1) − 2·3 = 0. No motion is the point.
  • F > 1 is underconstrained. A 5-bar with all pin joints has F = 2 — you need two motors to fully specify the pose.
  • F < 0 is overconstrained on paper but works in reality when redundant constraints are intentional (rigid frames) or when geometry creates an exception (e.g., parallelogram linkage, which Gruebler counts as F=0 but moves with F=1).
  • Half joints (J₂) are gear teeth or a cam riding on a flat — they let two motions (roll + slip) happen at the contact, so they remove only 1 DOF in plane.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Forgot ground — ground is link #1; four-bar has n=4, not 3.
  • Gear pair = 1 joint (J₂), not 2.
  • Slider-crank: 3R + 1P = 4 J₁ joints → F=1.
  • Parallelogram Gruebler says F=0 but it moves — special geometry exception.
