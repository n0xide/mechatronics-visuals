Rack & pinion

Parallel shafts. Teeth cut straight across the face. Cheap, simple, noisy at high speed because each tooth pair engages all at once (impulsive load). Standard hobby choice for low-speed reductions (~<3000 rpm). Pressure angle commonly 20°; 14.5° is legacy.

Teeth cut at a helix angle (15°–30°). Each tooth enters mesh gradually → quieter, higher contact ratio, smoother torque. Cost: axial thrust force must be absorbed by a thrust bearing (or use a herringbone / double-helical to cancel it). Stronger than a spur of the same width.

Conical pitch surfaces; shafts intersect (usually 90°). Straight bevels are spur-style on a cone — OK below ~5 m/s pitch-line velocity. Spiral bevels are the helical equivalent — quieter, higher capacity, used in differentials. Equal-ratio bevels are called miter gears.

Worm (screw) meshes with worm wheel — 90° non-intersecting shafts. Very high single-stage ratio (5:1 to 100:1+). Often self-locking when worm lead angle < friction angle: the wheel can't back-drive the worm — useful for hoists, lifts. Cost: low efficiency (30–80%), heat generation, sliding contact wear (bronze worm wheel + steel worm).

Rack = a gear with infinite radius — a straight strip of teeth. Pinion turns → rack translates. Linear travel per pinion revolution = z·m·π (= π·d). Used in CNC X/Y axes (long travel), automotive steering, garage doors. Backlash is the dominant accuracy spec.

What to notice

  • Module must match. Two gears with different modules will not mesh — teeth won't line up. Module is the "currency" of metric gear design (m = d/z).
  • Center distance C = m(z₁+z₂)/2. The exact spacing of the two shafts for backlash-free engagement. Set this wrong by ±0.5 mm and you've added backlash or jammed the mesh.
  • Ratio i = z₂/z₁. Output torque is multiplied by i (minus losses); output speed is divided by i. To step up speed instead of torque, reverse drive direction (drive z₂, driven z₁).
  • Involute tooth profile makes the contact path a straight line passing through the pitch point at the pressure angle — this is why slight changes in center distance still give constant velocity ratio. (Cycloidal teeth don't have this property.)
  • Minimum teeth. A 20°-PA pinion has minimum teeth around 17 before undercutting starts (with full-depth teeth and standard addendum). Below that you need profile-shifted or modified-tooth gears.
  • For more reduction than a single mesh can give (~6:1 practical), use a compound train (gear pair → second pair on the same shaft) or a planetary set. A single 80T:8T pair is theoretically 10:1 but the 8T pinion will have undercut teeth and fragile root sections.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

Module
m = d / z
Center dist.
C = m(z₁ + z₂) / 2
Ratio
i = z₂/z₁ = ω₁/ω₂
Torque out
T_out = i · T_in · η

MEMORIZE

  • Modules must match to mesh — m₁ = m₂ (else teeth incompatible)
  • 20° pressure angle is the world standard for spur/helical
  • Contact ratio ≥ 1.6 = quiet operation; <1.0 = skipped teeth
  • Min teeth ≈ 17 at 20° PA before undercut

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Worm self-locks when lead angle λ < arctan(µ) — irreversible drive (intentional for hoists)
  • Backlash required for thermal expansion; precision needs anti-backlash split gears or duplex worms
  • Single-stage limit ≈ 6:1; chain stages for higher ratios
