Timing-Belt Tension & Geometry

GT2 belts have a 2 mm tooth pitch — every revolution of a 20-tooth pulley moves the belt exactly 40 mm. That linear relationship is what makes 3D-printer and CoreXY motion systems possible. But the right pulley size is half the question; the other half is correct belt tension. Too loose: backlash, skipped teeth. Too tight: bearing wear and motor heating. The mid-span deflection method (0.4-0.5 mm per 1 kg load) is the field-quick way to get it right without specialty tooling.

GT2 belt geometry

"GT2" is a profile, not a brand: 2 mm tooth-to-tooth pitch, curvilinear tooth shape with a 0.75 mm tooth height. The curvilinear profile distributes load smoothly as teeth engage and disengage — much quieter and lower-friction than the old trapezoidal-tooth MXL or XL belts.

Reinforcement: GT2 belts have either steel or fiberglass tension cords running lengthwise inside the rubber. Fiberglass is the default — flexible, light, low stretch. Steel-cord (often labeled "GT2-6 SteelCord") has zero stretch and is used where positioning accuracy matters at long spans, but it's also stiffer and harder on idlers. Kevlar-corded belts exist for high-shock applications.

Linear motion per revolution

Pulley circumference = teeth × pitch (tooth-to-tooth distance, not arc length) For a 20-tooth GT2 pulley: C = 20 × 2 = 40 mm per revolution For a 16-tooth GT2 pulley: C = 16 × 2 = 32 mm per revolution → linear mm per stepper step = (teeth × pitch) / steps-per-rev Example: NEMA-17 stepper at 200 full-steps/rev, 1/16 microstep = 3200 microsteps/rev with 20-tooth pulley = 40 mm / 3200 = 12.5 µm per microstep

This is why 3D printer firmware "steps per mm" calibration depends on three things: pulley size, microstep setting, and motor steps-per-revolution. Change any one of those, recalculate the others, or your XY scale will be wrong.

Tension by mid-span deflection

For light-industrial belt drives, an exact tensiometer is overkill. The mid-span-deflection method works on any belt: push perpendicular to the belt at the middle of the free span with a known force, measure how far it deflects. The target is fixed by belt geometry — and it scales linearly with the span.

1. Measure the free span L (pulley center to pulley center, minus the pulley wrap). 2. Apply a known force F at mid-span perpendicular to the belt. For GT2 belts, F = 1 kg (9.81 N) is standard. 3. Measure the deflection δ at mid-span. 4. Target deflection for GT2: δ = (0.4 - 0.5) mm × (L / 100 mm) per 1 kg of test load. 5. Tension comes from geometry: T = F · L / (4·δ) for small deflection (geometry of a slack cable under point load).

For a 200 mm span: δ ≈ 0.8–1.0 mm under 1 kg = T ≈ 200·1·9.81 / (4·0.9) ≈ 545 N tension. For a 100 mm span: δ ≈ 0.4–0.5 mm under 1 kg = T ≈ 100·9.81 / (4·0.45) ≈ 545 N — same tension target, scaled by span.

Tooth-in-mesh rule: at least 6 teeth should be in contact with the driven pulley for full rated load. For a 20T pulley with a straight belt run, that's 6/20 = 30 % wrap, or about 108° of contact. For tight wrap angles, use an idler to increase the wrap.

Worked example — CoreXY printer Y-axis belt

The setup

Voron 2.4-style CoreXY: GT2-6 (2 mm pitch, 6 mm wide) belt, 20-tooth aluminum pulleys, NEMA-17 steppers at 200 full-steps/rev with 1/16 microstepping. Y-axis free span (between idler and motor pulley) is 350 mm. Carriage weight ~0.8 kg, peak acceleration target 8000 mm/s².

Compute step resolution

Linear mm/rev = 20 × 2 = 40 mm Microsteps/rev = 200 × 16 = 3200 Resolution = 40 / 3200 = 12.5 µm/microstep (well below typical positional tolerance)

Compute required tension

Inertial force F = m·a = 0.8 × 8 = 6.4 N peak at carriage Minimum tension (to prevent slack-side going limp) ≈ 4× peak force = ~25 N Recommended GT2 tension ≈ 30–50 N per cord (mid-range industrial) Target: T = 40 N → δ at mid-span under 1 kg ≈ 350 × 9.81 / (4 × 40) = 21.4 mm too high — that's the slack tension! Re-derive: for δ = 0.4 mm × (350/100) = 1.4 mm per 1 kg → T = 350 × 9.81 / (4 × 1.4) = 613 N Wait — that's nominally too high. Field rule for GT2-6: actual operating tension 50–80 N per side.

Resolve the rule

The mid-span deflection rule gives a useful relative number but the absolute tension depends on belt cross-section and assumed safety factor. For GT2-6 in CoreXY service, the field-rule is: tight enough that under-load deflection at mid-span with finger pressure is < 2 mm, and the carriage doesn't audibly skip on rapid moves. The resonance check is louder than the spec — once both belts are properly tensioned, a thumb-flick should produce a similar audible pitch (within a few percent) on both belts. The "guitar tuning" method, in fact.

Sanity check

Audible frequency of properly-tensioned GT2-6 at 350 mm span: ~110–130 Hz (a B♭/B note) Both belts identical resonance → carriage is square and equal-tensioned Re-check every 100 print-hours; fiberglass cord creeps ~3 % in first 100 hours

What to notice

  • Pitch × teeth = mm per rev — that's the linear motion fact. Memorize this; everything in motion-system math reduces to it.
  • Tighten by ear, not by ruler. The "guitar string" method (pluck the belt, listen for resonance) is more reliable than numerical deflection for GT2-class belts because the spec is just an order-of-magnitude rule.
  • Both belts on CoreXY must match. If one belt is tighter than the other, the carriage skews under acceleration and prints have ringing on diagonal moves. Equal-pitch belts = equal-tension belts.
  • Idler diameter ≥ 4× tooth pitch. For GT2 (2 mm pitch), idlers smaller than 8 mm wear the belt fast. Most kit idlers are 12 mm OD — safe.
  • Steel cord is for accuracy, not strength. Fiberglass and steel have similar tensile capacity per width. Steel just doesn't creep. Use steel cord for > 500 mm spans where accuracy matters.
  • Tension creeps down over time. Fiberglass belts lose ~5–10 % tension in the first 100 hours. Re-tension on a schedule; don't wait for skipped steps.
  • Too-tight kills bearings. 2× the recommended tension halves the bearing life. Don't crank the tensioner — the belt is happier at 50 N than 200 N for any reasonable load.

Where you'll see this

Real uses

  • 3D printers — both axes (X+Y on cartesian, A+B on CoreXY) are GT2. Z is usually a lead-screw, not a belt.
  • Laser cutters — XY gantry is GT2 or GT3. Larger machines use GT5 for the longer spans.
  • Plotters / pen-bots — GT2 for pen-axis motion.
  • CNC routers (small) — GT2 or GT3 for desktop machines; rack-and-pinion or lead screw for industrial.
  • Robot arms with shoulder/elbow belts — typically GT5 or HTD-8M because tooth load is high (single-stage reduction with high torque).
  • Conveyor belts (positive drive) — small medical/lab conveyors use GT3 for accurate position tracking.
  • Camera dolly / slider — GT2 with stepper for time-lapse and motion-control video.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS

  • linear = teeth × pitch (mm/rev)
  • µm/step = (teeth × pitch) / (steps_full × microsteps)
  • T ≈ F·L / (4·δ) mid-span deflection → tension
  • δ_target ≈ 0.4–0.5 mm × L/100mm per 1 kg test load (GT2)
  • idler_min_OD = 4 × pitch
  • teeth_in_mesh ≥ 6 for full rated load

NUMBERS

  • GT2 standard: 20-tooth pulley = 40 mm/rev
  • GT3 standard: 20-tooth pulley = 60 mm/rev
  • GT2-6 operating tension 50–80 N per side
  • Fiberglass cord stretch first 100 h: ~5 %
  • Audible belt pitch (GT2-6, 200 mm span): ~150 Hz
  • NEMA-17 + 20T GT2 + 1/16 microstep: 12.5 µm/step

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Mismatched belt tensions on CoreXY → diagonal-move ringing artifacts.
  • Idler too small (< 4× pitch) → belt cracks on inside.
  • Over-tensioning → bearing wear, motor heating, no accuracy benefit.
  • Under-tensioning → backlash, skipped teeth, lost steps.
  • Forgetting microstep ÷ pulley change after upgrade → wrong steps_per_mm in firmware.
  • Mixing belt brands / pitches on a multi-belt system → slight pitch differences walk one belt over time.
  • Misaligned pulleys → belt walks edges, edge fraying, premature failure.
