Forced vibration — magnification, resonance, isolation

A mass-spring-damper is driven by F(t) = F₀·cos(Ωt). The magnification factor MF shows how much bigger the response is than the static deflection F₀/k; transmissibility T shows how much of the driving force reaches the supporting structure. Sliders let you sweep frequency ratio r = Ω/ωn and damping ζ. Resonance lives at r ≈ 1; isolation lives beyond r = √2.

Vibration isolation design

What to notice

  • Resonance peak height ≈ 1/(2ζ). At r = 1 with ζ = 0.05 (lightly damped), MF ≈ 10. With ζ = 0.01, MF ≈ 50. Tiny damping → catastrophic amplification.
  • r = √2 ≈ 1.414 is the isolation threshold. Below it, the mount makes vibration worse (T > 1). Above it, the mount actually isolates.
  • Isolation = soft mount. To isolate from a high Ω, you need low ωn → low k or high m. The contradiction with stiffness is real: vibration mounts are deliberately wobbly.
  • More damping helps near resonance but hurts in the isolation region. ζ rises → resonance peak falls, but transmissibility for r >> 1 actually increases too. Pick ζ for the regime you're operating in.
  • Phase between F(t) and x(t): 0° below resonance, −90° at resonance, −180° well above. Phase tells you what the mass is "feeling" instant to instant.
  • Rule of thumb (lightly damped): ωn ≤ Ω/3 gives roughly T ≤ 0.1 for most engineered isolators. Useful first-pass design number.

Cheat sheet

FORMULAS (r = Ω/ω_n)

Magnification
MF = 1 / √((1−r²)² + (2ζr)²)
Transmiss.
T = √((1+(2ζr)²) / ((1−r²)² + (2ζr)²))
Peak MF
≈ 1 / (2ζ) at r ≈ 1

REGIME MAP

  • r < 1: stiffness-controlled, MF → 1
  • r = 1: resonance — MF peaks ≈ 1/(2ζ)
  • r = √2: T = 1, isolation crossover
  • r > √2: isolation works (T < 1); aim for r ≥ 3

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Isolating below √2 makes it WORSE — soft mount amplifies forcing
  • High damping flattens the resonance peak but worsens isolation at r >> 1
  • ω_n shifts if mass changes — re-check r after loading the platform
  • Isolator mount = deliberately wobbly — don't fight stiffness vs. isolation trade-off
