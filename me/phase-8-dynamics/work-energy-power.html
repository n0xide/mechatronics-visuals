Work, energy, power — three lenses on the same idea

Three panels: (a) work done by a force at an angle, (b) a rolling ball converting potential energy to kinetic energy on an incline, and (c) mechanical power as the product of torque and angular speed. Energy is the bookkeeping; power is the rate of payment.

Other PE forms

Same logic with elastic PE: PE_spring = ½kx². Compress a 5 kN/m spring by 50 mm and you've stored 6.25 J — exactly enough to launch a 0.5 kg projectile at 5 m/s if you converted it all to KE_linear.

C. Power: P = F·v = T·ω

Mechanical power is the rate of doing work. For a rotating shaft: P = T·ω, where ω is in rad/s. For RPM, ω = 2π · RPM / 60.

What to notice

  • Force ⟂ motion does zero work. Push down on a sliding box — vertical force, horizontal motion, cos(90°) = 0 → W = 0. Holding a heavy box still: zero work in the mechanical sense, no matter how tired your arms get.
  • Rolling without slipping splits energy. A solid ball rolling down the ramp converts gravitational PE into 5/7 linear KE + 2/7 rotational KE (because I = 2mr²/5, and ω = v/r). A sliding block would convert 100% to linear KE — and arrive faster.
  • Energy is conserved; power tells you how fast. Lifting a 100 kg crate 1 m takes 981 J regardless of who does it. A human takes ~10 s (≈100 W). A motor doing it in 1 s needs ~1 kW.
  • P = T·ω explains why gearing matters. A motor's output power is fixed at a given operating point. Geared 10:1 down, you get 10× torque, 1/10× speed, same power.
  • Efficiency multiplies through a chain. Motor (η = 0.9) × gearbox (0.95) × belt (0.98) → 0.838. Don't lose money in series.
  • Watts on a spec sheet need verification. A 12 V × 1 A = 12 W motor at η=60% delivers 7.2 W mechanical. The rest is heat.

MEMORIZE

  • 1 hp ≈ 746 W; 1 kW ≈ 1.34 hp
  • Conservation: KE + PE = const (no friction)
  • Chain efficiency multiplies: η_total = η₁ · η₂ · η₃ …
  • Gearing trades T ↔ ω at constant P

COMMON PITFALLS

  • P = T·ω needs ω in rad/s NOT RPM — convert ω = 2π·RPM/60
  • Spec-sheet "watts" is usually electrical input — multiply by η for mech output
  • Lost η in series chain: 0.9 × 0.95 × 0.98 ≈ 0.84 — small leaks add up
