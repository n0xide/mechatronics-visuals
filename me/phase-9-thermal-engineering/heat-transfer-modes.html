Heat transfer — three modes, side by side

Conduction (Fourier), convection (Newton), and radiation (Stefan–Boltzmann) all move heat from hot to cold, but they obey different laws and dominate at different scales. All three formulas below assume steady state (q constant in time) and 1D heat flow (no lateral spreading). Sweep the inputs and watch the heat flow change. Compare numbers on identical surface areas to feel why a black-anodized fin matters and a bare-aluminum one barely radiates.

What to notice

  • Conductivity matters by factor of ~10⁴. A 2 mm slab of copper passes ~1300× more heat than the same slab of FR4 — and ~16000× more than still air. This is why heat needs a path, not just space.
  • Forced convection wins on h. Going from natural (h≈10) to a tiny 40 mm fan (h≈50) gives a 5× improvement without changing the heatsink. That's why every laptop has a fan.
  • Radiation depends on emissivity. Polished aluminum (ε=0.05) radiates almost nothing. Black-anodize it (ε=0.9) and it radiates 18× more — same metal, same fin geometry. This is why heatsinks are anodized black.
  • Radiation scales as T⁴. Push T₁ from 80 °C to 150 °C and radiation roughly triples while convection only doubles — radiation becomes more important at high temperature (LEDs, power resistors, MOSFETs running hot).
  • Convection beats radiation at low ΔT. For typical electronics (ΔT < 50 °C), well-anodized natural convection radiates roughly 1× the convective amount — useful, but secondary. Forced convection swamps both.
  • Thickness is linear; area is linear; ΔT is linear (for conduction & convection). Doubling area doubles heat removed. Halving L doubles conducted heat. There's no magic — these are linear laws until you change which law applies (laminar → turbulent, etc.).

COMMON PITFALLS

  • Radiation uses K, not °C — T⁴ is meaningless in Celsius.
  • Sign convention — full Fourier law is q = −k·A·dT/dx; the minus sign means heat flows down the gradient. The magnitudes above use ΔT = T_hot − T_cold.
  • Units — k is W/m·K (per unit length); h is W/m²·K (per unit area). Don't swap.
  • Conduction assumes steady state — transient needs ρ·c_p.
  • h depends on geometry + orientation — vertical ≠ horizontal.
