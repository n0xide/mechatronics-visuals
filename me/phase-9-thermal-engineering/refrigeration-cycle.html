Vapor-Compression Refrigeration Cycle — pumping heat from cold to hot

Every fridge, AC, heat pump, and EV battery cooler runs a variant of the vapor-compression cycle: a refrigerant evaporates at low pressure (absorbing heat from the cold space), is compressed (now hot vapor), condenses at high pressure (rejecting heat to the warm space), and expands back to low pressure (cooled liquid). This visual builds the cycle on a pressure-enthalpy diagram, computes COP from operating conditions, and shows how refrigerant choice and superheat/subcool change the performance.

The math behind COP

Mass flow m_dot = mass of refrigerant per second circulating through the loop. Specific enthalpies h₁, h₂, h₃, h₄ at the four state points (kJ/kg, from P-h chart or refrigerant table). Heat absorbed by evaporator (cooling effect): Q_evap = m_dot · (h₁ − h₄) [kW] Work added by compressor (input): W_comp = m_dot · (h₂_actual − h₁) = m_dot · (h₂_isentropic − h₁) / η_isen Heat rejected at condenser: Q_cond = m_dot · (h₂ − h₃) = Q_evap + W_comp (energy balance) COP_cooling = Q_evap / W_comp typical 2-5 for AC, 1.5-3 for low-T fridges COP_heating = Q_cond / W_comp = COP_cooling + 1 typical 3-5 for heat pumps Theoretical maximum (Carnot): COP_Carnot = T_cold / (T_hot − T_cold) (temperatures in Kelvin) Real systems: 40-60 % of Carnot — losses from compressor inefficiency, throttle (3→4 destroys ~10 % of theoretical), heat-exchanger ΔT.

Worked example — bedroom mini-split AC sized for 3 kW cooling

Spec

Bedroom mini-split: cooling capacity 3 kW (10,000 BTU/h), indoor air 24 °C, outdoor 35 °C. Refrigerant R-32 (modern). Evaporator T ≈ 5 °C (cold enough to pull moisture), condenser T ≈ 50 °C. 8 K superheat, 5 K subcool, η_isen = 0.78 (rotary compressor).

Cycle calculation (using R-32 P-h chart)

State 1 (evap outlet): T = 5 + 8 = 13 °C, P_evap ≈ 9 bar, h₁ ≈ 521 kJ/kg State 2_isentropic (compressor outlet): P_cond ≈ 31 bar, h₂s ≈ 567 kJ/kg State 2_actual: h₂ = h₁ + (h₂s−h₁)/η = 521 + 46/0.78 = 521 + 59 = 580 kJ/kg State 3 (cond outlet): T = 50 − 5 = 45 °C, h₃ ≈ 269 kJ/kg (subcooled liquid) State 4 (after throttle): h₄ = h₃ = 269 kJ/kg, mix at evap pressure Mass flow: m_dot = Q_evap / (h₁−h₄) = 3 / (521 − 269) = 3 / 252 = 0.0119 kg/s = 12 g/s W_comp = m_dot · (h₂−h₁) = 0.0119 · 59 = 0.70 kW Q_cond = m_dot · (h₂−h₃) = 0.0119 · 311 = 3.70 kW COP_cooling = Q_evap / W_comp = 3 / 0.70 = 4.29 (matches SEER ~15 BTU/Wh) COP_Carnot = (5+273) / (50−5) = 278 / 45 = 6.18 (theoretical max) η vs Carnot = 4.29 / 6.18 = 69 % — solid for a residential unit.

For a heating-mode heat pump using the same hardware: COP_heating = COP_cooling + 1 = 5.29. That's why heat pumps win over resistive electric heat (which has COP = 1) by 4-5×.

Transcritical CO₂ — the special case

R-744 (CO₂) has a critical point at 31 °C / 7.4 MPa. When the heat-rejection side exceeds 31 °C — which is most of the time in summer — the refrigerant doesn't condense, it just cools as a supercritical fluid (no phase change). This is transcritical operation:

  • The high-side "condenser" becomes a gas-cooler — heat exchange with no phase change → ΔT across coil is variable, not fixed
  • System operates at much higher pressure: 8-12 MPa vs ~2 MPa for R-410A
  • Larger components, thicker tubing, special compressors needed
  • But: zero ODP, GWP = 1, abundant cheap natural refrigerant
  • Used in: supermarket refrigeration (Europe), commercial heat pumps, future EV cooling

Common pitfalls

  • Liquid slugging — if superheat = 0 or negative (liquid drops in suction line), the compressor tries to compress liquid (incompressible) and self-destructs. Always size the TXV bulb for ≥ 3 K superheat margin.
  • Wrong refrigerant retrofit — R-22 to R-410A requires NEW compressor (higher pressure), often new coils. Cannot just swap refrigerant. Lubricant compatibility matters too (POE oil vs mineral).
  • Air in the system — non-condensable gases raise high-side pressure dramatically; system runs hot, COP drops, compressor cycles on thermal. Always pull deep vacuum (< 500 µm Hg) for ≥ 30 min before charging.
  • Moisture — refrigerant + water → acid (especially with R-410A POE oil hygroscopic). Eats motor windings; opens hermetic compressor. Hence the filter-drier in every loop.
  • Subcool too low — flash gas at TXV inlet, erratic flow control, hunting; raise charge or add receiver.
  • Overcharging — flooded condenser, high head pressure, high power draw, possibly liquid slugging on start. Always weigh charge per manufacturer spec.
  • Glide — zeotropic blends (R-407C) shift composition through phase change. The "saturation temperature" changes during evap/cond. Causes apparent superheat where there's actually subcool, etc. Use the dew-point and bubble-point temperatures from the manufacturer's chart.
  • Ignoring pipe pressure drops — long line-sets between indoor and outdoor units cause 1-3 K saturation temperature loss per 10 m. Reduces effective ΔT, hurts COP.
