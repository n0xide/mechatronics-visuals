TIM and PCB thermal management

Once heat leaves the silicon, it crosses a few millimeters of thermal interface material (TIM) to the case or sink, then spreads through the PCB via copper pours and via arrays. Each step is just Θ = L/(k·A). The numbers are small — fractions of a degree per watt — but they decide whether an IC sits at 60 °C or 110 °C.

Under a QFN's exposed pad, designers drill a grid of plated through-holes. Each via is a small copper tube — a parallel low-resistance path from top-layer pad to bottom-layer copper pour. Each via behaves like a thermal resistor of value Θ_via; N of them in parallel sum as Θ_array = Θ_via/N.

What to notice

  • TIM BLT matters more than k. A silicone pad at k=5, BLT=0.5 mm has 100× the resistance of paste at k=8, BLT=0.05 mm — same chemistry family, different thickness. Thin layer wins.
  • Liquid metal isn't usually worth it. Yes, k=40, but it conducts electricity, eats aluminum (forms In-Al alloy), spreads if you tilt the device, and adds 2 mm² of risk for a fraction of a °C improvement over good paste. Reserved for overclockers and rare ME-engineered systems.
  • Each thermal via is ~2–5 °C/W. Numbers depend on plating and length. 9 vias in parallel give ~0.3 °C/W — a 10× improvement over 1 via.
  • Diminishing returns past N=16. Going from 9 to 25 vias only drops Θ from ~0.3 to ~0.1 — and you lose pad area, complicate soldering (paste wicks down the holes), and risk solder voids. Most designs land at 3×3 or 4×4 vias.
  • Copper pours conduct laterally. The pad doesn't have to push heat down — it can spread it across the board to a connector or chassis. A 1 oz copper pour over 30 × 30 mm acts as a wide low-Θ plane.
  • "Thermal relief" spokes hurt thermal performance. Standard PCB connections to a thermal pad use 4-spoke reliefs to prevent solder wicking. For high-power parts, request solid copper connections instead — accept the soldering challenge, gain ~2× thermal conductivity at that joint.

MEMORIZE / DECIDE

  • k: grease 3–10, pad 2–6, phase-change 2–6, graphene 10–20.
  • Liquid metal 20–40 — but conductive, eats Al.
  • Grease for one-off; pad for assembly speed.
  • Thinner BLT → lower R — pressure beats thickness.

COMMON PITFALLS

  • More paste ≠ better — excess squeezes out, adds R.
  • Liquid metal + Al = corrosion — In-Al alloy eats the sink.
  • Spoke reliefs vs solid pour — spokes halve thermal path.
